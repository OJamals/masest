## Decision

Lead with **marine HVAC and heat-exchange cleaning**. Expand into marine surface cleaning after technical trust is established.

Why this wedge:

- Maintenance triggers are concrete: restricted seawater flow, scale, dirty coils, condensate blockage, spring commissioning, drydock, and planned shutdowns.
- Buyers can measure flow, pressure drop, approach temperature, visible deposit, rinse condition, labor, downtime, and repeat work.
- Commercial users need documented methods, larger packs, and quote support—not another lifestyle boat-cleaner label.
- Surface cleaning adds recurring volume, but consumer wash/degreaser shelves are more crowded and price-sensitive.

This is a positioning recommendation, not a market-size claim. The research found a large vessel installed base but no defensible VertKleen serviceable-market estimate from public primary sources alone.

## Positioning

### Core position

**Cleaning chemistry for marine surfaces and HVAC systems.**

VertKleen should be sold by the buyer's actual job: surface, deposit, equipment, method, and pack size. Environmental or safety language must remain secondary and exact-product substantiated.

### Message pillars

1. **Start with the deposit.** Salt film, oil, organic buildup, rust, and mineral scale need different cleaning methods.
2. **Protect the equipment.** Confirm metallurgy, coating, seals, fins, controls, and OEM limits before cleaning.
3. **Control the work.** Isolate, apply or circulate, capture, rinse, inspect, and document.
4. **Prove one job.** Compare labor, water, product, downtime, finished condition, and repeat work before changing the program.

### Competitive whitespace

Competitors commonly win with either job-specific marine shelves, HVAC chemistry selectors, or clean-in-place technical selling. VertKleen can combine those strengths in one premium, evidence-led system:

- Consumer-simple navigation by job
- Trade-grade substrate and process detail
- Distributor-ready pack/case documents
- Quote and controlled-trial path for larger systems
- Premium visual presentation without presenting generated scenes as proof

### Marine page

Eyebrow: `Marine surfaces & systems`

Headline: **Clean hull film, bilge residue, aluminum, and cooling equipment with a controlled method.**

Body:

> Boatyards and vessel-service teams face different soils on different materials. Build the job around gelcoat, paint, aluminum, stainless steel, seals, fins, and the deposit that must come off. Keep wash water contained. Keep electrical systems and unknown materials out of the test area.

Task cards:

- **Hull and topside wash** — Remove salt and working film with a fresh-water pre-rinse, compatible cleaner, soft agitation, full rinse, and contained recovery.
- **Bilge and machinery surfaces** — Lift oily residue with controlled wet cleaning, absorbent recovery, and no open spray near energized equipment.
- **Aluminum care** — Confirm alloy, anodizing, existing corrosion, and adjacent materials before testing a small hidden area.
- **Marine HVAC and seawater circuits** — Isolate the system, confirm metallurgy and seals, circulate or soak under an approved method, capture the solution, rinse, and recommission.

CTA: `Request a vessel cleaning plan`

### HVAC page

Eyebrow: `Airside & waterside maintenance`

Headline: **Clean coils, drains, heat exchangers, and water circuits without guessing at the method.**

Body:

> Start with coil type, deposit, metallurgy, coating, water access, and rinse plan. Use low pressure around fragile fins. Isolate electrical and water-side equipment. Record concentration, dwell, circulation, capture, rinse, and restart condition.

Task cards:

- **Condenser and evaporator coils** — Match cleaner and pressure to fin material, coating, soil, coil depth, and rinse requirements.
- **Condensate pans and drains** — Trace the full route from pan through trap to outlet; remove organic film without borrowing disinfectant or mold-control claims.
- **Heat exchangers and condensers** — Build a closed loop with known system volume, visible return, controlled dwell, containment, and a documented rinse endpoint.
- **Cooling and water systems** — Coordinate cleaning with the facility's water-management, treatment, LOTO, and recommissioning procedures.

CTA: `Request an HVAC cleaning trial`

## Claims that stay out

Do not publish without exact-product evidence and clearance:

- `safe`, `non-toxic`, `ocean-safe`, or broad `green` claims
- `biodegradable` without test method, conditions, and exact formulation
- `OEM approved`, `Coast Guard approved`, `NAVSEA approved`, or similar agency language
- `kills`, `sanitizes`, `disinfects`, `controls mold`, or other pesticidal claims
- universal material compatibility
- quantified energy, labor, water, or downtime savings
- customer identities, endorsements, or performance results without authorization
- `VIDA compliant` while Coast Guard implementation rules remain pending
