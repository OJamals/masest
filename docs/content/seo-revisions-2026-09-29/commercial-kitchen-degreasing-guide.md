## Match the degreaser to the surface

Commercial kitchen degreasing starts with two decisions: what soil needs removing, and what surface lies underneath it. Baked-on fat, a greasy floor edge, and soap film on a wash-area wall may need different products and methods. Food-contact equipment also needs the kitchen's approved sanitation process after cleaning.

Before work, identify stainless steel, aluminum, painted surfaces, seals, and electrical components. Check the equipment instructions and the exact product label. Test an inconspicuous area where appropriate; a successful spot test does not replace the manufacturer's compatibility requirements.

## Use a clear closing-shift sequence

1. **Prepare the area.** Remove or protect food, utensils, and packaging. Let equipment reach the condition required by its cleaning instructions and follow the site's isolation procedure. Mark wet work areas and keep them clear of normal traffic.
2. **Remove loose material.** Collect scraps and accessible grease using tools approved for the surface. This makes the remaining soil easier to assess and keeps unnecessary material out of the wash water.
3. **Apply the selected cleaner.** Use the product's instructions for the exact application. There is no single dilution or dwell time that fits every VertKleen product and kitchen surface. Keep liquid away from electrical components as directed by the equipment manufacturer.
4. **Work and remove the soil.** Use an approved pad, brush, cloth, or cleaning machine. Follow the labeled working time and removal procedure. Do not mix products or increase strength simply because the first pass was incomplete.
5. **Finish the required rinse and sanitation steps.** Check corners, seams, and floor edges for remaining soil or cleaner. Complete the approved food-contact sanitation process where applicable, and reopen the area only after the required checks.

Hoods, filters, and exhaust systems have their own maintenance requirements. A general surface-degreasing guide does not replace the equipment instructions or the kitchen's exhaust-cleaning program.

## Keep degreasing separate from food-contact sanitation

Removing grease does not by itself sanitize a food-contact surface. The [FDA Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022) describes cleaning, applicable rinsing, and sanitizing requirements for food-contact equipment and utensils. Follow the requirements adopted by the local authority, the kitchen's approved procedure, and the product labels.

Do not infer food-contact approval, a no-rinse use, or a sanitizing claim from a cleaner's water-based formulation, HMIS rating, or shipping classification. Confirm the specific product, surface, and process before using it in a food-contact application.
