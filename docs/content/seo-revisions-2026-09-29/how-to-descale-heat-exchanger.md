## Start with the deposit and the equipment

To descale a heat exchanger, identify the fouling, confirm a compatible cleaning process with the equipment manufacturer, remove the deposit, then rinse and verify performance. A cleaner that works on an exposed stainless-steel plate is not automatically suitable for every internal passage, braze, gasket, or pump in a circulation loop.

Record the exchanger model, wetted materials, operating conditions, and deposit appearance before choosing a chemical. Include temporary hoses, fittings, seals, and the cleaning pump in the compatibility check. If the equipment contains aluminum, get written application guidance before selecting HCR or CR; the available product documents do not establish a single reliable aluminum-use envelope.

Check strainers, flow, and operating conditions before attributing poor performance to scale. [Alfa Laval's troubleshooting guidance](https://www.alfalaval.com/service-and-support/product-services/plate-heat-exchanger-services/troubleshooting-for-plate-heat-exchangers/) identifies increased pressure drop and reduced thermal performance as useful cleaning signals. Compare readings under similar flow and load conditions.

## Build the cleaning plan before the shutdown

Confirm these items with the equipment and product instructions:

- **Isolation and access:** the shutdown, cooling, depressurization, and isolation procedure for the system.
- **Chemical compatibility:** the exchanger and every wetted component in the temporary cleaning circuit.
- **Operating limits:** the permitted concentration, temperature, flow, pressure, and exposure time for this application.
- **Completion criteria:** the specified rinse endpoint, inspection method, and return-to-service checks.
- **Waste handling:** a collection and disposal route appropriate to the cleaner and the material removed.

Do not borrow a dilution or contact time from an unrelated label application or case study. Request the current [product label, TDS, and SDS](/resources) and resolve any conflict with the equipment instructions before work begins. Follow the label and SDS for handling, ventilation, and protective equipment; an HMIS rating does not replace those instructions.

## Clean, rinse, and verify

Qualified maintenance staff should follow the approved equipment-specific process. Where circulation cleaning is permitted, monitor the loop and operating limits throughout the job. Keep different cleaning stages separate and use the specified intermediate rinse; do not improvise chemical mixtures.

After cleaning, complete the required rinse and inspect accessible surfaces. Check for leaks and restore the unit according to the manufacturer's procedure. Compare pressure drop and thermal performance against the pre-cleaning baseline under similar conditions. Record what changed, including any remaining restriction or visible deposit.

Appearance alone does not prove restored heat transfer. Equally, a change in readings at a different flow rate or load does not establish cleaning performance.

## What the existing field evidence shows

The [Brevard HCR rust-cleaning case](/blog/hcr-brevard-hvac-rust-case-study) concerns an exposed stainless-steel plate and drain. It is useful evidence for that job's rust removal. It does not establish an internal exchanger circulation procedure, a universal cleaning time, or a measured heat-transfer improvement.

For recurring work, retain the deposit description, chosen process, chemical usage, rinse checks, and before-and-after operating readings. Use those records to decide when the exchanger needs its next inspection. The wider [HVAC and water-system application guide](/industries/hvac-water) covers related cleaning needs.
