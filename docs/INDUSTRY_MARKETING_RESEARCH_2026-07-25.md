# MASEST / VertKleen Industry Marketing Research

Date: 2026-07-25
Scope: current repository, the complete `~/Desktop/masest` master set, and current primary-source requirements for every maintained industry route.

## Executive judgment

The industry library is structurally strong but commercially incomplete.

- **Relevance is high.** All 27 maintained routes now describe a real asset, soil/deposit, method, process boundary, verification endpoint, material risk, and wastewater path. That is much stronger than generic “safe green chemical” marketing.
- **Record verification is complete; claim scope remains bounded.** `data/industry-applications.json` records 10 routes with verified public field records as `context_only` and 17 as `absent`; none is reclassified as qualified performance proof. Generated task scenes remain labeled, and exact-record verification does not broaden technical or procurement claims.
- **Audience reach was repaired in this pass.** The hub and each canonical parent now link all 11 narrower buyer routes; previously, the public hub hid 41% of the library.
- **Prominent claim conflicts were corrected in this pass.** Named distribution-center “proof,” brewery “proven” wording, absolute healthcare/fume claims, exact-product NSF implications, broad “safer” language, and compliance language were removed or narrowed to current-document, compatibility, and controlled-trial review.
- **Product reach is concentrated.** HCR appears on 16/27 routes, CR HD and MultiWash on 14/27 each, and Descaler on 11/27. This can support a compelling four-product job router, but without task-specific proof it can also read as the same products forced into every vertical.

Best commercial position: **“Give us the asset, deposit, materials, shutdown window, and wastewater route. We will return a documented compatibility-and-trial plan.”** This is credible across every sector and aligns with the current page architecture.

## Current architecture and layout

Every maintained sector route follows a useful B2B sequence:

1. sector-specific outcome and operating-pressure hero;
2. “why VertKleen fits” positioning;
3. buyer documentation requirements;
4. labeled task imagery;
5. recommended products;
6. `asset → soil → method → concentration → boundary → verification`;
7. controlled technical documents;
8. prefilled quote, compatibility review, controlled trial, and supply CTAs.

That sequence matches technical buying better than a lifestyle-first layout. Keep it.

Highest-value layout changes:

1. **Implemented: expose supplemental pages.** A “Specific operation?” block now appears on each canonical page and the hub:
   - Manufacturing → Aviation / FBO / MRO.
   - Food & Beverage → Breweries / Distilleries / Wineries; Agriculture; Restaurants / Commercial Kitchens.
   - Construction → Drone Cleaning; Pressure / Soft Wash.
   - Distribution / Cold Storage → Fleet / Trucking / Car Wash; Warehousing / Distribution.
   - Healthcare → Senior Living.
   - Marine → Marinas / Boatyards.
   - HVAC / Water → Mechanical Contractors / Water Treatment.
2. **Put the buying proof before the product grid.** A technical buyer first needs substrate compatibility, exact method, wastewater route, and a credible endpoint. Product cards should follow that evidence.
3. **Add role-specific entry points.** “I manage maintenance,” “I own EHS/compliance,” “I buy chemicals,” and “I run a service company” should change proof and CTA wording, not create duplicate pages.
4. **Label evidence classes visibly.** Use `Technical document`, `Illustrative task scene`, `Field context`, `Controlled trial`, and `Approved case study`. Never let all five look like equivalent proof.
5. **Add a compact trial economics module.** Ask for current chemical, dilution, labor, water, downtime, disposal, and asset life. Return cost per completed task, not bottle-price comparison alone.

## Immediate claim and copy corrections

These are marketing-risk and trust problems, not minor style edits.
The wording below records the pre-change baseline found during this audit. This pass corrected the repository-controlled public copy and completed current record/publication verification; unsupported certification, equivalency, endorsement, and broader performance claims remain excluded.

1. **Distribution / Cold Storage:** `industries.html` and the route metadata used named-customer “documented results” and “proof” language. The source remains restricted because it includes confidential customer/contact/commercial detail. Public copy now uses an anonymized assessment summary and does not assert endorsement or verified performance.
2. **Food & Beverage:** “CR and HCR proven on brewery and distillery jobs” overstates unsigned trial notes. Use “documented in MASEST field-trial notes; request the trial protocol and evidence status.”
3. **Healthcare:** “no fume events, no evacuations, no shutting a wing” is absolute and unsupported. Use “screened for occupied-facility constraints; work remains subject to facility infection-control, ventilation, isolation, and shutdown approval.”
4. **Manufacturing:** “less HazCom paperwork” implies regulatory relief. OSHA Hazard Communication still requires classification, labels, SDS access, training, and workplace controls where applicable. Use “a simpler chemical review package” only when the exact SKU documents support it. [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200).
5. **Construction / hotel / property copy:** replace broad “safer around the crew” and “does not create fume issues” wording with exact hazard-classification and procedure language from the current SKU SDS/label.
6. **HVAC / Data Centers:** a chemical does not make a site “Legionella compliant.” Say “supports the facility's water-management plan when used under the approved program”; keep ASHRAE 188/CDC roles separate from product performance.
7. **Municipal / Water Utilities:** do not imply exact-product NSF/ANSI 60 certification until the certificate holder, exact trade name/SKU, scope, listing, and private-label equivalence are in the file. The repository's current official-directory check found no exact WaterSafe60 or VertKleen name match.
8. **Purgo:** do not use “EPA registered.” Current local evidence supports, at most, a conditional FIFRA 25(b) minimum-risk position whose ingredients, label claims, and state registrations must satisfy every applicable condition. [EPA minimum-risk conditions](https://www.epa.gov/minimum-risk-pesticides/conditions-minimum-risk-pesticides).
9. **Government:** verify current SAM.gov status, CAGE, representations, country of origin, and solicitation fit before saying “ready.” A CAGE code is not product approval.
10. **All sectors:** HMIS, transport status, “biodegradable,” “non-toxic,” “noncorrosive,” OEM, customer, environmental, disinfectant, and certification claims must remain exact-SKU and evidence-scoped. Follow the current claim ledger, not older collateral.

## Sector playbook

Buyer roles below are marketing synthesis from each operation's responsibilities. Validate named titles account by account.

### 1. Oil, gas, and process plants

- **Jobs and pressure:** isolated valves/manifolds, exchangers, pumps, fin-fan coolers, and tanks; hydrocarbon soil, rust, scale, and turnaround deadlines.
- **Hard boundary:** de-inventory, isolate, gas-free, test atmosphere, manage confined-space/hot-work hazards, capture residues, and recommission under the site's procedure. OSHA describes tank-cleaning work as a full sequence from decommissioning through gas freeing, atmospheric testing, cleaning, and recommissioning. [OSHA tank-cleaning activities](https://www.osha.gov/storage-tanks/work-activities).
- **Buyers:** turnaround manager, maintenance/reliability, operations, EHS, contractor manager, procurement.
- **Sell:** a contained, materials-checked trial for already isolated assets; lead with endpoint, waste characterization, return-to-service record, and shutdown-window fit. Never show chemistry being applied to live or uninventoried equipment.
- **Proof/image:** gas-free tag, blinds/LOTO, contained pump skid or exchanger, pre/post deposit measurement, wastewater tote/manifests. Local engineering rust/descaling material is context, not oil-and-gas customer proof.

### 2. Marine, marinas, and boatyards

- **Jobs and pressure:** hull/waterline film, aluminum oxidation, bilges, engines/parts, decks, marina pads, salt and oily soil; finish damage and direct-to-water discharge are core objections.
- **Hard boundary:** vessel/boatyard washwater and stormwater rules are location and activity specific. EPA has enforced failures to prevent boat pressure-wash wastewater reaching surface water. [EPA marina/boatyard enforcement](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epa/newsreleases/epa-enforcement-actions-reduce-pollution-and-improve-compliance-clean-water-laws-marina.html).
- **Buyers:** yard manager, service manager, marina environmental manager, fleet owner, mobile marine-wash contractor.
- **Sell:** coating/metal compatibility plus contained-pad procedure; “clean finish without avoidable coating or soft-metal damage” is stronger than generic eco language.
- **Proof/image:** same vessel, same angle, measured dwell, runoff recovery, finish inspection. Local vessel and airboat files are useful leads, but the airboat source is text-poor and neither is an approved case study.

### 3. Manufacturing

- **Jobs and pressure:** stamping presses, extrusion/processing equipment, parts washers, reusable filters, floors, tools, oil/grease, rust, and scale; downtime dominates.
- **Hard boundary:** cleaning during servicing can trigger hazardous-energy control. OSHA explicitly includes cleaning where unexpected energization or stored energy can injure workers. [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147).
- **Buyers:** maintenance manager, reliability engineer, plant manager, EHS, quality, procurement.
- **Sell:** offline procedure, material/elastomer compatibility, cycle time, residue/rinse endpoint, parts-ready or inspection-ready result, and line restart record.
- **Proof/image:** isolated press/guarding, parts washer, filter before/after, residue test. K&N filter material is a useful task case, not plant-wide proof.

### 4. Aviation, FBOs, MROs, and airports

- **Jobs and pressure:** approved exterior cleaning, wheels/ground-support equipment, hangar floors, parts, exhaust tracks, and corrosion-control preparation.
- **Hard boundary:** aircraft manufacturer or operator maintenance programs take precedence; FAA AC 43-4B warns that cleaner concentration and pH can cause damage and points to approved cleaning compounds. [FAA AC 43-4B](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentid/1034449).
- **Buyers:** director of maintenance, MRO production/quality, corrosion-control engineer, FBO ops, airport facilities/EHS, approved-vendor procurement.
- **Sell:** never “aircraft safe” or “Boeing/Airbus approved” without exact current qualification. Sell document review, coupon compatibility, ground-support/hangar pilot, and OEM-program alignment.
- **Proof/image:** approved substrate coupons, GSE wash rack, hangar floor with FOD control, technician following task card. Avoid implying flight-article use from a generic aircraft hero.

### 5. Distribution, cold storage, warehousing, and 3PL

- **Jobs and pressure:** freezer/warehouse floors, tire marks, pallet grime, docks, forklifts, evaporator coils, condensate, drains, and industrial refrigeration assets; slip risk, food segregation, temperature uptime, and ammonia safety.
- **Hard boundary:** food facilities require sanitary operations; ammonia refrigeration may trigger process-safety and Risk Management Plan duties. EPA enforcement highlights corrosion, ice buildup, incomplete maintenance procedures, and forklift-impact protection. [EPA refrigerated-warehouse case](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/us-epa-requires-hawaiis-largest-refrigerated-food-warehouse-improve-chemical-safety).
- **Buyers:** facility engineering, refrigeration manager, sanitation, safety, operations, loss prevention, procurement.
- **Sell:** separate three programs—floor/dock soil, food-area sanitation sequence, and isolated refrigeration maintenance. Lead with low-foam recovery, dry-down/reopening, aisle uptime, and wastewater routing.
- **Proof/image:** working auto-scrubber/recovery, evaporator or exchanger under isolation, floor coefficient/slip endpoint, dock/forklift context. Owner-confirmed field photos may remain public context; the customer source remains restricted and only its anonymized summary may appear publicly.

### 6. Food and beverage processing

- **Jobs and pressure:** tanks, kettles, fermenters, lines, conveyors, exchangers, krausen/organic film, beer or mineral stone, and pre-sanitation cleaning; contamination, allergens, residue, and release-to-production control matter more than “green.”
- **Hard boundary:** FDA CGMPs cover plant equipment, sanitary operations, facility sanitation, and process controls. [FDA CGMP overview](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/current-good-manufacturing-practices-cgmps-food-and-dietary-supplements). Cleaning is not sanitizing.
- **Buyers:** sanitation manager, QA/food safety, plant engineer, maintenance, production, EHS, procurement.
- **Sell:** soil-specific alkaline/acid-replacement sequence, exact concentration/temperature/flow/dwell, final-rinse endpoint, allergen controls, material compatibility, and QA release record.
- **Proof/image:** CIP skid tied to a named vessel, spray-ball coverage, conductivity/pH endpoint, exchanger plates, production-release signoff. Keep food-contact claims exact and documented.

### 7. Breweries, distilleries, and wineries

- **Jobs and pressure:** cellar tanks, beer/wine stone, lines, draught systems, exchangers, caustic/acid handling, flavor risk, and short CIP windows.
- **Industry requirement:** Brewers Association guidance treats line cleaning as a documented recurring process; its manual calls for routine line cleaning at least every 14 days and quarterly acid cleaning, with manufacturer directions and final water/pH checks. [Brewers Association Draught Beer Quality Manual](https://www.brewersassociation.org/educational-publications/draught-beer-quality-manual/).
- **Buyers:** owner/brewmaster, cellar lead, QA, packaging, maintenance, taproom/line-service operator.
- **Sell:** measured CR/HCR trial against the customer's current cycle, not a universal replacement claim. Compare chemical use, labor, cycle time, final rinse, sensory/QA release, and scale endpoint.
- **Proof/image:** same tank/line, verified concentration, circulation record, rinse endpoint, swab/inspection, signed brewer authorization. The local trial set is the strongest vertical evidence in the master files but still needs formal case-study release.

### 8. Agriculture and farm operations

- **Jobs and pressure:** harvest/packing equipment, bins, belts, hoppers/augers, dairy equipment, irrigation hardware, heavy equipment, fertilizer/mineral deposits, and biofilm/organic soil.
- **Hard boundary:** FDA's Produce Safety Rule covers equipment, tools, buildings, sanitation, agricultural water, and contamination controls for covered produce. [FDA Produce Safety Rule overview](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/what-produce-safety-rule-means-consumers).
- **Buyers:** farm/packinghouse manager, food-safety lead, dairy sanitation, equipment maintenance, irrigation manager.
- **Sell:** keep food-contact and nonfood equipment programs separate. Lead with cleanability, rinse/sanitize sequence, crop/animal/exposure boundary, seasonal downtime, and water-management plan.
- **Proof/image:** packing belt/bin or dairy CIP with food-contact boundary; contained machinery wash; irrigation valve/scale close-up. Do not use a generic tractor wash as proof for produce sanitation.

### 9. Restaurants and commercial kitchens

- **Jobs and pressure:** hood filters, fry-line grease, stainless equipment, floors, walls, drains, and nonfood-contact exteriors; reopening time, slip risk, odor, and health inspection.
- **Hard boundary:** the FDA Food Code separates cleaning from sanitizing; food-contact surfaces must be clean to sight and touch and require task/frequency-specific cleaning. [FDA Food Code 2022](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022). Use the site's adopted jurisdictional code.
- **Buyers:** owner/operator, facilities, kitchen manager, sanitation contractor, hood-service contractor.
- **Sell:** a four-step operating story—remove gross soil, clean, rinse, then apply the facility-approved sanitizer where required. State food-contact/rinse/reopening limits explicitly.
- **Proof/image:** filter soak/rinse/clean sequence, greasy floor with recovery and dry slip-safe endpoint, stainless appliance exterior. Local hood, floor, and CR HD files are valuable task leads but need controlled replication.

### 10. Healthcare and senior living

- **Jobs and pressure:** HVAC/water-side assets, exchangers, occupied-building exteriors, ordinary soil removal, resident spaces, and environmental-services workflows; infection prevention and continuity dominate.
- **Hard boundary:** CDC guidance requires room-specific protocols, training, defined responsibility, monitoring, and cleaning before an EPA-registered hospital disinfectant where disinfection is indicated; facility risk and policy control the method. [CDC infection-control guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthcare-associated-infections/hcp/infection-control/index.html); [EPA registered disinfectants](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/selected-epa-registered-disinfectants).
- **Buyers:** facilities director, plant operations, environmental services, infection prevention, Materials Management/supply chain, water-management team, safety.
- **Sell:** keep mechanical-water treatment, ordinary cleaning, and registered disinfection as separate offers. Lead with occupied-space planning, isolation, ventilation, water-management documentation, and exact approval chain.
- **Proof/image:** isolated exchanger/mechanical room, EVS workflow with zone controls, canopy/glass work with pedestrian isolation. Never use patient imagery or imply infection reduction without a study and approved claim.

### 11. Education facilities

- **Jobs and pressure:** hydronic exchangers, AHU coils, cafeterias, restrooms, gyms, locker rooms, walkways, and district-wide chemical review; summer windows and occupied-campus safety.
- **Hard boundary:** EPA school guidance emphasizes chemical inventory, proper storage, labels/SDS, staff training, ventilation, and less-hazardous purchasing—not blanket product safety. [EPA chemicals and cleaning supplies at school](https://www.epa.gov/schools/using-chemicals-and-cleaning-supplies-school).
- **Buyers:** facilities director, maintenance/HVAC, custodial director, EHS/risk, food service, purchasing.
- **Sell:** campus-standardization audit, summer pilot, approved-product packet, per-building use map, simple staff SOP, measurable service level, cost per diluted gallon, and inventory reduction. APPA frames task frequency, staffing, safety, service levels, and measurement as core educational-facility cleaning concerns. [APPA cleaning operations](https://www.appa.org/cleaning-operations). Local district material gives regional relevance but needs permission and exact case boundaries.
- **Proof/image:** campus asset with zero students present, controlled wet-floor zone, hydronic/HVAC work under isolation, summer schedule.

### 12. HVAC, water treatment, and mechanical contractors

- **Jobs and pressure:** cooling towers, closed loops, heat exchangers, boilers, coils, strainers, scale/corrosion/biofouling, commissioning, and service callbacks.
- **Hard boundary:** water-management standards assign organizational duties; product use must fit the facility's program. DOE notes that fouling increases chilled-water load and that side-stream filtration can reduce cleaning frequency, downtime, water loss, and chemical use. [DOE cooling-tower filtration](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/water-efficient-technology-opportunity-side-stream-filtration-cooling-towers).
- **Buyers:** service contractor owner, project manager, commissioning agent, facility engineer, water-treatment provider, water-management team.
- **Sell:** white-label-capable service package: sample/testing, deposit identification, materials review, temporary recirculation method, endpoint, neutralization/waste plan, recommissioning report, and recurring treatment.
- **Proof/image:** tube sheet/plate exchanger with contained cart, tower fill during shutdown, trend or approach-temperature data. Do not promise energy savings without baseline and post-clean measurement.

### 13. Data centers

- **Jobs and pressure:** chilled/condenser-water loops, cooling towers, CDUs, plate exchangers, strainers, pumps, and coils; uptime, thermal risk, change control, water use, and contamination near energized space.
- **Operational basis:** DOE notes that 24/7 data centers have persistent cooling demand and that water performance depends on every step of heat removal; O&M changes must protect IT equipment from contaminants and humidity. [DOE data-center cooling water](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/cooling-water-efficiency-opportunities-federal-data-centers).
- **Buyers:** critical-facilities manager, mechanical engineer, reliability/change-control board, EHS, water-treatment vendor, procurement.
- **Sell:** “mechanical-space only, isolated asset, written MOP/SOP/EOP alignment, compatibility review, contained flush, measurable return-to-service.” Treat white-space/electronics cleaning as excluded unless an OEM-approved procedure exists: equipment makers may prohibit ammonia, peroxide, chlorine, solvents, direct spray, or other methods on specific surfaces. [Dell equipment-cleaning guidance](https://www.dell.com/support/kbdoc/en-us/000133659/guidance-for-keeping-your-dell-technologies-equipment-clean).
- **Proof/image:** opened exchanger/CDU in mechanical space, containment, tagged isolation, trend data, no exposed electronics. If depicting white space, show dry/HEPA particulate control and external surfaces only—not liquid near live racks. Local regional and hyperscale documents are prospect research, not customer proof.

### 14. Plumbing

- **Jobs and pressure:** tankless heaters, heat exchangers, fixtures, valves, fire-system components, and calcium/rust scale; occupied-building scheduling and material/seal compatibility.
- **Hard boundary:** follow equipment-manufacturer maintenance procedures; direct drinking-water treatment and system chemicals may invoke different certification requirements than temporary offline descaling.
- **Buyers:** plumbing contractor, service manager, facility engineer, property manager.
- **Sell:** closed-loop service kit with exact equipment/material screen, measured flow/temperature, flush endpoint, wastewater route, and recommissioning checklist. Market reduced acid-handling burden only if exact SDS comparison supports it.
- **Proof/image:** tankless isolation-valve setup, pump/cart, scale mass or flow recovery, clean flange. Local fire-system and bathroom-scale files are context; do not generalize them across potable systems.

### 15. Municipalities and water utilities

- **Jobs and pressure:** pumps, impellers, valves, lift-station components, storage, treatment equipment, public-works fleets, bid review, and service continuity.
- **Hard boundary:** distinguish temporary offline maintenance from chemicals introduced into drinking water. Wastewater discharge follows permits and pretreatment/local authority requirements. [EPA municipal wastewater](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/municipal-wastewater); [NSF water/wastewater standards](https://www.nsf.org/nsf-standards/standards-portfolio/water-wastewater-standards).
- **Buyers:** utility maintenance, treatment superintendent, engineering, safety, purchasing, public works.
- **Sell:** frame chemistry and cleaning inside asset management: required service, lifecycle cost, equipment condition, and documented O&M. EPA addresses owners, managers, operators, and local officials around service level and lowest lifecycle cost. [EPA asset management guide](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-04/documents/epa816f08014.pdf). Add bid-ready technical packet, offline pilot, exact certificate/equivalency record where applicable, lot traceability, disposal route, and emergency-stock plan.
- **Proof/image:** removed pump/valve on contained bench, lift-station component above ground, clear public-works context. Avoid unsupported “NSF-60 product” badges.

### 16. Fleet, trucking, and car washes

- **Jobs and pressure:** exteriors, undercarriages, trailers, engine bays, shop floors, parts, road film, salt, grease, water/reclaim cost, throughput, and finish damage.
- **Hard boundary:** EPA recommends designated impermeable wash areas that contain, treat, reuse, or route washwater with sewer-authority approval; polluted washwater should not reach storm drains. [EPA municipal vehicle/equipment washing BMP](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-municipal-vehicle-and-equipment-washing.pdf).
- **Buyers:** fleet maintenance, wash-bay manager, dealership/detail director, transit shop, EHS, owner/operator.
- **Sell:** cost per vehicle/turn, dilution control, reclaim compatibility, finish/metal coupon test, dry-down, and bay throughput. Separate exterior wash, heavy degreasing, and aluminum brightening.
- **Proof/image:** active wash rack with berm/reclaim, same vehicle before/after, gloss/finish inspection, measured chemical and water use. The local Durango case is a lead, not fleet-scale validation.

### 17. Military and government

- **Jobs and pressure:** fleet/depot/GSE, public works, pumps, parts, floors, transit and facility assets; solicitation fit, traceability, sourcing, lead time, and approved-product controls.
- **Procurement basis:** federal purchasers use solicitation-specific requirements and EPA-recommended standards/ecolabels; DoD also weighs mission performance, readiness, resilience, safety, supply, lifecycle cost, and applicable Military Specifications. Exact current listings matter. [EPA Buying Green](https://www.epa.gov/greenerproducts/buying-green-federal-purchasers); [DoD sustainable products](https://www.denix.osd.mil/spc/).
- **Buyers:** contracting officer/specialist, program/facility manager, depot maintenance, environmental, quality.
- **Sell:** bid-ready packet: legal entity, SAM/CAGE status, country of origin, insurance, SDS/TDS, lot traceability, shelf life, lead time, exact certifications, equivalency matrix, and pilot protocol.
- **Proof/image:** public-works/depot asset, controlled wash/recovery, parts bench, lot/document packet. Never imply “military approved,” NAVSEA approval, or agency endorsement from a logo or generic customer history.

### 18. Construction

- **Jobs and pressure:** concrete pumps/forms/tools/mixers, form oil, grease, uncured residue, efflorescence, hardscape, coating/turnover prep, and narrow cleanup windows.
- **Hard boundary:** OSHA restricts dry sweeping/brushing where it can contribute to respirable-silica exposure; EPA construction permits prohibit uncontrolled washout and soaps/solvents in equipment washwater. [OSHA silica rule](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1153); [EPA construction stormwater](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/stormwater-discharges-construction-activities).
- **Buyers:** superintendent, equipment/fleet manager, concrete contractor, coatings contractor, safety, environmental, rental yard.
- **Sell:** clean before residue cures; surface-specific trial; contained washout; coating-ready endpoint. Avoid one “concrete cleaner” claim across fresh residue, cured scale, decorative finish, and coated metal.
- **Proof/image:** concrete pump/formwork with contained washout, wet/HEPA silica-aware housekeeping, surface profile/appearance endpoint.

### 19. Pressure-washing, soft-wash, and drone-cleaning contractors

- **Jobs and pressure:** facades, roofs, hardscape, glass, canopies, inaccessible surfaces, vegetation/overspray, payload compatibility, runoff, labor, and property-damage risk.
- **Hard boundary:** Part 107 limits commercial UAS operations and prohibits hazardous-material carriage/transport; payload, total weight, controllability, people/airspace, and platform approval remain operator duties. [FAA Part 107](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/small-unmanned-aircraft-systems-uas-regulations-part-107); [FAA UAS hazardous materials](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/air_carriers/operations/drones).
- **Buyers:** exterior-service owner, remote pilot/ops manager, property/facility manager, insurer/risk reviewer.
- **Sell:** contractor system, not “drone-rated chemical”: SDS classification, payload/pump/seal/nozzle compatibility, droplet/overspray control, substrate/vegetation test, exclusion zone, runoff plan, labor/coverage economics.
- **Proof/image:** actual connected drone or soft-wash system, exclusion zone, ground observer, runoff capture, no detached fantasy tools. Local university-site drone material is promising context but needs authorization and method detail.

### 20. Golf courses and sports facilities

- **Jobs and pressure:** reel mowers, carts, maintenance pads, irrigation valves, sprinklers, locker rooms, stadium hardscape, turf-adjacent soil, water use, and member/guest disruption.
- **Hard boundary:** course BMPs emphasize site-specific water, chemical, spill, wash-pad, and environmental practices; pesticide/antimicrobial claims remain label-controlled. Equipment may be washed daily, and clippings/washwater can add nutrient load; contained pads, collection, filtration, and reuse are more persuasive than “biodegradable.” [GCSAA BMP hub](https://www.gcsaa.org/facility/environment-hub/best-management-practices); [USGA washwater-recycling case](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/course-care/water-resource-center/bmp-case-studies/2017/equipment-wash-water-recycling.html).
- **Buyers:** superintendent, equipment manager, irrigation technician, clubhouse/facility director, athletics operations.
- **Sell:** three zones—equipment wash pad, irrigation maintenance, and guest-facing property cleaning. Lead with asset life, reel/finish compatibility, contained washwater, scheduled dry-down, and turf/landscape boundary.
- **Proof/image:** mower/cart on compliant pad, removed irrigation valve, locker/stadium hardscape with reopening controls. Avoid pristine-course lifestyle imagery with no cleaning task.

### 21. Hotels, resorts, and property management

- **Jobs and pressure:** facades, pool waterlines, bathrooms, kitchens/hood filters, laundry, HVAC, hardscape, mold/algae/odor complaints, guest disruption, and brand standards.
- **Hard boundary:** pool operations follow adopted health codes; water-management and disinfection remain separate from ordinary cleaning. CDC's MAHC is model guidance, not itself federal law. [CDC Model Aquatic Health Code](https://www.cdc.gov/model-aquatic-health-code/index.html).
- **Buyers:** chief engineer, facilities/property manager, housekeeping, pool operator, food and beverage, regional procurement.
- **Sell:** guest-safe scheduling rather than “fume-free”: low-visibility work windows, area isolation, ventilation, dry/reopen criteria, substrate test, and one-vendor task map. Labor, utilities, and supply costs are active hotel pressures, so show verified dilution cost, workflow steps, training time, water/product use, and reopening speed. [AHLA 2026 operating-pressure survey](https://www.ahla.com/news/rising-cost-staffing-challenges-persist-hotels-travel-demand-expected-hold-steady).
- **Proof/image:** pool-waterline scale, facade wash with guest exclusion, laundry/hood-filter cleaning, mechanical room. Show the operational controls, not luxury lifestyle stock.

### 22. Solar farms and panel cleaning

- **Jobs and pressure:** dust, pollen, bird debris, bio-soiling, glass/coating abrasion, water scarcity, electrical risk, robot/brush compatibility, and production-loss economics.
- **Operational basis:** NREL quantifies site-specific soiling losses; IEA PVPS warns that cleaning can scratch glass/coatings or damage cells and recommends qualifying the intended module/soil/device/water/agent combination. OEM instructions control: for example, First Solar's Series 4A guide prohibits wet contact tools and warns that noncompliance can affect warranty. [NREL soiling map](https://www.nrel.gov/pv/soiling); [IEA PVPS soiling report](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IEA-PVPS-T13-21-2022-REPORT-Soiling-Losses-PV-Plants.pdf); [First Solar Series 4 guide](https://www.firstsolar.com/-/media/First-Solar/Technical-Documents/User-Guides/Series-4-Module-User-Guide-Global.pdf).
- **Buyers:** asset/O&M manager, performance engineer, cleaning contractor, robot provider, EHS, warranty manager.
- **Sell:** first diagnose whether cleaning is economic from measured soiling loss; then obtain module/warranty approval and validate water quality, brush/robot force, additive compatibility, temperature/time window, and post-clean power recovery.
- **Proof/image:** connected soft-brush/robot on the exact array type, no walking on modules, electrical/isolation controls, pre/post soiling ratio or yield. Never market a universal “panel-safe” chemical.

## Cross-sector content system

### One intake schema

Use the same six buyer inputs on every page and in every quote:

1. asset and exact substrate/coating/elastomers;
2. soil/deposit and suspected composition;
3. current method/chemical and dissatisfaction;
4. temperature, flow/agitation, dwell, shutdown, rinse, and occupancy constraints;
5. wastewater/recovery/disposal route;
6. measurable acceptance endpoint.

The website already carries most of this into contact CTAs. Make it visible as the core sales process.

### One evidence ladder

1. **Technical fit:** current label/SDS/TDS and material compatibility.
2. **Bench/coupon test:** customer soil/substrate, controlled method, recorded endpoint.
3. **Site pilot:** exact asset, controls, chain of custody, wastewater, result, and limitations.
4. **Approved case study:** signed customer/OEM authorization and public-use scope.
5. **Program evidence:** repeat sites, reliability/cost data, revision-controlled SOP.

No current route reaches level 4 based on the repository evidence status.

### Sector-specific conversion assets

Each page should have:

- one buyer-specific one-page trial brief;
- one material-compatibility table;
- one method card with dilution/temperature/dwell/rinse ranges only where label/TDS supports them;
- one wastewater/reopening boundary;
- one downloadable evidence-status sheet;
- one cost-per-task calculator;
- one real case-study slot that stays visibly empty until approved.

### Imagery standard

Keep the current documentary task style:

- real asset, visible soil/deposit, mechanically plausible tool connection;
- isolation/containment/recovery visible where required;
- before/process/after sequence at matched angle;
- exact industry setting without logos or implied customer endorsement;
- no exaggerated foam, chemical suits without task basis, product pouring into live systems, uncontrolled runoff, disconnected hoses, or people beside energized/open equipment.

Master-file photos should enter the public site only after four checks: source and owner, publication permission, task/date/product/method record, and limitations. Otherwise use them in internal sales enablement or label them `Field context — not performance proof`.
