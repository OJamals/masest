# Industry Cleaning Source Notes

Research date: 2026-07-24
Scope: source-backed content guidance for MASEST industry pages; not a current-site audit or legal opinion.

## Source labels

- **Regulation:** binding federal requirement within its stated scope; state, local, permit, contract, and site requirements may add obligations.
- **Standard:** consensus or certification standard; binding only when adopted by law, permit, specification, contract, or owner policy.
- **Guidance:** nonbinding regulator, government-lab, or industry-body practice.
- **Procurement:** buyer qualification or contracting requirement, not proof of legal compliance.

## Baseline evidence every industry page should offer

- Current SDS and TDS: composition disclosure permitted by law, hazards/PPE, use dilution, temperature, contact time, agitation, rinse/neutralization, storage, shelf life, and disposal.
- Substrate-compatibility matrix: carbon steel, stainless grades, aluminum, copper/brass, galvanized steel, glass, coatings, concrete/stone, plastics, and common elastomers; identify test method and exposure conditions.
- Task-matched performance evidence: named soil/deposit, concentration, temperature, dwell time, mechanical action, rinse volume, before/after endpoint, and control/comparator.
- Environmental-claim support: VOC, biodegradability, aquatic toxicity, phosphorus, pH, and wastewater statements backed by test reports. Never imply drain, stormwater, potable-water, food-contact, disinfectant, or environmental approval without the applicable document.
- One industry case study: equipment/process, soil, prior method, exact procedure, labor/downtime/water outcome, compatibility observation, and customer-verifiable result.
- Claim discipline: cleaning is not sanitizing or disinfecting. Show EPA registration and label directions only when making pesticidal/disinfectant claims; show NSF, Safer Choice, or other certification only when the exact product and manufacturing location appear in the certifier’s current listing.

## 1. Oil & Gas

**High-value task targets**

- Degrease isolated valve manifolds, pumps, skids, heat exchangers, and maintenance tools.
- Remove oily residues from tank exteriors, loading areas, secondary containment, and non-slip walkways.
- Pre-clean isolated equipment before inspection, coating, gasket work, or non-hot maintenance.
- Support tank-outage cleaning only as one controlled step inside a confined-space, isolation, gas-freeing, and waste-recovery plan.

**Buyer concerns**

- Tank work can involve fire/explosion, toxic atmosphere, oxygen deficiency, falls, and permit-required confined spaces; product prose must never make tank entry sound routine. **Guidance + referenced regulation:** [OSHA Storage Tank Hazard Solutions](https://www.osha.gov/storage-tanks/hazard-solutions).
- Cleaning-related oily water and recovered residue require site-specific containment and disposal; SPCC applicability depends on facility conditions and oil-storage thresholds. **Regulation/guidance:** [EPA SPCC](https://www.epa.gov/oil-spills-prevention-and-preparedness-regulations/spill-prevention-control-and-countermeasure-spcc).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- SDS/TDS; flash point/VOC data; carbon-steel, stainless, coating, gasket, and seal compatibility.
- Closed-system or controlled-wash procedure with LOTO/isolation boundary, recovery method, rinse endpoint, and waste characterization.
- Case study on pump/skid or heat-exchanger cleaning; do not use a tank-entry case unless competent-party safety controls and customer approval are documented.

## 2. Marine

**High-value task targets**

- Remove salt, oxidation, waterline film, and biological residue from above-water hull, topside, rail, and deck surfaces.
- Degrease bilges, engine-room floors, machinery housings, and maintenance parts with washwater capture.
- Clean aluminum workboats, fiberglass/gelcoat, painted steel, stainless hardware, and nonskid decking using substrate-specific procedures.
- Pre-clean isolated tanks or compartments before inspection/repair only under shipyard gas-freeing and confined-space controls.

**Buyer concerns**

- Vessel discharges include oily bilgewater, graywater, deck runoff, and cleaning-related streams; applicable requirements vary by vessel and discharge. **Regulation/guidance:** [EPA Commercial Vessel Discharge Standards](https://www.epa.gov/vessels-marinas-and-ports/commercial-vessel-discharge-standards).
- Shipboard tank cleaning can create explosive/toxic atmospheres and may require marine-chemist certification before hot work. **Guidance + referenced regulation:** [OSHA Shipbuilding Tank Cleaning](https://www.osha.gov/ship-building-repair/tank-cleaning).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Compatibility by marine substrate/coating: aluminum, gelcoat, antifouling/topcoat, teak, acrylic glazing, sealants, and nonskid.
- Aquatic-toxicity/phosphorus data plus explicit washwater-capture and disposal guidance; never advertise “safe to discharge” without jurisdiction- and use-specific proof.
- Boatyard or fleet case study showing salt/grease removal, finish retention, rinse demand, and corrosion observation.

## 3. Manufacturing

**High-value task targets**

- Degrease presses, conveyors, machine bases, guards, tooling, parts, and maintenance benches.
- Remove oil/coolant film and tire marks from production floors and traffic lanes.
- Clean heat exchangers, process piping exteriors, tanks, and utility equipment during planned outages.
- Prepare metal or concrete surfaces for inspection, bonding, painting, or repair.

**Buyer concerns**

- Cleaning that exposes workers to unexpected energization or stored energy falls within hazardous-energy controls; OSHA expressly includes some cleaning and unjamming in servicing/maintenance. **Regulation:** [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147).
- Floors and work areas must remain clean, orderly, sanitary, and as dry as feasible; wet-process areas need drainage and dry standing places where feasible. **Regulation:** [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.22](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Machine/OEM compatibility, paint and elastomer testing, residue/noninterference evidence, and LOTO-aware application SOP.
- Soil-specific test for cutting oil, hydraulic oil, coolant residue, carbon, or shop grime; quantify dwell, temperature, agitation, rinse, and downtime.
- Manufacturing case study with labor, line downtime, wastewater handling, and post-clean surface acceptance.

## 4. Distribution / Cold Storage

**High-value task targets**

- Remove forklift grease, tire marks, food residue, and packaging soil from cold-room floors, docks, and rack bases.
- Clean evaporator housings, fan guards, drip pans, and accessible coil surfaces under OEM-approved shutdown procedures.
- Clean doors, curtains, dock plates, condensate paths, and high-touch material-handling equipment.
- Address freezer mineral/ice-related deposits only after safe defrost/isolation; do not position cleaner as an ice-removal shortcut.

**Buyer concerns**

- Food-holding facilities must prevent contamination and allergen cross-contact; non-food-contact equipment surfaces must be cleaned as often as needed for that purpose. **Regulation:** [21 CFR 117.35](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-117/subpart-B/section-117.35).
- Covered ammonia-refrigeration processes may be subject to OSHA process-safety-management requirements; cleaning must respect refrigeration isolation, contractor, and mechanical-integrity controls. **Regulation:** [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.119).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Low-temperature efficacy curve, freeze point, dwell/rinse behavior, slip-control procedure, and aluminum/galvanized/copper compatibility.
- Food-facility use statement defining where product may be used, required rinse, storage controls, and whether any third-party food-area listing actually applies.
- Cold-store case study measuring soil removal, floor drying/reopening time, labor, and impact on refrigeration uptime.

## 5. Food & Beverage

**High-value task targets**

- CIP of sanitary tanks, piping, fillers, pasteurizers, and heat exchangers using validated circulation, concentration, temperature, time, flow, rinse, and endpoint.
- COP/manual cleaning of removable parts, mixers, conveyors, slicers, and utensils.
- Remove fats, proteins, carbohydrates, mineral scale, and mixed organic/inorganic deposits with soil-specific chemistry.
- Clean floors, drains, walls, and equipment exteriors without spreading contamination or creating aerosols near exposed product.

**Buyer concerns**

- FDA defines CIP as mechanically circulating/flowing detergent, rinse water, and sanitizing solution through piping/equipment surfaces; manual in-place cleaning alone is not CIP. **Model-code guidance:** [FDA Food Code 2022](https://www.fda.gov/media/184685/download).
- Food-plant cleaning must protect against contamination and allergen cross-contact; chemicals must be safe and adequate under conditions of use. **Regulation:** [21 CFR 117.35](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-117/subpart-B/section-117.35).
- Meat/poultry establishments require sanitary operations, Sanitation SOPs, corrective actions, records, and verification. **Regulation:** [9 CFR Part 416](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-9/chapter-III/subchapter-E/part-416).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Dedicated CIP technical guide: conductivity/concentration correlation, soil-loading capacity, temperature/time/velocity window, foaming profile, rinse endpoint, and metallurgy/elastomer compatibility.
- Food-contact rinse instructions, letter of guarantee where applicable, current NSF nonfood-compound listing if held, and clear separation between cleaning and sanitizing claims.
- Validation pack: riboflavin/coverage or equivalent system test, ATP/micro/allergen verification protocol selected by customer, and beverage/dairy/bakery/meat case studies as applicable.

## 6. Healthcare

**High-value task targets**

- Clean environmental surfaces before facility-approved disinfection; distinguish patient-care from nonpatient areas.
- Clean plant-room heat exchangers, cooling towers, boilers, and closed-loop water equipment under facility water-management controls.
- Remove grease/scale in kitchens, laundries, loading/service areas, and nonclinical maintenance shops.
- Clean floors and durable surfaces using low-residue, material-compatible procedures that control splash and aerosols.

**Buyer concerns**

- CDC recommends detergent cleaning for many surfaces and structured environmental-cleaning programs; healthcare infection prevention decides cleaning/disinfection protocols. **Guidance:** [CDC Environmental Infection-Control Recommendations](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/recommendations.html).
- Healthcare cooling towers require proper maintenance; cleaning can aerosolize contaminated water and demands worker/site controls. **Guidance:** [CDC Healthcare Water](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/water.html) and [CDC Cooling-Tower Cleaning Procedure](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/appendix-c-water.html).
- Antimicrobial/disinfectant claims require an EPA-registered product used according to its label; a cleaner should not borrow another product’s registration. **Regulation/resource:** [EPA-Registered Disinfectants](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/selected-epa-registered-disinfectants).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- SDS/TDS, residue and odor/VOC data, surface compatibility, healthcare application SOP, and explicit “cleaner vs disinfectant” claim boundary.
- Water-side case study tied to facility water-management plan, with metallurgy, neutralization, disposal, and return-to-service controls.
- If any VertKleen SKU is EPA registered, link exact EPA label/registration and allowed sites/pathogens/contact times; otherwise omit disinfection claims.

## 7. Construction

**High-value task targets**

- Clean concrete pumps, forms, tools, mixers, saws, and equipment before residue hardens.
- Remove form-release oil, hydraulic grease, asphalt/tar residue, and tracked soil from equipment and slabs.
- Remove efflorescence/mineral deposits from masonry using substrate-tested methods.
- Prepare concrete, masonry, and metal for coating, sealant, inspection, or turnover cleaning.

**Buyer concerns**

- Where housekeeping can contribute to respirable crystalline-silica exposure, OSHA restricts dry sweeping/brushing and compressed-air cleaning unless stated conditions apply; wet methods or HEPA vacuuming are preferred when feasible. **Regulation:** [OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1153).
- Construction stormwater permits require erosion, sediment, and pollution controls; washwater must follow the site SWPPP and permit, not enter storm drains by convenience. **Regulation/guidance:** [EPA Construction Stormwater](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/stormwater-discharges-construction-activities).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Concrete/masonry/metal/coating compatibility, etch profile or appearance data, runoff pH, collection/neutralization procedure, and silica-aware SOP.
- Task demos for uncured concrete residue, cured mineral deposit, form oil, and heavy equipment grease; do not generalize one result to every substrate.
- Contractor case study showing cleanup window, labor, water, wastewater handling, and coating/finish acceptance.

## 8. Military / Government

**High-value task targets**

- Degrease fleet, depot, public-works, and ground-support equipment.
- Clean maintenance-bay floors, tools, parts, loading areas, and spill-containment surfaces.
- Clean building exteriors, transit/public-safety assets, and institutional facilities.
- Support coating/inspection preparation on specified metals and finishes.

**Buyer concerns**

- Federal buyers use applicable statutory purchasing programs plus EPA-recommended standards/ecolabels; claims must match the exact solicitation and current certified-product listing. **Procurement guidance:** [EPA Buying Green for Federal Purchasers](https://www.epa.gov/greenerproducts/buying-green-federal-purchasers) and [EPA Federal Standards/Ecolabel Recommendations](https://www.epa.gov/greenerproducts/recommendations-specifications-standards-and-ecolabels-federal-purchasing).
- Mission readiness, corrosion control, runoff, worker exposure, supply continuity, domestic-sourcing clauses, and approved-product lists are contract/site specific; avoid blanket “military approved” or “government compliant” language.

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Bid-ready packet: SDS/TDS, country of origin, packaging/lot traceability, shelf life, lead time, insurance, certifications actually held, and substitution/equivalency matrix.
- Compatibility evidence for fleet coatings, aluminum, steel, electrical connectors, seals, and nonskid surfaces.
- Government/depot pilot protocol and approved case study with asset type, soil, runoff handling, corrosion observation, labor, and downtime.

## 9. Education

**High-value task targets**

- Clean cafeterias, restrooms, locker rooms, athletic areas, floors, and high-traffic common spaces.
- Remove exterior organic/mineral staining from walks, stairs, entries, bleachers, and masonry.
- Clean HVAC coils, condensate components, and mechanical-room equipment under maintenance controls.
- Address workshop, fleet, kitchen, and facility-maintenance grease without exposing students.

**Buyer concerns**

- EPA notes children may be more sensitive to cleaning chemicals and recommends controlled chemical management, safer products, and trained use. **Guidance:** [EPA Chemicals and Cleaning Supplies at School](https://www.epa.gov/schools/using-chemicals-and-cleaning-supplies-school).
- Cleaning products and maintenance practices can affect school indoor air quality; EPA recommends IAQ-aware preventive maintenance and products with lower human/environmental impact. **Guidance:** [EPA IAQ Preventive Maintenance](https://www.epa.gov/iaq-schools/indoor-air-quality-tools-schools-preventive-maintenance-guidance-documents).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- SDS/TDS, VOC/fragrance/residue data, dilution-control and locked-storage procedure, occupied-space restrictions, floor-slip compatibility, and Safer Choice listing only if current.
- Separate custodial-cleaner claims from EPA-registered disinfectant claims and link exact labels for any registered SKU.
- School/district pilot case study: area closed during use, reopening criteria, labor, finish retention, odor feedback, and procurement documentation.

## 10. HVAC / Water Treatment

**High-value task targets**

- Remove mineral scale, corrosion products, silt, and organic fouling from cooling towers and basins.
- Descale plate-and-frame/shell-and-tube heat exchangers, condensers, boilers, and isolated loops.
- Clean coils, drain pans, strainers, filters, and accessible condensate components.
- Support seasonal startup/shutdown cleaning and side-stream-filtration maintenance.

**Buyer concerns**

- ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 188 establishes minimum Legionellosis risk-management requirements for covered building water systems; cleaning chemistry is one control inside a water-management program, not a substitute for it. **Standard:** [ASHRAE Standard 188 scope](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/standards-and-guidelines/titles-purposes-and-scopes).
- Cooling-tower cleaning requires shutdown, worker PPE, aerosol controls, and compliant water disposal. **Guidance:** [CDC Cooling-Tower Cleaning Procedure](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/appendix-c-water.html).
- Scale, corrosion, solids, and microbiological growth reduce cooling-system performance; side-stream filtration and concentration control are O&M tools. **Guidance:** [DOE Cooling Water Efficiency](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/cooling-water-efficiency-opportunities-federal-data-centers).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Deposit analysis, acid/base capacity, corrosion-rate or coupon data, metallurgy/elastomer compatibility, inhibitor package, neutralization, disposal, and return-to-service SOP.
- System-sizing calculator using volume, deposit load, concentration, circulation rate, temperature, and endpoint.
- Before/after heat-transfer, approach-temperature, pressure-drop, water, and downtime case study; avoid Legionella-control claims absent registered biocide evidence.

## 11. Plumbing

**High-value task targets**

- Closed-loop descale tankless water heaters and isolated heat exchangers.
- Remove mineral deposits from fixtures, valves, aerators, shower components, and nonporous restroom surfaces.
- Clean grease/organic buildup from serviceable drain components using approved mechanical/chemical procedures.
- Restore isolated potable-water equipment only with complete flush, rinse verification, and OEM/site approval.

**Buyer concerns**

- OEM warranty, metallurgy, elastomers, cross-connection control, potable-water exposure, flush volume, neutralization, and disposal drive product acceptance.
- NSF/ANSI/CAN 60 covers health effects of chemicals added to drinking water; NSF/ANSI/CAN 61 covers drinking-water system components. Neither should be claimed for a cleaner unless the exact product/use is within a current listing. **Standards:** [NSF Water and Wastewater Standards](https://www.nsf.org/nsf-standards/standards-portfolio/water-wastewater-standards).
- Centralized building-water work may fall within an owner’s Legionella water-management program. **Standard/guidance:** [ASHRAE Standard 188 and Guideline 12 scopes](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/standards-and-guidelines/titles-purposes-and-scopes).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- OEM-acceptance matrix, metal/elastomer compatibility, closed-loop procedure, concentration/time/temperature limits, neutralization, flush volume, pH/conductivity endpoint, and return-to-service checklist.
- Current NSF listing only if applicable to exact SKU/use/location; otherwise state “not intended to remain in drinking water” only when supported by rinse procedure and technical review.
- Tankless-heater or heat-exchanger case study with model, scale type, pressure/temperature change, water used, and warranty-safe process.

## 12. Data Centers

**High-value task targets**

- Descale isolated plate heat exchangers, condensers, cooling towers, and water-side economizers.
- Clean cooling distribution units, strainers, side-stream filters, and accessible chilled/condenser-water components.
- Remove oil/grease from generator, pump, and mechanical support areas away from energized IT equipment.
- Clean raised-floor/mechanical surfaces only with low-residue, tightly controlled moisture and containment.

**Buyer concerns**

- Data-center cooling commonly transfers heat through chilled-water, condenser-water, heat-exchanger, and cooling-tower stages; fouling control affects water and energy performance. **Guidance:** [DOE Data Center Cooling Water Efficiency](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/cooling-water-efficiency-opportunities-federal-data-centers).
- Uptime, leak detection, energized-equipment separation, maintenance windows, redundancy state, residue, and change-control approval dominate acceptance; page must not depict spraying near live racks.

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Method of procedure: isolation boundary, redundancy/failover state, leak containment, chemical inventory, sensors protected, flush endpoint, waste route, recommissioning, and rollback.
- OEM/metallurgy/elastomer compatibility plus residue/conductivity data for any application near electrical spaces.
- Data-center case study reporting pressure drop, approach temperature, pump/chiller effect, water, downtime, alarms, and zero-impact return to service.

## 13. Golf Courses

**High-value task targets**

- Clean mower decks, reels, utility vehicles, golf carts, and maintenance-shop equipment.
- Degrease wash pads, shop floors, fueling-area surfaces, and parts.
- Remove scale from isolated irrigation strainers, pumps, fittings, and emitters where OEM-approved.
- Clean clubhouse/service hardscapes without runoff onto turf, ponds, or storm drains.

**Buyer concerns**

- Equipment washwater may contain clippings, oil/grease, nutrients, and pesticide rinsate; GCSAA BMPs recommend controlled wash areas, solids/oil separation, water minimization, and no untreated flow to surface water or storm drains. **Industry guidance:** [GCSAA Golf Course BMP example](https://www.gcsaa.org/docs/default-source/environment/ontario-bmps.pdf).
- Turf/ornamental phytotoxicity, irrigation-water chemistry, washwater permits, and pesticide-equipment decontamination are site- and jurisdiction-specific.

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Turf/plant contact limits, rinse and runoff controls, metal/paint/rubber compatibility, wash-pad SOP, and oil/solids-separator compatibility.
- Mower/fleet case study measuring cleaning time, water, clipping/grease removal, corrosion/paint condition, and washwater handling.
- Link facility BMP or state/local washwater requirement alongside product docs; never use generic “biodegradable” wording as discharge permission.

## 14. Solar / Panel Cleaning

**High-value task targets**

- Remove dust, pollen, bird residue, soot, and localized organic deposits from module glass.
- Address hard-water spotting only with module-manufacturer-approved water quality, tools, and chemistry.
- Clean racking and accessible non-electrical surfaces while protecting connectors, junction boxes, seals, and cable management.
- Combine cleaning with visual inspection and performance-based O&M rather than fixed-frequency washing alone.

**Buyer concerns**

- DOE/NREL treat inspection and cleaning as preventive O&M and recommend system-specific plans tied to safe, long-term performance. **Guidance:** [DOE PV Operate and Maintain](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/life-cycle-photovoltaic-systems-operate-and-maintain-existing-photovoltaic-system) and [NREL PV O&M Best Practices](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/89249.pdf).
- Roof/array access, fall protection, electrical isolation, thermal shock, abrasion, anti-reflective coatings, water quality, warranty language, and runoff determine method suitability. **Regulation for covered work:** [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.28](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.28).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Written module-manufacturer compatibility or warranty position; abrasion/residue/reflectance test; water-quality limits; brush/pad specification; temperature and irradiance limits.
- SOP covering de-energization/qualified-person boundary, fall plan, water containment, connector protection, rinse/dry endpoint, and post-clean inspection.
- Solar case study with soiling type, weather, baseline/normalized output, water/labor, modules inspected, defects found, and post-clean performance.

## 15. Municipalities & Water Utilities

**High-value task targets**

- Clean isolated pumps, valves, screens, grit equipment, clarifier components, and maintenance-shop assets.
- Remove scale/corrosion deposits from treatment-process equipment and non-product-contact surfaces.
- Clean potable-water storage facilities only within utility-approved inspection, disinfection, sampling, and return-to-service plans.
- Clean sewer/wastewater equipment while controlling aerosols, confined-space exposure, and treatment-process impacts.

**Buyer concerns**

- Municipal wastewater discharges operate under NPDES permit limits and conditions; cleaning waste must be evaluated against plant process and permit requirements. **Regulation/guidance:** [EPA Municipal Wastewater](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/municipal-wastewater).
- AWWA C652 addresses water-storage-facility preparation, disinfection, sampling, and testing; it is a consensus standard, not a generic cleaner approval. **Standard:** [AWWA C652 scope](https://www.awwa.org/AWWA-Articles/awwa-comment-period-on-awwa-c652-disinfection-of-water-storage-facilities/).
- NSF/ANSI/CAN 60 applies to drinking-water treatment chemicals within its scope; show a current exact-product listing before making the claim. **Standard:** [NSF Water and Wastewater Standards](https://www.nsf.org/nsf-standards/standards-portfolio/water-wastewater-standards).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Utility submittal pack: SDS/TDS, exact intended-contact surfaces, NSF listing if applicable, material compatibility, chemical oxygen demand/pH/metals data where relevant, neutralization, waste route, and flush/sampling endpoint.
- Confined-space/LOTO boundaries and utility-owned disinfection/return-to-service steps clearly separated from cleaner instructions.
- Water/wastewater case study with process unit, deposit analysis, metallurgy, outage, waste volume, post-clean inspection, and operator-verifiable result.

## 16. Hotels / Property Management

**High-value task targets**

- Remove scale, body-oil film, and biofilm-supporting soil from pool tile, deck, grout, showers, and spa surrounds during closure.
- Clean kitchen grease, laundry soils, loading/service areas, floors, façades, walks, and parking assets.
- Clean cooling towers, heat exchangers, boilers, and building-water equipment under the property water-management plan.
- Use surface-specific procedures for natural stone, grout, stainless, glass, sealants, coatings, carpet/fabric, and high-touch finishes.

**Buyer concerns**

- CDC’s Model Aquatic Health Code covers public aquatic-venue operation and maintenance, including hotels, but is guidance unless adopted by a jurisdiction. **Guidance:** [CDC MAHC overview and legal status](https://www.cdc.gov/model-aquatic-health-code/index.html).
- MAHC guidance calls for removal of debris, organic material, and slime/biofilm from accessible aquatic surfaces and chemical use according to instructions; local pool codes remain controlling. **Guidance:** [2023 CDC MAHC](https://www.cdc.gov/model-aquatic-health-code/media/pdfs/2023-mahc-508.pdf).
- Hotel water systems are within ASHRAE Guideline 12’s stated scope, supporting water-management coordination for cooling towers, spas, and centralized potable systems. **Standard/guidance:** [ASHRAE Standard 188 and Guideline 12 scopes](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/standards-and-guidelines/titles-purposes-and-scopes).

**Evidence/materials to link**

- Surface matrix for natural stone, grout, pool finishes, stainless, glass, sealants, carpet/fabric, and slip-resistant flooring; include spot-test and reopening criteria.
- Pool-area SOP: venue closed, chemical isolation, runoff kept out of pool unless specifically approved, rinse/recovery, slip check, water rebalancing, and operator signoff.
- Hotel case study reporting guest-area closure time, odor, finish retention, labor/water, pool-water impact, and return-to-service; separate cleaner claims from EPA-registered disinfectant claims.

## Page-level claim checklist

Before publishing any industry page:

1. Name the exact cleaning task, soil, equipment/surface, and operating boundary.
2. Link current SDS/TDS and compatibility evidence for every promoted SKU.
3. Label sources correctly as regulation, standard, guidance, or procurement requirement.
4. State who owns validation: customer sanitation lead, EHS, infection prevention, water-management team, OEM, engineer, utility operator, or authority having jurisdiction.
5. Remove unqualified claims such as “non-toxic,” “food safe,” “hospital grade,” “NSF approved,” “EPA approved,” “potable-water safe,” “military approved,” “zero corrosion,” or “safe to discharge.”
6. Add one verifiable task-specific case study before making quantified savings, downtime, safety, or performance claims.
