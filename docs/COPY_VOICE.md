# MASEST / VertKleen — Customer Voice Guide

Write for customers, not lawyers, chemists, or internal teams. Sound clear, warm, confident, and useful. Keep product names, prices, numbers, and claims accurate, but rewrite everything else as freely as needed to make it easy to understand.

## Who reads it
People trying to solve a cleaning problem. Some buy for a plant or facility. Others run a boatyard, restaurant, brewery, school, shop, service company, or small business. They should not need chemical, legal, or purchasing expertise to understand the site.

## The voice
**A knowledgeable salesperson explaining a better cleaner to a real customer.**

Lead with what the product does and why the customer should care. Explain technical details only when they help someone choose, use, or buy. Use everyday words, natural sentences, and direct calls to action.

## Answer the customer's questions
What does it clean? Why is it better? Is it right for my surface or system? What does it cost? How do I try or buy it? If a detail does not help answer one of those questions, move it out of the main sales story.

## Do
- Start with the mess, surface, equipment, or result the customer recognizes.
- Lead with the benefit, then show the result: "Cleared 20-year rust in 30 minutes where CLR ran 36 hours and quit."
- Explain unfamiliar terms the first time they matter.
- Use short paragraphs, active verbs, and specific examples.
- Sound confident and human. A little personality is welcome.

## Avoid
- Internal labels such as "exact-product record," "document control," "route," "scope," "workflow," "candidate," or "owner-approved" in customer copy.
- Lawyerly openings, long disclaimers, and long lists of conditions before the customer learns the benefit.
- Chemistry jargon without a plain explanation.
- Robotic fragments, repetitive three-part slogans, and corporate filler.
- Generic hype. Specific results sell better.

## Worked example
Before: "Match VertKleen to the soil, surface, process, and finish target, then verify the exact-product record before a controlled comparison."

After: **Tell us what you need to clean. We’ll help you choose the right VertKleen product and try it on one real job.**

## Calls to action
Use natural customer language: "Find my cleaner," "Try it on my job," "Get a quote," "Shop products," and "Talk to our team."

## Language patterns customers already know
Strong industrial-cleaning sites make the buying decision easy to scan. They name the mess, surface, equipment, cleaning method, and result before adding technical detail.

- Start with a concrete action: cut through, lift away, break down, dissolve, rinse clean, flush out, or bring back.
- Pair the cleaning result with the business result: fewer passes, a cleaner rinse, restored flow, easier training, or equipment back in service.
- Use familiar section labels: "What it removes," "Where it works best," "How to use it," "Available sizes," and "SDS & guides."
- Make the next step obvious: shop a small pack, try a sample, compare formulas, check compatibility, or ask for bulk pricing.
- Put exact certifications, ratings, test results, and registrations beside the product they support. Never turn one product's proof into a family-wide slogan.

## Original VertKleen phrase bank
Use these as flexible building blocks, not a slogan pasted onto every page. Match each line to the actual product and job.

### Headlines
- Cut through buildup. Keep the job moving.
- Built for hard-working equipment and the crews who clean it.
- Bring back flow, heat transfer, and a clean finish.
- Heavy soil calls for the right cleaner, not more guesswork.
- From everyday grime to the buildup that stops equipment.

### Product-page language
- Made for jobs like these.
- Why crews choose it.
- Choose the strength that fits the mess.
- Cleans hard-to-reach areas and rinses away cleanly.
- Small packs are ready online. Drums and totes are priced for the job.

### Industry language
- Tell us what is dirty, what it is made of, and how you clean it now.
- Put VertKleen to work on one real cleaning job.
- Start with one surface, one system, or one piece of equipment.
- Match the cleaner to the mess, surface, and way your crew works.

### Results and proof
- See the job, the method, and the result.
- Real cleaning jobs. Clear before-and-after results.
- Compare the whole job: product, labor, water, repeat passes, downtime, and finish.
