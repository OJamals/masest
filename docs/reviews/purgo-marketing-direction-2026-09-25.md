# Purgo: product marketing direction

Lead with clean surfaces, odor control, and the buying power of a concentrate. Show customers where Purgo fits their operation and what a gallon delivers. Let the product photography, application guidance, and supporting documents reinforce that story.

Distributor research now sharpens this direction: show recognizable surface-cleaning jobs, give fleet interiors their own place, explain concentrate value, and make samples and bulk buying easy. See the [source-to-page integration notes](../research/purgo-distributor-page-integration-2026-09-25.md). The supported surface-care positioning, use list, and buying prompts have been applied to the local catalog and generated Purgo page.

## Lead with the product

**Purgo**

### Professional surface cleaning. Concentrated value.

Clean facility surfaces and control odor-causing bacteria with Purgo. This versatile concentrate supports everyday surface care and water-system maintenance, with application-specific dilutions that put every gallon to work.

**Shop Purgo** · **Find your dilution**

Use the actual package customers receive. Give the facility product a surface-cleaning setting. Give Marine Antimicrobial its marine packaging and setting. The name, image, and application should tell the same story at a glance.

## Show customers where it works

### Facility surfaces

**Keep shared spaces clean and fresh.**

Bring Purgo into your surface-cleaning routine for commercial facilities, fitness spaces, and fleet interiors. Control odors on compatible surfaces with clear directions for mixing, application, and contact time.

### Drains & water systems

**Put odor control into your maintenance routine.**

Use Purgo for drain and water-system care, with dedicated dosing for odor and fouling control. Give maintenance teams a straightforward application guide they can use on the job.

### Fleet interiors

**Keep the cabin ready for the next shift.**

Put Purgo to work on hard interior surfaces in buses, service vehicles, and commercial fleets. Make surface cleaning and odor control part of the regular turnaround routine.

Present these sections on the page. Buyers should see Purgo's range immediately and reach the right directions in one click.

### Choose your size. Plan your supply.

Order for daily use or talk to MASEST about larger packs and recurring supply. Keep the package selector, sample request, and bulk quote close to the application story.

## Make application methods a selling point

### Apply it your way

**Spray and wipe. Spray and leave. Fogging applications.**

Purgo supports multiple application methods across surface-care programs. Choose the method and dilution for your task, then follow the application guide.

Use concise method cards with practical photography. Add a clear **Get application guidance** action for commercial fogging projects. EMS already lists fogging; bring that capability into the sales story. Add equipment-specific ULV and electrostatic guidance when those protocols are available.

## Put useful proof within reach

### Product information you can put to work

Give buyers direct access to the label, technical data, safety information, and laboratory reports. Each card should explain what the document helps them decide: how to mix, how to apply, how to handle, or what was tested.

Feature the Canadian milestone with a clear date:

> **Health Canada disinfectant registration — 2021**
>
> Explore EMS's Canadian registration announcement for Purgo.
>
> **Read the announcement**

Keep the card focused on the dated disinfectant-registration milestone and the original announcement.

Use **HMIS 0–0–0** as a clean, legible technical proof point once the applicable SDS is matched to the sold product. Label the three ratings plainly: Health, Flammability, Physical Hazard. The supplied January 2020 EMS SDS supports that rating; the current website SDS needs correction.

## Keep the performance story strong and specific

Lead with surface cleaning, control of odor-causing bacteria, versatile application methods, and concentrate economics. These give customers concrete reasons to buy.

Keep the persistence report available in the evidence library. A four-day protection headline needs a study that demonstrates continuing protection under the claimed use conditions. The reviewed report has method and result inconsistencies, so it cannot carry a 96-hour protection promise or an invented protective-barrier explanation. Purgo N's residual-performance patents concern a separate formulation.

Use straightforward handling and application language. Skip sweeping promises about eliminating workplace injury, requiring no containment, or unrestricted spraying around occupants. Specific product strengths make a more credible sales case.
