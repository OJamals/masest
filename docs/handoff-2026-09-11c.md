### The totals never needed the catalog

`#cartEstimate` was treated in the last handoff as un-reservable because "it needs prices".
It needs prices for one **value**, not for its **shape**. There are always three rows; the
Shipping cell always reads "Calculated next" on first render, because a rate only exists
once the buyer asks for one; the Tax cell is a constant; and the item count is the sum of
the quantities already in `masest_cart`. Only the subtotal figure waits, so only that cell
is a bar — and `tests/cart-page.test.mjs` already pinned that no review verdict may appear
before the catalog answers, which makes a bar the only honest filler as well as the only
available one.

Measured **0px delta** at five viewports × four cart shapes, and separately **0px against
production's own CSS, fonts and prices** by swapping the reserve's exact string into the
live hydrated page (`cart-estimate-prod-parity.mjs`). That second check is the one worth
repeating for any future reserve: the local tree agrees with itself by construction.

### The ZIP form is replayed, never guessed

It is offered only when every line is a priced, buyable variant, and storage holds
quantities, not prices. `updateSubtotal()` records that verdict in
`masest_cart_estimable_v1` against the **exact sku set** it was reached for; the pre-paint
reserve replays it. A set that does not match, a verdict of `false`, or no record at all
reserves nothing and leaves the single reveal in place — so the mechanism can only remove
shift, never add it. The cost is the first cart view in a browser.

### "Pending review" was a 21.7px shift of its own

When a line is not a priced buyable variant the subtotal cell reads "Pending review". At
the subtotal's own 20px it wrapped to two lines at 393–412px, which the reserve cannot
predict — whether a cart is priced is precisely what it does not know. It now renders at
the small scale inside the dd's own `line-height: 1` 20px strut, so the priced and unpriced
states measure identically. It also reads better: a status word in the money slot at money
size was claiming an emphasis it had not earned.

## Traps this session paid for

**A runtime-injected `<style>` beats every linked stylesheet.** `js/account-nav.js` appends
a `<style>` to `<head>` carrying `.nav-cart { width:42px; height:42px }`. An injected sheet
lands after every `<link>`, so it wins. `css/style.css` already carried
`.nav-cart { min-width: 44px }` **twice** and the button still rendered 42px — one of those
copies was *also* cancelled by `min-width: 0` in `css/navigation.css`, which loads after
style.css at equal specificity. **Ask the browser which rule wins before editing one**
(CDP `CSS.getMatchedStylesForNode`; `.qa-local/frontdoor/why-nav-cart.mjs` does it). A
string assertion against a stylesheet would have passed the entire time, which is why the
new gate in `tools/site-audit-regressions.spec.mjs` measures rendered boxes and resolves
`::after` overlays.

**The local cart CLS number was always about half the field number.** Against the local
static tree `/api/products` 404s, so no line is ever priced, the ZIP form never un-hides,
and its reveal is simply absent from the measurement: 0.0609 local against 0.1271 field.
`tools/homepage-vitals.spec.mjs` now has a second cart test that fulfils a priced catalog.
Fulfilling is right in that one place — prices are the variable under test, not a network
characteristic — while the image test above it still continues rather than fulfils, because
there the bytes are the whole point.

**Playwright samples share one browser context.** The "cold" samples of that new test have
to clear `masest_cart_estimable_v1` themselves, or samples two and three inherit sample
one's verdict and measure the warm path three times. Two of three passing on a regressed
cold path would still have been green.

**Both cart gates were confirmed to fail with their commits reverted**, and so was the tap
target gate. Do that check; it is cheap and it is the only thing that distinguishes a gate
from a comment.

**A `<label>` that merely points at a control elsewhere is not a tap target** — the control
is. A label that *wraps* its control is. A probe that does not separate the two reports a
dozen 372×20 field labels as failures and buries the four real ones.

**A control inside a closed `<details>` has no box to measure.** "Talk with our team" looked
fixed in the first probe because it was unmeasurable. Open every disclosure before reading
target sizes.

## The label claim, closed — and my count of it was wrong

Raised for four sessions as a contradiction between `vertkleen-lam3-label-front.pdf` and
the SDS. **The owner confirmed on 2026-09-11 that "SAFE ON SKIN AND EYES" reflects an EPA
designation meaning no permanent damage or injury to eyes or skin — mild irritation only,**
and chose to note it and move on. No artwork change, no code change. It is not an open
item and should not be raised again.

That designation checks out against the documents. CR, HCR, LAM3 and SAR all classify
**Skin Corrosion/Irritation Category 3** and **Serious Eye Damage/Eye Irritation Category
2B** — mild and reversible. The claim was never false about severity.

**Two things I had been repeating that are wrong, corrected by extracting the PDFs
directly rather than trusting `docs/label-claim-review-2026-09-10.md`:**

- It is **10 human-contact claims across 6 documents, 1 printed label front** — not "15
  occurrences, 7 documents, 2 printed labels". The inflated figure came from counting
  `dist/` duplicates and the handoff notes describing the issue.
- Most "Safe on …" hits in the library are **material-compatibility** claims — metals,
  glass, chrome, paint, grass, equipment — and were never part of this. Only these are
  human-contact: `vertkleen-lam3-label-front.pdf` p2, `vertkleen-lam3-tds.pdf` p2,
  `vertkleen-cr-tds.pdf` p2, `vertkleen-hcr-tds.pdf` p2 (x2), `vertkleen-sar-tds.pdf` p2
  (x2), `watersafe60-cr-nsf60-user-guide.pdf` p2-3 (x3).

Worth recording for whoever next opens these files:

- The same four SDS carry signal word **Warning** and a first-aid line to rinse with
  copious water, and `vertkleen-lam3-label-front.pdf` carries **no** signal word, no
  first aid and no "rinse" wording at all. If the artwork is ever reissued, the drafted
  alternative was to state the consequence rather than the conclusion — "NO BURNS — NO
  PERMANENT SKIN OR EYE DAMAGE", with "rinse on contact" in the fine print — which keeps
  the real differentiator against the hydrochloric and sulfuric descalers these replace.
- `• Non-Toxic` appears as a headline bullet in six TDS and is the same class of absolute
  claim, already corrected sitewide on 2026-09-10 but still in the PDFs. This is **not**
  the same as `(rat oral: NON-TOXIC`, which appears in twelve and is a cited LD50 result;
  that one is a test outcome and is fine as written.
- `tests/health-claim-guard.test.mjs` skips `docs/` entirely, which is why the PDFs were
  never in its scope while the HTML was cleaned.
