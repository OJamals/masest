# Marine, HVAC, and surface-cleaning market research

Research date: 2026-08-03

Status: **internal strategy, evidence, photography, and copy brief — not public copy**

## Executive decision

VertKleen should enter these markets as **job-matched cleaning chemistry with a
documented work method**, not as another generic “green cleaner.” The commercial
promise is:

> Identify the soil or deposit, verify the substrate and OEM limits, run a
> controlled clean, capture the waste stream, and document the result.

Recommended order:

1. **Marine HVAC and water-side mechanical contractors** — lead with seawater
   condenser and heat-exchange cleaning, temporary circulation plans, rinse
   verification, and return-to-service records. This crossover has the clearest
   maintenance trigger, professional repeat use, downtime value, and fit with
   existing VertKleen HVAC routes.
2. **Florida marinas and boatyards** — expand into contained vessel washing,
   marine-film and grease removal, aluminum test patches, and washwater planning.
3. **Commercial facility HVAC owners** — sell contractor-supported maintenance
   methods tied to inspection, flow, pressure drop, approach temperature, and
   downtime.
4. **Broad facility surface cleaning** — use as an application and cross-sell
   layer, not the primary industry identity. “Surface cleaning” is too broad to be
   a useful market position by itself.

Do not launch a generalized air-duct-cleaning, indoor-air-health, disinfectant,
“safe to discharge,” universally coil-safe, or universal marine-surface claim.

## Recommended industry taxonomy

Use two dimensions throughout navigation, CRM, SEO, and trial intake:

1. **Industry/owner context** — who owns the asset and what rules govern the job.
2. **Cleaning job** — soil/deposit × substrate/equipment × method × verification.

Do not create “Surface Cleaning” as a peer industry. It is a job family that cuts
across marine, HVAC, manufacturing, education, healthcare, hospitality, fleet,
and facilities.

## Regulatory, standards, and claim boundaries

### Marine water and waste

- EPA identifies boat cleaning, hull maintenance, fueling, and stormwater as
  potential marina pollution sources. Its marina guidance calls for minimizing
  release of cleaners, solvents, and antifouling paint to surface water
  ([EPA marinas overview](https://www.epa.gov/nps/nonpoint-source-marinas-and-boating),
  [EPA marina management measure](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=20004KEK.TXT)).
- Florida DEP’s current Clean Marina program covers marinas, boatyards, and
  marine retailers. Its boatyard BMPs include recirculating pressure-wash
  systems and wastewater recycling ([Florida Clean Marina Program](https://floridadep.gov/rcp/clean-marina/content/clean-marina-program)).
- Florida’s boatyard guidance says pressure-wash water should be contained and
  routed to recycling or an authorized treatment path, not surface water,
  storm drains, groundwater, or septic systems absent applicable authorization
  ([Florida Clean Marina BMPs](https://floridadep.gov/sites/default/files/2020_Florida_Clean_Marina_Best_Management_Practices.pdf)).
- EPA finalized national VIDA performance standards in October 2024, but those
  standards do not become effective until corresponding U.S. Coast Guard
  regulations are final, effective, and enforceable. Applicable 2013 VGP and
  state/local interim requirements therefore remain in place; do not advertise
  “VIDA certified” or imply the future rule is already the operating regime
  ([EPA status](https://www.epa.gov/vessels-marinas-and-ports/2013-vessel-general-permit-and-interim-requirements)).
- The future federal standard at 40 CFR Part 139 defines exact tests for
  “minimally-toxic, phosphate-free, and biodegradable,” requires deck-wash
  discharges to be minimized in port, and requires qualifying cleaners for
  covered deck, graywater, and vessel-surface discharges. The part excludes
  recreational vessels and is not yet effective pending the Coast Guard rule
  ([40 CFR Part 139](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-139)).

**Marketing boundary:** “Biodegradable,” “phosphate-free,” or a low HMIS score
does not mean a product can be discharged. Say “build the washwater route into
the job plan,” never “safe for the water” or “safe to discharge,” unless an exact
product, exact use, and applicable jurisdictional approval support it.

### Worker safety and shipyard scope

- OSHA’s Hazard Communication Standard requires hazard classification,
  workplace labeling, SDS access, and employee information/training for covered
  hazardous chemicals ([OSHA Hazard Communication](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200)).
- Shipboard tank cleaning can involve flammable, toxic, and oxygen-deficient
  atmospheres; OSHA describes confined-space controls and marine-chemist
  certification before hot work ([OSHA shipbuilding tank cleaning](https://www.osha.gov/ship-building-repair/tank-cleaning)).

**Scope boundary:** do not market ordinary VertKleen vessel cleaning as tank
entry, gas-freeing, or confined-space cleaning. Those are separate specialist
services with vessel-, cargo-, and operation-specific controls.

### HVAC maintenance and IAQ

- ASHRAE Standard 180 establishes minimum commercial HVAC inspection and
  maintenance requirements intended to preserve comfort, efficiency, and IAQ
  capability ([ASHRAE Standards 180 and 211](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/bookstore/standards-180-and-211)).
- NADCA’s current ACR 2025 standard covers HVAC assessment, cleaning,
  restoration, engineering controls, component inspection, source removal, wet
  cleaning, and cleanliness verification ([NADCA 2025 release](https://nadca.com/press-releases/nadca-releases-2025-edition-acr-nadca-standard-assessment-cleaning-and-restoration)).
- EPA says evidence does not support sweeping health benefits or routine duct
  cleaning. It distinguishes possible efficiency gains from cleaning dirty
  coils/fans/heat exchangers from weak evidence for cleaning ducts alone
  ([EPA duct-cleaning guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/should-you-have-air-ducts-your-home-cleaned)).
- DOE describes planned O&M as a cost-effective route to equipment longevity,
  reliability, safety, and efficiency ([DOE O&M](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/improve-operations-maintenance)).
- EPA treats HVAC&R as a unique antimicrobial use site. A disinfectant or
  sanitizer registered for ordinary hard surfaces is not authorized for HVAC&R
  unless the label specifically includes that use, application method, dose,
  target organism, PPE, and re-entry controls
  ([EPA HVAC&R antimicrobial guidance](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-labels/use-disinfectants-and-sanitizers-heating-ventilation-air-conditioning-and)).

**Marketing boundary:** sell inspection-triggered component cleaning and
measured equipment outcomes. Do not promise healthier air, allergy relief,
energy savings, or routine duct-cleaning need without job-specific evidence.

### Cooling towers and Legionella

CDC says an effective water-management program is the primary control strategy
for Legionella. Scale, corrosion, sediment control, cleaning, and a managed
disinfectant program are components of that system
([CDC cooling-tower module](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/cooling-towers-module.html)).

**Marketing boundary:** cleaning chemistry supports a water-management program;
it does not replace it. Never claim that an unregistered cleaner “controls,”
“kills,” or “prevents” Legionella.

### Cleaning versus disinfection

- CDC separates cleaning, sanitizing, and disinfecting. It recommends cleaning
  visibly dirty surfaces with an appropriate cleaner and using an EPA-registered
  disinfectant when disinfection is required
  ([CDC facility cleaning](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/when-and-how-to-clean-and-disinfect-a-facility.html)).
- EPA treats direct or implied pest-mitigation claims in labels, websites,
  advertising, testimonials, and sales activity as pesticidal claims subject to
  FIFRA unless an exemption applies
  ([EPA cleaning-product FIFRA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/determining-if-cleaning-product-pesticide-under-fifra)).

**Marketing boundary:** “removes dirt, grease, film, mineral scale, or organic
residue” is cleaning language. “Kills mold/bacteria,” “sanitizes,” “disinfects,”
or disease/health imagery requires exact label authority.

### Environmental marketing

FTC guidance rejects broad unqualified “green” or “eco-friendly” claims. An
unqualified “non-toxic” claim requires competent and reliable scientific
evidence that the product is safe for people and the environment; degradability
claims require disposal-context support
([FTC Green Guides summary](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/environmental-claims-summary-green-guides)).

EPA Safer Choice evaluates exact products and ingredients against its current
standard. The label cannot be inferred from a chemistry family or supplier
([EPA Safer Choice Standard](https://www.epa.gov/saferchoice/standard)).

**Marketing boundary:** publish a certification only when the exact VertKleen
trade name, product, formulation, manufacturing location, and authorization are
current in the certifier’s live registry.

### OEM compatibility is the decisive gate

Official OEM instructions conflict across equipment and models:

- Carrier’s residential guidance allows specialized coil cleaner or mild
  detergent with low pressure and warns against highly acidic cleaners and
  pressure washers ([Carrier coil-cleaning guidance](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/residential/hvac-resources/air-conditioners/ac-coil-cleaner-tips/)).
- A current Carrier commercial manual prohibits chemical cleaners on certain
  microchannel coils and warns that acids, bases, bleach, and high-pressure jets
  can accelerate corrosion or bend fins ([Carrier 30XB manual](https://brandportal.carrier.com/m/48b4e6ef1d06b8bd/original/10224_IOM_10_2024_30XB_250_1700_XBE_XBP_250A_1700A.pdf)).
- Daikin says water is the safest method for its aluminum indoor coil and warns
  that corrosive cleaners can cause premature failure; approved cleaners are
  model-controlled ([Daikin air-handler instructions](https://dwt-front-prod.daikincomfort.com/docs/default-source/dmvt-%28r-32%29/im-iod-4040b.pdf)).
- A current Dometic chilled-water manual says excessive acid exposure can reduce
  condenser life and specifically prohibits muriatic acid, bleach, and bromine
  for the covered condenser-cleaning procedure
  ([Dometic chilled-water manual](https://www.dometic.com/externalassets/dometic-mtcg_9108893793_75371.pdf?ref=2034421891)).
- Yamaha tells owners to rinse salt with fresh water, use mild cleaner and low
  pressure, and avoid spraying sensitive intake areas
  ([Yamaha corrosion-prevention guidance](https://yamahaoutboards.com/maintenance-matters/corrosion-prevention)).

**Commercial consequence:** MASEST needs an OEM/model compatibility intake and
approval workflow. “Safe for coils,” “safe for aluminum,” and “marine safe” are
too broad.

### Defensible VertKleen position

Competitors own convenience (“spray and go”), breadth (“all surfaces”), or
specialized product grids. VertKleen can own **controlled trial + documented
job completion**:

- fewer products selected by job;
- exact document pack before work;
- substrate/OEM check before application;
- concentration, dwell, agitation, rinse, and recovery recorded;
- outcome inspected or measured;
- completed-task cost, not drum price, used for comparison.

This position fits the existing site’s “prove it on your job” model and avoids
claiming evidence the current catalog does not have.

**CTA:** Match a cleaner to the job

### Message pillars

1. **Job fit before product pitch** — soil/deposit and substrate/equipment decide
   the method.
2. **Proof before scale** — begin with a controlled test area or isolated loop.
3. **Method before claim** — show concentration, dwell, agitation, rinse,
   recovery, and verification.
4. **Documents at point of decision** — label, SDS, TDS, compatibility and
   disposal information stay with the exact SKU.
5. **Completed-task economics** — compare chemistry used, labor, water,
   downtime, rework, and waste, not gallon price alone.

### Approved language pattern

Use:

- “designed for [named job] under the current VertKleen directions”;
- “test on an inconspicuous or representative area first”;
- “select concentration and contact time for the deposit and system”;
- “confirm material compatibility and OEM requirements”;
- “capture and route washwater through the approved site process”;
- “compare the inspected or measured result after the clean.”

Avoid unless exact current evidence authorizes it:

- safe, non-toxic, zero-hazard, no-PPE, eco-friendly, marine-safe;
- biodegradable without test method, result, medium, time, and disposal context;
- safe to discharge, drain-safe, direct release;
- safe for all metals, all coils, all gelcoat, all boats, or all surfaces;
- non-corrosive or prevents corrosion;
- disinfects, sanitizes, kills mold/bacteria/Legionella, purifies air;
- EPA approved, EPA certified, NSF approved, Safer Choice certified;
- no-rinse, food-safe, occupied-space safe;
- restores efficiency, saves energy, extends equipment life, prevents failure,
  or reduces labor by a percentage without a complete exact-job record.

## Procurement package

Every B2B campaign should offer one downloadable exact-SKU evidence pack:

- current label, SDS, and approved TDS;
- product/revision/lot identity and emergency contact;
- allowed applications, restrictions, dilution, temperature, contact time,
  agitation, rinse, storage, shelf life, and disposal language;
- exact substrate/metallurgy/elastomer evidence and prohibited materials;
- VOC, phosphorus, biodegradability, aquatic-toxicity, or corrosion data only
  where the complete test supports the exact claim;
- current certifier listing and mark-use authorization, when held;
- pack sizes, finished-use volume, lead time, minimum order, freight/hazmat
  status, and distributor/service availability;
- field method, training record, spill response, and waste-routing checklist;
- case record with asset, soil/deposit, starting condition, exact SKU/lot,
  method, measurements, result, limitations, and approval.

### Required lead-intake fields

**Marine:** vessel/facility; work location; in-water vs hauled; surface/coating;
soil; prior products; wash method; pad/recovery; drain/sewer/recycler route;
owner/OEM limits; trial area; acceptance endpoint.

**HVAC:** equipment manufacturer/model; air-side vs water-side; coil/exchanger
construction; metallurgy/elastomers; deposit or fault; system volume; baseline
flow/pressure/temperature/amperage; OEM cleaner limits; isolation/LOTO;
circulation rig; waste route; rinse/restart endpoint; water-treatment provider.

**Surface:** surface/finish; soil; occupied status; ventilation; drainage;
current process; dilution/dwell/agitation; rinse; slip/residue/reopening endpoint.

## Measurement

Track by segment and job:

- qualified account → method review;
- method review → sample/trial;
- trial scheduled → trial completed;
- trial completed → accepted result;
- accepted result → first PO;
- first PO → 60/90-day reorder;
- chemistry, labor, water, downtime, rework, and waste per completed job;
- page entrance → selector/document/intake action;
- organic/paid query → qualified application, not traffic alone;
- product return, callback, compatibility failure, spill/waste exception, or claim
  complaint.

Primary success measure: **accepted, repeatable completed jobs per segment**, not
impressions, rankings, samples shipped, or gallons sold alone.
