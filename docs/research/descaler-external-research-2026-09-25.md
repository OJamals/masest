Lead with **scale removal for coils, pumps, plumbing, and heat-transfer equipment**. Sell the practical combination: strong mineral cleaning, a straightforward rinse, HMIS 0-0-0, zero VOCs, and non-regulated transport. Use the documented fire-pump maintenance result and AC-coil restoration narrative. Suggested copy direction:

> Clear stubborn scale. Keep equipment working. VertKleen Descaler breaks down calcium, lime, and mineral buildup in coils, pumps, plumbing, and heat-transfer equipment. Choose a label dilution for the buildup, circulate or soak as the application requires, then rinse clean.

## Product facts suitable for the page

The supplied Descaler TDS identifies calcium, lime, scale, milk stone, and beer stone removal, including heat exchangers, heating coils, cooling towers, and boilers. It lists zero VOCs, no phosphates, nonflammability, full water solubility, and HMIS 0-0-0. The SDS corroborates HMIS 0-0-0 and non-regulated hazardous-material transport for DOT, Canadian TDG, IMO, and IATA. Rail is marked not evaluated.

The label specifically names copper, aluminum, 316 stainless steel, and brass. Its caution limits copper/aluminum safety to dilution. Use **“Copper and aluminum compatible at label dilution”**, with coatings, fins, seals, hoses, and complete system materials checked when planning the job. Do not broaden this into neat-product compatibility with every metal or every coil coating.

Practical benefits: remove hard mineral deposits, expose cleaner heat-transfer surfaces, support restored flow where scale is restricting components, simplify recurring descaling supply, and avoid hazardous-material shipping classification for the supplied product. These are stronger than a generic “green cleaner” description.

### Handling and disposal source reconciliation

The SDS says normal ventilation is generally adequate and does not require routine eye/skin/body/respiratory protection, while allowing task-specific protection. It also states mild skin and eye irritation warnings and advises avoiding inhalation of mist. For the fire-system field job, the source specifies nitrile gloves, eye protection, and closed-toe shoes.

The SDS expressly prohibits entry into storm drains and directs surplus/non-recyclable solutions to a licensed disposal company. Its product-specific instructions do not support unlimited release into ground or waterways. Keep public operational wording short: **“Rinse clean and handle used solution through the site's approved collection and disposal process.”** The product's biodegradability does not characterize the contaminants removed from equipment.

The supplied TDS contains broader biodegradability and sanitary-disposal language. Treat the practical SDS instructions as the controlling workflow when reconciling these documents; this disagreement belongs in internal review, not in a legalistic sales-page paragraph.

## Label dilution and application guidance

The rendered HVAC Descaler label states:

| Buildup | Printed dilution | Printed method |
|---|---|---|
| Light buildup / regular | 20:1 | Circulate through coil, then rinse |
| Moderate cleaning | 3:1 | Circulate, then rinse |
| Severe buildup / scale | 1:1 | Circulate, then rinse with water |

The label does **not** define ratio order or a universal circulation duration. Preserve the printed ratios. Do not invent water-first wording, RTU yield/cost arithmetic, or a contact time.

Recommended job workflow:

1. Identify equipment, deposit, materials, coating, and system volume.
2. Isolate the equipment or component and plan collection of the used solution.
3. Select label dilution and job-specific circulation or soak conditions.
4. Monitor cleaning, drain, and rinse with fresh water.
5. Inspect and verify the equipment before return to service.

### Source guide problems: exclude these numbers from the page

The two-column equipment guide prints a heat-exchanger volume band as `500-200 gallon`, between `0-50` and `200-500`. Do not silently correct the source by publishing an assumed band. Cooling-tower instructions give five gallons per ten tons for “descale” and three gallons per ton for “light descale,” reversing the expected relative dose. These need source clarification.

The same guide gives Descaler or HCR solution strengths of 10% for light and 25% for medium heat-exchanger descaling, plus different boiler/HCR and evaporative-pad instructions. These are application-specific and should not overwrite the product's label table. The separate water-treatment guide mixes HCR and Descaler sections, online dosing, potable-water-style maximum rates, and high-concentration offline cleaning. Do not turn that into a universal Descaler dosing calculator.

## Comparison data: what can and cannot be used

### Current BlowOut / supplied Descaler TDS

Both visually inspected sheets show HCl 8.9 and Descaler/BlowOut 8.1, followed by urea HCl 7.2, urea sulfuric 6.1, phosphoric 0.9, citric 0.0, lactic 0.2, acetic 0.1, glycolic 0.2, oxalic 0.0, and malic 0.4. Conditions: 200 grams of 5%-active solution, one calcium-oxide cube, three minutes at 70 °F. The table does not state the measurement unit. Do not invent percent dissolved, grams, or a rate unit.

The headline prose claims approximately 15% more calcium dissolution than muriatic acid, which conflicts with its table. The July 26 internal research dossier also flips HCl/BlowOut in one earlier paragraph, while its later findings give the visually correct order. This review uses the actual rendered table. A numerical superiority banner is unsuitable until the source discrepancy is resolved.

### Corrosion chart

The local S026 marketing sheet gives Descaler 0.59 mpy and HCl 609.5 mpy; its headline says 280× less corrosion. That ratio is approximately 1,033, not 280. The same sheet uses a dissolution value of 9.8 and calls the substrate calcium carbonate, unlike the supplied TDS. Its footnote says carbon steel and mils per year, but omits the alloy, concentration, temperature, and duration.

The current EMS site embeds a SynTech-branded chart with the same 0.59/609.5 mpy pair, without underlying conditions. That establishes manufacturer provenance for the graphic, not a complete Descaler corrosion test. The prior HCR research recovered a distinct 0.59 **mmpy** result on SAE 1020 carbon steel; mpy and mmpy must not be interchanged, nor should an HCR result be silently assigned to Descaler.

**Recommendation:** flag the S026 chart for source correction; use a qualitative product comparison on this page. No defensible Descaler-versus-HCl corrosion pair with stated material, concentration, temperature, and exposure duration was located.

### Qualitative comparison supported by primary sources

The [CDC/NIOSH hydrogen-chloride entry](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0332.html) identifies hydrochloric acid as highly corrosive to most metals, documents solution skin/eye burns, and gives DOT 1789 for solution. That supports a practical contrast with Descaler's label-compatible diluted use on copper/aluminum and its SDS non-regulated transport profile. Keep the comparator specifically conventional hydrochloric acid; do not extrapolate to every branded inhibited descaler or every concentration.

Suggested rows: metal compatibility at specified dilution; shipping classification; routine handling; water rinse. Avoid numerical corrosion multipliers, guaranteed freight prices, zero worker risk, or superiority over CLR/Calci-Solve without their exact current specifications and matching tests.
