# Purgo: historical Canadian approval remains valid evidence

Reviewed September 25, 2026. Source supplied by the user: [EMS Health Canada press release](/Users/omar/Desktop/masest/EMS+Purgo+Health+Canada+Press+Release.pdf), dated January 12, 2021. Its substantive text matches the [release currently hosted by EMS](https://www.enviromfg.com/s/EMS-Purgo-Health-Canada-Press-Release.pdf).

The document still stands as a dated announcement of historical Canadian disinfectant approval. Later pre-market cancellation does not erase that history or demonstrate a failed safety or efficacy assessment. The earlier report's phrase “outdated Canadian approval” was too broad; it has been clarified.

## What the supplied release establishes

The headline and opening announce Health Canada registration of Purgo All Purpose Antimicrobial Cleaner as a disinfectant. A later distributor quotation describes plans to offer white-label cleaners, wipes, and hand sanitizers. That future product-offering statement does not identify a hand-sanitizer authorization, approved skin-use label, or authorization number. The release therefore supports historical disinfectant approval; this document alone does not establish approval as a hand sanitizer. [EMS release](https://www.enviromfg.com/s/EMS-Purgo-Health-Canada-Press-Release.pdf).

## Voluntary non-marketing and the public record

The user reports that a Canadian expert explained that the company voluntarily chose not to market the product. Record that explanation as user-supplied expert information. It is consistent with Health Canada's status; the public entry does not disclose the particular company's commercial motivation.

Health Canada defines Cancelled Pre Market as cancellation before a product was ever marketed in Canada. It lists Cancelled (Safety Issue) separately. Consequently, the recorded status is not evidence that Purgo was rejected or withdrawn for safety or efficacy reasons. It also does not mean the DIN remains active. [Health Canada terminology](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/drug-product-database/terminology.html).

Health Canada's DIN guidance gives the matching administrative mechanism: section 6.5 describes a manufacturer's discontinuation notice for an approved product that was never marketed, resulting in Cancelled (Pre-Market). Section 6.6.1 separately addresses safety-related cancellation. This supports the plausibility of the expert's explanation without independently proving Purgo's specific motive. [Health Canada DIN guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/regulatory-requirements-drug-identification-numbers/document.html).

## Recommended treatment in the document library

Retain the press release with its original date and a short context note:

> EMS announced Canadian registration of Purgo as a disinfectant in January 2021. The identified Canadian DINs were subsequently cancelled before marketing. According to the owner's Canadian expert, the company voluntarily chose not to commercialize the products in Canada.

For public use, retain the expert attribution unless written confirmation supports stating the specific business decision without attribution. Do not relabel this release as proof of hand-sanitizer approval. The remaining evidence for that separate assertion is the exact hand product's DIN or NPN and authorized label. Preserve the distinction between historical approval and current authorization when describing the document.
