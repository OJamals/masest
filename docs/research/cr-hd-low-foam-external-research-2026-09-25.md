# VertKleen CR HD Low Foam: external research and evidence review

Research date: September 25, 2026. Product: VertKleen CR HD Low Foam (`cr-hd-low-foam`). Scope: current EMS literature, supplied product documents, official registration records, distributor positioning, and application-image provenance. Research only; no publication.

## Product identity and strongest positioning

The exact EMS counterpart is **SynClean LF**, the low-foaming industrial cleaner/degreaser. EMS distinguishes it from high-foaming SynClean HD and neutral-pH SynClean N. Its current product page specifically recommends LF for **wash cabinets and parts cleaners**. That is the strongest lead for this page: industrial grease removal where machine agitation makes excess foam undesirable. [EMS product catalog](https://www.enviromfg.com/products1)

## LF-specific application and technical data

The supplied LF sheet, pages 1–2, supports these points:

- Low-foam operation in carpet and rotary cleaners; use in steam cleaners and processes requiring little foam. Warm or cold water is supported.
- Removal of petroleum oils, hydraulic-fluid deposits, animal and vegetable fats, dirt, and grime.
- No butyls or solvents. Manufacturer describes grease lifting without emulsification, keeping removed grease recoverable from traps or containment. Good copy: **“Lift grease from parts and surfaces. Keep recovered oil easier to separate.”** Do not promise zero separator maintenance or a measured reduction in service frequency.
- Formula includes detergents, wetting and sequestering agents, penetrants, and rust/corrosion inhibitors. The sheet describes it as non-corrosive and suitable for vehicle finishes. It supplies no alloy-by-alloy matrix, pH value, gasket compatibility table, machine operating temperature, cycle length, or dwell time.
- Zero VOCs, no phosphates, nonflammable, fully water-soluble. A triple-zero hazard diamond appears on page 1. Avoid calling that pictured diamond an HMIS label; the current catalog's HMIS claim is a separate existing product datum.
- One-year shelf life and 30°F cold stability. Do not replace this with distributor claims of three years.
- DOT, TDG, IMO, IATA and IMDG non-regulated classification is stated. Non-hazardous transport is a sound benefit; freight prices still depend on pack, weight, destination and carrier.
- The manufacturer summarizes non-toxicity, non-mutagenicity and non-skin-irritant findings and calls the product 100% biodegradable. These are manufacturer study summaries, not attached full laboratory reports. No numerical LC50, test concentration, report identifier or investigator appears. Do not invent those details or equate biodegradability with permission to release recovered oil or dirty wash water.

Source: supplied LF PDF above; independently verified against the currently linked official PDF.

## Dilution: two source-backed starting points

| Job in LF sheet | Published ratio |
|---|---|
| Caked hydraulic fluid or grease | 10:1 |
| Carpets, vehicles and floors | 30:1 |

The sheet does not explicitly define ratio order. Preserve its notation; do not calculate ready-to-use yield or label it water-to-product without a current LF label. Page 2 directs the reader to the product label for dilution specifications. No exact LF-specific label or SDS was located in the supplied collection or current EMS links during this review. Generic CRHD label/SDS files exist, but product family membership does not establish that they are LF instructions.

Keep the HD page's 5:1 heavy-deposit, 10:1 hood/milk, and 100:1 glass table separate. The LF sheet contains only the two examples above. Similarly, no fixed wash-cabinet concentration or heated cycle can be inferred from the generic 10:1 example. Ask for machine model, tank volume, soil, finish and operating temperature when sizing a machine-cleaning program.

## Certifications and food-processing use

The LF TDS itself gives conflicting NSF category lists: page 1 lists A1/A4/A5/A8/C1/K1; page 2 lists A1/A2/A3/A4/A5/A8/C1/C2/D1. Neither supplies a registration number. Its FDA/USDA statements identify no finished-product authorization document. Keep this inconsistency in the research record, not as customer-facing legal prose.

The [official NSF EMS listing](https://info.nsf.org/USDA/Listings.asp?Company=C0146843), current September 25, 2026, returns two products: Elevate 148037 (A1) and Renovo HD 148038 (A1/K2). No SynClean LF or VertKleen CR HD Low Foam listing appears under those names. The Renovo HD record cannot silently become an LF badge. It also does not establish no-rinse food-contact use for LF.

The TDS says the formula and ingredients meet or exceed Safer Choice requirements. That wording differs from a current finished-product certification. The [EPA product-search page](https://www.epa.gov/saferchoice/products) links the [official dataset](https://data.epa.gov/efservice/t_safer_choice_and_design_for_the_environment/JSON). Its 4,993 records were fetched and searched across product/company names for `SynClean`, `Environmental Manufacturing`, and `VertKleen`; zero matches. Do not create a current LF certification badge from the manufacturer’s standards statement. Food-facility copy can focus on grease removal and the facility's cleaning/rinsing program without claiming sanitization or no-rinse approval.

## Renovation recommendations

Lead with **“Heavy grease removal. Low foam for machine cleaning.”** Show two practical application groups: wash cabinets/parts washers and rotary/carpet/floor cleaning. Surface the two LF dilution examples, solvent/butyl-free chemistry, zero VOCs and transport profile. Link the exact official LF TDS, and request a current LF SDS/label through the product-specific inquiry.

Use a factual HD/LF/neutral choice: HD for visible foam on manual jobs; LF for low-foam machine applications; neutral when a neutral-pH cleaning program is required. Avoid guarantees of foam-free operation, pump performance, universal material safety, cost savings, or measured productivity gains without job data.

Flag the existing representative machine-wash scene for photographic replacement rather than treating a synthetic setup as product evidence. Preferred replacement: documented LF jug plus identifiable parts washer or floor-cleaning machine, credible plumbing/recovery arrangement, readable setup information, and actual LF trial provenance. Keep authentic packaging separate from application proof.
