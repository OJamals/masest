# HVAC HCR: product research and page direction

Reviewed September 25, 2026. Product: VertKleen HVAC HCR, `hcr-t16`, SKU stem `VK-HCR-T16`. Research only; no publication or deployment.

## Positioning

Lead with **calcium, scale, and rust removal for HVAC equipment**, supported by the actual Brevard County surface-restoration result. Give isolated water-side cleaning its own workflow. Small packs are real offers, so bulk supply should be a purchasing option rather than the product's defining benefit. The catalog seed has active industrial `HCR-1G` and `HCR-25G`, plus quoted `HCR-55D` and `HCR-275T`; it also carries marine Scale Buster aliases. Neither the SKU stem nor these records establishes a special concentrate strength or a sixteen-drum requirement.

The owner identifies MASEST as a reseller of the same EMS chemistry. Within that scope, the supplied HCR TDS matches the historical EMS SapH HCR sheet. Current EMS BlowOut and Foro sheets remain separate formulations. Their applications can help organize the page, but their numerical comparisons, approvals, and use rates should not be relabeled as HCR data.

## Best result: Brevard County HVAC restoration

The supplied case report describes a 20-year-old stainless-steel diamond plate and drain area at a Brevard County School District HVAC installation. CLR had been tried for 36 hours without clearing the rust. DDC Engineering then selected VertKleen HCR. The team sprayed the rusty surfaces, allowed 30 minutes, and rinsed with a garden hose; the report says no scrubbing was needed. DDC Engineering and Ivey Construction reported that using HCR first would have saved time and money.

Use this as a clearly identified **surface-restoration case**. It does not measure heat-transfer recovery, energy savings, metal loss, or in-loop descaling. It is not a controlled 72× performance comparison. The report's final recommendation for periodic tower injection names CR and Descaler and includes broader prospective benefits; keep those recommendations separate from the observed HCR result.

Public existing first-party account: [Brevard HVAC rust case study](https://masest.co/blog/hcr-brevard-hvac-rust-case-study). This is MASEST's publication of the same result, not independent corroboration.

## Directions: preserve application boundaries

### HVAC package-label ratios

| Label condition | Printed dilution | Printed contact time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light soil / mild odor | 10:1 | 10–15 minutes, then rinse |
| Moderate soil / odor | 5:1 | Dwell, then rinse; no duration specified |
| Severe soil / odor | 2:1 | 20 minutes, then rinse |

The label does not define which side of the ratio is water. Publish only with the source's exact convention, or omit ratio economics until confirmed. Do not invent finished gallons or price per diluted gallon. These label rows are surface-application directions, not a universal closed-loop recipe.

### Offline systems

The pipe/offline guide instructs isolation/disconnection, draining, filling at selected concentration, at least 30 minutes of contact, then draining and fresh-water flushing until pH returns to normal before reconnecting. It recommends no less than 15% strength for effective cleaning and elsewhere expresses dose as 175–500 gallons product per 1,000 gallons water. These expressions require an explicit mixture-volume basis before conversion.

The equipment guide gives 10% for light heat-exchanger descaling and 25% for medium scale, circulation with pH checks every half hour, and separate boiler methods. The printed volume/time table contains “500-200 gallon”; the cooling-tower text alternates five gallons per ten tons and three gallons per ton for light scale. These are editorial errors or ambiguities. Do not publish a consolidated recipe from this table.

Recommended page workflow: identify the deposit and materials; isolate/drain the circuit; choose concentration/contact time for system volume and scale load; circulate and monitor; drain, flush, and check pH before return to service. Request a cleaning plan for the actual system. This is actionable without turning inconsistent source tables into universal directions.

## Technical marketing and unresolved source details

- **Use confidently:** SynTech acid replacement; calcium/scale/rust removal; non-fuming; HMIS 0-0-0; no VOCs; biodegradable; non-regulated DOT/TDG transport. Describe simpler handling and shipping without inventing a guaranteed freight saving amount or claiming elimination of all worker risk.
- **HCR benchmark:** supplied TDS reports a one-inch calcium-carbonate cube, eight hours at 100°F: neat HCR 100% dissolved; 50% HCR 97%; 33% HCR 54%; 15% HCl 87%; 7.5% HCl 46%. Preserve concentrations and conditions if used. This is not the three-minute calcium-oxide test in the separate Descaler/Foro sheets.
- **Materials:** package label lists 316 stainless, PVC, copper, brass and gaskets, while its caution says aluminum-safe at dilution. HCR TDS and recovered EMS sheet exclude aluminum piping/fittings. Do not promise blanket aluminum compatibility. Ask for the system's metal, coating, seal, hose and fitting details.
- **Certification:** the HVAC label's NSF60 and FDA-GRAS badges carry a footer stating certification listings await EMS verification. Do not elevate this footer-qualified badge into a newly verified NSF HCR listing. The supplied equipment guide directs potable systems to WaterSafe60.
- **Corrosion:** earlier research recovered 0.59 **mmpy** on SAE 1020 carbon steel; no underlying 280× HCl control protocol was found. Use the actual value only with attribution and units; do not create a 280× guarantee.
- **Shelf life:** HCR supplied TDS says one year, two years and more than one year in different places. Omit a new shelf-life promise pending corrected sheet.
- **Source copy:** phrase “no potential for generation of Carbon Dioxide” tied to NIOSH 7903 in supplied material is not suitable as an explanatory chemistry statement for carbonate dissolution. Do not reproduce it.

## Suggested page structure

1. HVAC identity and job-led hero: remove scale and rust from HVAC equipment, with clear small-pack and bulk purchasing.
2. Brevard before/after result with 30-minute contact, water rinse, no scrubbing, and named field context.
3. Two applications: exposed HVAC surfaces and isolated water-side systems.
4. Label-led surface directions; separate offline workflow.
5. SynTech technical profile and product-specific HCR comparison if space warrants.
6. Guidance for selecting HCR, general Descaler, or WaterSafe60; no crossed dosing tables.
7. Technical inquiry requesting equipment type, materials, system volume, buildup and current method.

Use real documented job imagery. Synthetic representative loop art must not be presented as field evidence; flag it for replacement with an actual isolated-loop maintenance photograph.
