Research date: 2026-07-26

Scope: online primary and first-party sources for EMS, pHlexaPeel, MASEST/VertKleen, related patents, certifications, regulatory records, and relevant chemistry.

Purpose: support scientifically sharper product and site copy without turning supplier claims into unsupported facts.

## Executive findings

1. The supplied image is already in MASEST's document room: [pHlex Guide 1](https://www.masest.co/docs/sds/watersafe60-cr-nsf60-user-guide.pdf), version 1.4, revised 2026-02-04. It is an EMS Distribution flyer, not an independent study.
2. The defensible core mechanism is conventional acid chemistry delivered through a proprietary formulation. Hydrogen ions react with carbonate scale; patent literature proposes complexation, buffering, and inhibitors that moderate free chloride, acid spending, and corrosion. Do not say that hydrogen “bonds to H+.”
3. Public patents describe acid-containing formulations. “Acid-free” should therefore be treated as supplier category or replacement language, not a literal molecular-composition claim, unless the current SKU formula is documented.
4. The strongest comparative story is a **lower-hazard profile for specified jobs**, not “zero hazard.” Current VertKleen and pHlex labels warn of mild skin and eye irritation.
5. WaterSafe60 and pHlex CR have current NSF/ANSI/CAN 60 listings, with exact functions and maximum-use levels. That certification is not blanket potable-water approval or proof of cleaning efficacy.
6. Current EPA Safer Choice data returned no product or company match for EMS, SynTech, SynClean, pHlexaPeel, MASEST, VertKleen, BlowOut, Hydro+, or WaterSafe60. Historical EPA stewardship recognition and ingredient-criteria claims are not product certification.
7. pHlexaPeel says its GRAS position is its own certification/opinion. Do not call this “FDA approved” or “FDA certified.”
8. MASEST's controlled document room already uses a strong evidence model: source class, technical approval, legal approval, revision, and restrictions. New prose should preserve that discipline.
9. The current MASEST terms page says VertKleen, SynTech, and SynClean are MASEST trademarks. Public trademark records reviewed identify other registrants for SynTech and SynClean. Legal should correct or qualify that sentence before the technology pages ship.

## What the science supports

### 1. Carbonate-scale removal

The general reaction is:

`2 H+ + CaCO3 → Ca2+ + CO2 + H2O`

EPA's Class VI well guidance gives this net reaction for acid and calcium carbonate. USGS also demonstrates carbon dioxide evolution when hydrochloric acid contacts carbonate minerals.

This supports copy such as:

> Available hydrogen ions react with carbonate scale, converting the deposit into soluble calcium species, water, and carbon dioxide.

Sources:

- [EPA, Underground Injection Control Program Class VI Well Construction Guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa816r11020.pdf), EPA 816-R-11-020, May 2012.
- [USGS, “The Acid Test”](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/acid-test), undated.

It does **not** support the flyer explanation that hydrogen in SynTech has a stronger bond to the “H+ ion.” That statement is chemically incoherent.

### 2. Proposed formulation mechanism

Relevant patent literature offers two different formulation narratives:

- [US9056815B2, “Synthetic acid and associated methods”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9056815B2/en), issued 2015-06-16. It proposes a glycine/zwitterion–hydrochloric-acid complex, buffering, reduced free chloride, and strong-acid-like pH.
- [US7938912B1, “Composition for removing cementitious material from a surface and associated methods”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7938912B1/en), issued 2011-05-10. Its disclosed embodiments include hydrochloric acid, urea, a keto-amine hydrochloride complex, alcohol, ethoxylate, and ketone.
- [US8580047B1, “Composition and method for treating subterranean formations”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8580047B1/en), issued 2013-11-12. A claim specifies a formulation containing hydrochloric acid and urea with inhibitors and solvents.

Safe interpretation:

> Patent literature proposes acid-complexing, buffering, and corrosion-inhibiting systems that preserve useful hydrogen-ion activity while moderating free chloride and acid spending.

Required qualification:

- A patent proves a disclosed invention and legal claim scope, not the composition of a current SKU.
- It does not independently prove safety, efficacy, certification, or superiority.
- No public source reviewed ties a specific current VertKleen lot to one exact patent embodiment.
- “Acid replacement” is defensible positioning. “Contains no acid” is not established by these patent records.

### 3. Proposed high-pH mechanism

[EP3527652A1/B1, “Synthetic base and associated methods”](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3527652A1/en), published 2019-08-21 and granted 2022-08-10, describes embodiments formed by mixing glycine with calcium hydroxide and using sodium percarbonate. It discusses cleaning, peeling, pH adjustment, and replacement of conventional sodium hydroxide.

Safe interpretation:

> Related patent literature describes buffered high-pH systems based on glycine and alkaline components for cleaning and pH adjustment.

Do not infer that current pHlex CR, Elevate, or VertKleen CR uses that exact formula. Obtain a current technical statement or composition attestation first.

### 4. Encapsulation claim for degreasing

[SynClean HD TDS](https://www.enviromfg.com/s/syncleanhdwso-tds.pdf), ©2022, says solvent/butyl cleaners emulsify grease while SynClean encapsulates and lifts individual hydrocarbon molecules.

This is a supplier mechanism claim. No independent microscopy, particle-size data, analytical method, or comparative grease-trap study was found. Use only as:

> The supplier describes the cleaning action as lifting and dispersing hydrocarbon soils rather than relying on butyl solvent.

Avoid “encapsulates every molecule” until validated.

## What the evidence shows

### Supplier bench tests

#### BlowOut

[EMS BlowOut TDS](https://www.enviromfg.com/s/blowout-tds.pdf), ©2022:

- Test condition reported: 200 g of 5% active solution, one calcium oxide cube, three minutes, 70 °F.
- Table values: muriatic acid 8.1; BlowOut 8.9.
- The numerical increase from 8.1 to 8.9 is about 9.9%, not “nearly 15%.”
- The TDS alternates between “calcium” and calcium oxide. Neither is calcium carbonate scale.
- No laboratory identity, replicates, uncertainty, raw data, or complete method is supplied.

Use:

> In an EMS three-minute bench comparison at 5% active and 70 °F, BlowOut produced a higher reported result than muriatic acid.

Do not turn this into a universal “15% more scale” claim.

- Explicitly connects HCR to EMS SynTech.
- MASEST's controlled cover says technical/legal claim approval is not asserted.

### MASEST laboratory and field evidence

#### Carib Brewery

[Carib Brewery laboratory report](https://www.masest.co/docs/carib-brewery-lab-report.pdf), report dated 2023-12-15; controlled cover effective 2026-07-24:

#### Brewlando CIP

[Brewlando brewery CIP trial](https://www.masest.co/docs/brewery-cip-trial-brewlando.pdf), controlled cover effective 2026-07-24:

- Uses the same reported concentration and cycle conditions as the prior caustic/acid process.
- CR: 5 L per 55 gal, 160–170 °F, 25 minutes.
- HCR: 5 L per 55 gal, 30 minutes.
- Outcome is primarily visual.
- No replicate, control assignment, residue assay, ATP result, microbiological endpoint, or statistical analysis appears.

Call this a field trial or operating observation, not a scientific proof of superiority.

#### WaterSafe60 titration

[WaterSafe60 titration test](https://www.masest.co/docs/sds/watersafe60-titration-test.pdf), test dated 2024-10-28:

- Starts from one water sample at pH 8.0 and EC 3.700.
- Estimated doses per 1,000 gallons: 14.08 oz to pH 7.0; 19.20 oz to 6.5; 28.16 oz to 6.0; 38.40 oz to 5.5.

Present this as one source-water example. Dose demand varies with alkalinity, buffering, temperature, and target pH.

## Safety and conventional-product comparison

Official NIOSH Pocket Guide entries document the well-known hazards of conventional chemicals:

- [Hydrogen chloride](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0332.html): 5 ppm ceiling; severe irritation/burn potential; corrosive to most metals.
- [Sodium hydroxide](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0565.html): 2 mg/m³ ceiling; severe corrosive effects; heat generated on water contact.
- [Sulfuric acid](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0577.html): corrosive exposure hazards.

Current VertKleen evidence still requires caution:

- [VertKleen Descaler SDS](https://masest.co/docs/sds/vertkleen-descaler-sds.pdf), source SDS version 1.1 revised 2025-01-27: acute oral category 5, skin irritation category 3, eye irritation category 2B, signal word “Warning,” pH 0.0–0.5.
- [VertKleen HCR label](https://masest.co/docs/sds/vertkleen-hcr-label.pdf), controlled revision 1.0 effective 2026-07-24: warns of mild skin and eye irritation; provides dilution and compatibility limits.
- [WaterSafe60 label](https://www.phlexapeel.com/s/pHlex-WaterSafe60-Label.pdf), undated: warns of possible mild skin and eye irritation.
- [pHlex CR label](https://www.phlexapeel.com/s/pHlex-CR-Label-1.pdf), undated: includes warning and irritation language.
- [EMS Hydro+ TDS](https://www.enviromfg.com/s/hydro-tds.pdf), ©2022: calls the product a mild skin irritant and warns against aluminum piping/fittings.

Defensible comparison:

> Designed to provide acid- or caustic-like process utility with a lower handling and transport hazard profile than conventional hydrochloric acid or sodium hydroxide in specified applications.

Required qualifiers: current SDS, dilution, substrate/metallurgy, contact time, temperature, local risk assessment, and task-specific PPE.

Avoid:

- “Safe on skin and eyes”
- “No PPE”
- “Zero hazard”
- “Non-corrosive on all materials”
- “Safe for people, pets, and the environment”

## Recommended site architecture

### `/technology` — “How the chemistry works”

Sequence:

1. The problem: carbonate scale, rust/oxide, organic soil, or pH control.
2. The governing chemistry: hydrogen-ion or hydroxide activity.
3. The proposed formulation behavior: complexation, buffering, controlled spending, inhibition.
4. The measured result: one named test with all conditions.
5. The safety/compatibility boundary: current SDS, metallurgy, dilution, temperature, PPE.
6. The proof link: source, date, revision, and evidence class.

Suggested top copy:

> Conventional acids remove carbonate scale because hydrogen ions react with the deposit. SynTech-based products are designed to deliver that useful acidity through a proprietary, inhibited formulation intended to moderate free chloride, fuming, and corrosion behavior. Performance still depends on the deposit, concentration, temperature, contact time, and substrate—so every claim links to its test conditions and current safety document.

### Product page pattern

Use six compact modules:

1. **Job to be done** — exact soil/deposit and system.
2. **How it works** — mechanism, clearly labeled supplier/patent theory where applicable.
3. **What testing showed** — numeric result plus substrate, concentration, time, temperature, comparator.
4. **Why operators choose it** — handling, shipping, downtime, equipment, wastewater; only substantiated benefits.
5. **Limits and compatibility** — prohibited materials, dilution, rinse, disposal, PPE.
6. **Evidence** — SDS, TDS, label, certification, bench test, field trial, revision date.

### `/proof` and `/resources`

Retain MASEST's current controlled-evidence logic:

- Evidence class: certification, registered label, SDS, TDS, controlled bench test, third-party laboratory test, field trial, testimonial.
- Source owner.
- Test date and document revision.
- Technical approval status.
- Legal/claims approval status.
- Product/SKU and lot where known.
- Exact claim the document supports.
- Explicit non-supported inferences.

### Comparison pages

Compare process outcomes, not vague “green” adjectives:

| Dimension | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Cleaning/descaling | Mass loss, surface area, deposit identity, concentration, time, temperature |
| Corrosion/material compatibility | Alloy, coupon method, exposure time, temperature, corrosion rate |
| Worker hazard | Current SDS classifications and task exposure assessment |
| Transport | Current classification and packaging |
| Wastewater | Used-bath pH, dissolved metals, soil load, local discharge limits |
| Process economics | Product dose, labor, cycle time, rinse water, downtime, waste handling |

Do not publish uncited competitor hazard or efficacy claims. The current [VertKleen CR/HD comparison](https://www.masest.co/docs/sds/vertkleen-crhd-degreaser-comparison.pdf) should remain restricted until every competitor statement is sourced and current.

4. **CR:** the TDS says pH 13 and no conventional hydroxides, classifies the product as “Practically Non-Skin Irritant,” suggests safety glasses, and says to avoid aluminum piping and fittings. A high pH still requires application-specific handling and compatibility guidance.
5. **CRHD / Neutral:** the most defensible mechanism is wetting, detergent action, sequestration, and lifting of hydrophobic soils. Publish “non-emulsifying” or grease-separation benefits only after a named test shows the downstream oil/water behavior.
6. **MultiWash / Purgo:** old local material combines cleaner, deodorizer, and antimicrobial language. Current EPA records apply to exact registered products and labels; do not transfer those claims to MultiWash or another Purgo variant by name association.
