# CIP HCR: manufacturer, distributor, and application research

Reviewed September 25, 2026. Product: VertKleen CIP HCR, VK-HCR. Research only; no publication or deployment.

## Page direction

Lead with brewery mineral cleaning: remove beer stone, scale, and rust from tanks, kegs, lines, and heat exchangers. Show the CR organic-cleaning stage followed by the HCR mineral-cleaning stage. Give brewers the actual CIP label doses, a documented brewery example, and a clear path to sizing the complete cycle.

The most useful newly recovered primary source is an EMS SapH HCR technical sheet preserved in an official USPTO filing. It independently establishes the origin of the calcium-carbonate benchmark in MASEST's supplied HCR TDS. It is historical manufacturer literature, not a newly issued 2026 sheet.

### Calcium-carbonate performance

The supplied HCR TDS and historical EMS HCR sheet agree on this test: one-inch calcium-carbonate cube, eight hours, 100°F; endpoint is percentage of the cube dissolved.

| Test liquid | Cube dissolved |
|---|---:|
| HCR concentrate | 100% |
| HCR at 50% | 97% |
| HCR at 33% | 54% |
| HCl at 15% | 87% |
| HCl at 7.5% | 46% |

The HCR and HCl percentages identify different test solutions. Keep them visible. This is a material-dissolution benchmark, not a claim that a brewery CIP completes in eight hours or that every dose outperforms every acid blend. The full source also includes HCl/acetic mixtures and acetic-only controls; a smaller page table should be labeled selected results.

### Corrosion comparison

Recovered EMS HCR evidence supports **0.59 mmpy** on SAE 1020 carbon steel. “mpy” is a different unit and must not replace it. The recovered sheet does not show an HCl control or a 280× calculation. Searches of the supplied HCR TDS, label, related local guides, and official web results did not identify the underlying 280× comparison protocol. MASEST's existing web/blog statement is not independent corroboration. Prefer the directly supported carbonate test and brewery observations on this renovation; keep the 280× claim out of new prominent comparisons until its exact source is available.

### Application and materials

The CIP label supplies 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 liters per 10 gallons for light, moderate, and heavy cleaning respectively. Preserve its quantities directly rather than combining them with unrelated online dilution tables. The label does not itself specify a universal heating temperature or contact time.

The older general HCR label recommends 316 stainless steel, polypropylene, or schedule 80 PVC and explicitly excludes aluminum fittings. The HCR TDS likewise excludes aluminum piping/fittings. Compatibility copy should name supported materials and have customers confirm their seals, fittings, and complete circuit; avoid implying every gasket or metal is interchangeable. A broad silicone-gasket promise was not established by the primary sources inspected here.

The two similarly named HCR/Descaler guides have different application formats. One includes heat-exchanger percentage solutions and boiler/cooling-tower steps; the other provides isolated-pipe/offline guidance. Their rates are for those specific applications. Do not substitute industrial offline descaling strengths for the brewery CIP label.

## Brewery evidence

**Brewlando:** the supplied trial states a mash tank had not received a caustic/acid CIP for around ten days after IPA production. CR was mixed at 5 liters into 55 gallons of water at 160–170°F and circulated for 25 minutes. After CR exposed mineral deposits, HCR was mixed at 5 liters into 55 gallons at 160–170°F and circulated/jet-washed for 30 minutes. Present this as a documented two-stage trial, preserving both products' contributions. The report records personnel PPE and valve/leak checks before mixing. It is not a universal prescribed cycle for every brewery.

**Carib Brewery:** the lab comparison reports HCR sample concentration 7%, conventional acid sample 15%, and pH 1.0 versus below 1. It found the HCR sample more effective in that application and recommended an industrial trial. The rust-removal portion included mechanical cleaning; report that contribution when discussing the cleaned plate. Do not turn the concentration comparison into a guaranteed 53% reduction in chemical cost: the source does not provide equivalent formulation costs, delivered prices, or identical active concentrations. Link the public Carib PDF as specific brewery evidence.

## Patents and certification scope

The current EMS technology page supports describing the underlying SynTech platform as patented. Existing repo research, `docs/research/ems-patent-records-2026-09-25.md`, traces US7938912B1 to John MacDonald/Green Products and an official declaration establishing EMS's exclusive-license relationship at the time. That patent concerns cementitious-material removal. No product-specific CIP HCR patent-number crosswalk was established here. Do not use a patent number as brewery efficacy proof, infer a proprietary formula, or describe a regulatory approval from patent issuance.

Manufacturer source badges should preserve their meaning: a technology statement, a product's shipping classification, and a finished-product certification are distinct evidence. The inspected exact HCR material supports the HMIS/non-fuming/non-regulated transport story. A product-specific current EPA Safer Choice certification for VK-HCR was not established in this research. The supplied SDS's material and disposal instructions remain the practical application reference; biodegradability is not used to promise unrestricted discharge of contaminated cleaning liquid.
