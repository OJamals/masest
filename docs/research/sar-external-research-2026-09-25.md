# VertKleen SAR: external research and page direction

Research date: 2026-09-25. Scope: SAR product identity, current EMS documents, related patent evidence, and distributor messaging. MASEST is a direct reseller of the EMS chemistry and has authorized use of manufacturer materials. No deployment, manufacturer contact, or form submissions performed.

## Core finding

**Sell SAR as a sulfuric acid replacement for pH control.** The supplied SAR TDS explicitly prioritizes pH adjustment over scale and paraffin removal. Its strongest product-specific points are titration equivalence to 40% sulfuric acid, near-zero heat release when diluted with water, non-fuming operation, HMIS 0-0-0, and non-DOT shipping. The current rust-and-scale framing obscures the actual buying reason.

## Product identity confirmed independently

An EMS September 20, 2016 product-name email, included in an official USPTO proceeding, explicitly identifies **SapH SAR as sulfuric acid replacement**. The same record distinguishes HCR, CR, and WaterSaph60. It is useful historical identity evidence; it is not a new SAR TDS. [USPTO filing, PDF pages 15 and 18](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-91256388-OPP-39.pdf).

MASEST's own [company listing](https://www.linkedin.com/company/masest) identifies VertKleen SAR as a 40% sulfuric acid replacement. The supplied SAR TDS is the stronger source because it states what the comparison means: titration equivalence.

Avoid unrelated search collisions: SAR can mean sodium adsorption ratio, spent acid regeneration, or synthetic aperture radar. Also do not merge EMS SAR with unrelated products carrying SynTech or Enviro-Syn names.

## Claims to carry into the page

The following are supported by the supplied SAR documents:

- **Purpose:** lower pH in process liquids; water treatment and food processing appear on the label, and oilfield pH control appears in the TDS.
- **Strength:** titrates equally to 40% sulfuric acid. Present this as the stated titration comparison, not a 40% ingredient concentration or universal gallon-for-gallon dosing instruction.
- **Dilution behavior:** near-zero exothermic response when mixed with water. Keep the water-dilution condition beside the claim; do not turn it into a promise of zero heat in every neutralization reaction.
- **Handling and logistics:** HMIS 0-0-0, non-fuming, non-flammable, non-DOT-regulated, Canadian TDG non-regulated.
- **Composition profile:** zero VOCs, no phosphates, fully water soluble, 100% biodegradable.
- **Dosing:** add SAR to the liquid until the desired pH is reached. No fixed dilution ratio is supplied.
- **Pumping hardware:** the label recommends 316 stainless steel, polypropylene, or schedule 80 PVC and expressly prohibits aluminum fittings.

No public titration curve, test endpoint, test temperature, or dilution calorimetry protocol was located. A concise specification card is justified; a fabricated performance graph or RTU yield calculator is not.

The TDS contains a one-year/two-year shelf-life inconsistency and lists a very mild skin-irritation classification on page 2. The label lists mild skin and eye irritation. Avoid a shelf-life number and universal no-irritation promise. Keep the useful pumping-material instructions visible rather than hiding them in a PDF.

## Distributor research: solid ideas to adapt

**EMS Russia — sell the operating task.** Its [pH-reduction article](https://ems-rus.com/articles/kak-snizit-uroven-ph-vody/) leads with the plant's need to lower water pH, then explains direct addition or integration into a system and dosing to the job. Adapt that customer journey: application → target pH → dosing setup → sample or bulk discussion. Its subject is Hydro+, so do not transfer its 15% dissolution comparison or other numerical claims to SAR.

**EMS Cleaning Europe — compare operating costs, not bottle price alone.** Its [acid-versus-SynTech article](https://www.emscleaning.eu/en/acido-vs-syntech-coste-real-limpieza-industrial/) emphasizes transport, handling, maintenance, and equipment wear. For SAR, use the exact non-DOT and non-fuming specifications, then offer a process-cost comparison. No guaranteed freight percentage, injury elimination, or universal total-cost saving was established.

**Heartland Energy Group — make the application instructions concrete.** Its [Phix I product page](https://heartlandenergygroup.net/product/phix-i-oil/) clearly explains pH adjustment, dilution heat, pumping fittings, and bulk supply. This is a useful information structure. Phix I explicitly claims equivalence to **50%** sulfuric acid, so its numbers and formula are not SAR evidence. Keep SAR's own 40% comparison and label materials.

FutureSafe's EMS page appeared in search results but returned HTTP 404 on live retrieval. No new claim was adopted from that stale result.

## Patent and certification scope

The published [US8853446B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8853446B2/en) specification describes a synthetic-acid family and includes process/wastewater pH adjustment among its uses. The later [US11034598B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11034598B2/en), granted June 15, 2021, claims specified glycine/water/hydrogen-chloride compositions and methods. These are published patent texts accessed through Google Patents; the [official application reference](https://patentcenter.uspto.gov/applications/16915204) is provided for docket verification.

No public record inspected connected a particular patent number to the marketed SAR formula. Use EMS's documented technology story without displaying an unverified patent number as SAR's formula proof. Patent disclosures do not establish SAR's 40% performance test, shipping classification, or a product certification.

No SAR-specific EPA Safer Choice product listing or NSF 60 entry was established in this review. The SAR TDS says it is made from CleanGredients and contains classification/approval language; that is not an identified product-listing record. The historical EMS naming record assigns NSF 60 context to WaterSaph60, a separate product. Preserve the exact SAR technical advantages instead of borrowing a neighboring product's badge.

## Recommended page copy and structure

**Positioning:** “Lower process pH with a sulfuric acid replacement.”

**Lead:** “VertKleen SAR delivers the titration strength of 40% sulfuric acid for water treatment and industrial pH control, with a non-fuming formula and near-zero heat release when diluted with water.”

**Prominent specifications:** “40% sulfuric-acid titration equivalent” · “HMIS 0-0-0” · “Non-DOT shipping” · “Zero VOCs.”

**Application section:** identify starting and target pH; evaluate a representative process sample; add to the liquid and monitor the endpoint; choose the specified compatible fittings. The sample-evaluation step is a practical recommendation, not a new manufacturer dosing protocol.

**Primary CTA:** “Plan my pH-control changeover.”

**Sample CTA:** “Test SAR in my process.”

**Cross-sell:** route heavy mineral-scale removal toward Descaler/HCR and potable-water-specific requirements toward WaterSafe60. Let SAR own the sulfuric-to-pH-control decision.

**Visuals:** show dosing equipment, process-water tanks, or a clearly labeled representative treatment installation. Do not imply an unobserved pH reading, customer result, or rust-removal demonstration.
