# Industrial-cleaning website copy benchmark

Research date: 2026-08-22

Status: internal marketing and copy reference; no production copy changed by this brief

## Purpose and method

This review looks at how established cleaning-product manufacturers explain difficult cleaning jobs to ordinary buyers. It covers industrial degreasers, descalers, safer-chemistry positioning, odor and organic-buildup treatment, HVAC and water-side maintenance, and marine cleaning.

Only official manufacturer and brand pages were used. Competitor claims are recorded as examples of positioning, not independently verified facts and not evidence for a VertKleen claim. Quoted fragments are intentionally short. Every suggested VertKleen line below is newly written rather than copied from a competitor.

## Executive readout

The strongest sites do six things consistently:

1. **Start with a recognizable mess or maintenance problem.** Grease, scale, rust, salt film, bilge oil, odor, low flow, and dirty equipment appear before chemistry details.
2. **Use active cleaning verbs.** “Cuts through,” “breaks down,” “lifts,” “dissolves,” “rinses clean,” and “restores” make the product feel easy to understand.
3. **Name the job and surface.** Strong copy says which engines, floors, heat exchangers, hulls, metals, drains, or food-processing surfaces the product is made for.
4. **Turn concentration into value.** Yield, dilution, pack sizes, fewer passes, and cost per use are easier to buy than a vague claim of “economical performance.”
5. **Put reassurance beside the benefit.** Low foam, easy rinse, no residue, no solvent fumes, material fit, short directions, labels, and SDS links answer practical concerns without a wall of legal text.
6. **Match the CTA to buyer readiness.** Ready buyers see “Add to cart” or “Where to buy.” Uncertain buyers see product matching or samples. Complex and bulk buyers see an expert, distributor, or quote.

MASEST already has a strong category-native direction in **“Start with the mess. Find the cleaner.”** Keep that. The next improvement is not a new voice; it is applying that voice more consistently to product result lines, product cards, industry groupings, proof, bundles, and bulk buying.

## Twelve-site benchmark

### 1. Simple Green Industrial

[Simple Green Pro HD](https://simplegreen.com/industrial/products/pro-hd-cleaner-degreaser/) leads with professional strength, named soils, familiar equipment, and flexible dilution. Its useful short phrases include “cuts through tough grease” and “clean rinsing formula.” The page moves from four quick benefits to product features, directions by cleaning strength, SDS, sizes, ingredients, and buying options.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Keep the first screen concrete: what comes off, what it cleans, how buyers can size the strength, and where to buy. “Heavy,” “medium,” and “light” job guidance is easier to scan than a long technical method.

### 2. Zep Professional

[Zep Industrial Purple](https://zep.com/products/zep-industrial-purple-cleaner-and-degreaser-1-gallon-r45810) combines a result-heavy title with price, reviews, trust cues, a cart action, and short benefit blocks. It makes concentrate value tangible by stating how much ready-to-use cleaner one gallon makes. It also uses plain operational language such as low foam, easy rinse, faster cleanup, and lower cost per use.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Put price or quote path, pack size, yield, and primary result together. Product names do not need to carry the entire sales pitch; a short outcome line directly below can do that work.

### 3. CLR PRO

[CLR PRO Calcium, Lime and Rust Remover](https://www.clrbrands.com/proline/products/clr-pro/clr-pro-calcium-lime-and-rust-remover/) opens with the deposit and outcome, then offers tabs for overview, ingredients, and instructions. It provides pack sizes, surfaces, dilution by soil level, SDS and technical sheets, a distributor CTA, and a closing “Ready to go pro?” prompt. Its [professional product finder](https://www.clrbrands.com/product-category/proline/) uses the customer-friendly idea of matching a product to the mess.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Descaler pages should say “scale, rust, flow, and heat transfer” before acid chemistry. A surface list, simple job-strength guide, and prominent document downloads build confidence without slowing the sales story.

### 4. Krud Kutter / Rust-Oleum

[Krud Kutter Original Cleaner & Degreaser](https://www.rustoleum.com/product-catalog/consumer-brands/krud-kutter/cleaners-and-stain-removers/original-krud-kutter?lc=&ls=KK05) uses a memorable hard-job/gentler-formula contrast, followed by a highly concrete list of removable messes. Directions are divided into heavy, general, and light cleaning. Formula exclusions, surface notes, certification badges, and “Find a Retailer” sit near the buying story.

**Lesson for MASEST:** A short contrast can sell the idea quickly, but the next line must name actual soils and surfaces. Use specific reassurance—such as no solvent fumes or no residue where supported—instead of broad “safe” language.

### 5. Ecolab

[Ecolab Scientific Clean degreasers](https://www.ecolab.com/pages/ecolab-home-depot-products/degreasers-home-depot) use energetic, memorable retail language, including “clean like you mean business,” then return quickly to grease, buildup, stains, speed, aluminum fit, and professional applications. [Ecolab Exelerate](https://www.ecolab.com/offerings/exelerate-cleaning-and-sanitizing) sells food and CIP cleaning through productivity, time savings, and specific burnt-on soils rather than abstract chemistry.

**Lesson for MASEST:** One distinctive brand line can create energy, but the product explanation should immediately answer what it removes, where it works, and how it saves labor or time.

### 6. Buckeye International

[Buckeye Industrial](https://www.buckeyeinternational.com/products/industrial) organizes products by job and equipment: vehicle cleaning, metal cleaning, high-pressure systems, resin removal, scrubbers, and aerospace. Each card combines a functional product label, one-paragraph fit, SDS, and product literature. The category also offers industry programs and a clear distributor path.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Functional subtitles help unfamiliar names. “Foam-free high-pressure degreaser” or “concentrated metal cleaner” tells buyers more than another adjective. Pair every SKU with a one-line job descriptor.

### 7. Bio-Circle

[Bio-Circle CB 100](https://www.bio-circle.com/products/cleaner/industrial-cleaner/cold-cleaner-alternative/cb-100/) has the deepest product-page structure in the sample: product/SKU, overview, pack sizes, “Request product consulting,” full advantages, application examples, a structured soil/material/method table, videos, data sheets, reviews, and practical FAQs. Its language frames the product as an alternative to an older process, then explains why daily work becomes easier.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Give serious buyers a fast path and a deep path on the same page. The hero should stay simple; soil, material, equipment, application method, pack, documents, and FAQs can follow below.

### 8. Goodway Technologies

Goodway’s first-party [ScaleBreak-MP sheet](https://www.goodway.com/sites/default/files/documents/2022-03/scalebreak_mp_cutsheet_fnl0919.pdf) and [boiler-cleaning guide](https://www.goodway.com/sites/default/files/downloads/boiler_cleaning_buying_guide_fnl_0619.pdf) sell a system, not only a jug. The copy identifies water-side equipment, explains what scale does, quantifies dissolving capacity, lists compatible materials, connects cleaning to equipment life and efficiency, and tells readers to speak with a salesperson for selection help.

**Lesson for MASEST:** HVAC and water-treatment buyers respond to system outcomes: restore flow, improve heat transfer, reduce shutdown time, protect equipment, and know when the clean is finished.

### 9. Nu-Calgon

[Nu-Calgon Eco-Lyme](https://nucalgon.com/product-list/eco-lyme/) opens with five compact product facts, then explains the maintenance problem in plain operating terms. The page includes equipment and metal fit, step-by-step directions, a visible finish condition, pack information, video, SDS, bulletin, label, related products, and a contact path.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Turn a technical cleaning cycle into a visible sequence: start condition, add product, watch for a simple signal, know when it is done, flush, and restart. This makes professional chemistry feel manageable.

### 10. RYDLYME Marine

[RYDLYME Marine Biodegradable Descaler](https://rydlymemarine.com/product/rydlyme-marine-biodegradable-descaler/) uses vessel and equipment language immediately: workboats, cruise ships, yachts, coolers, condensers, engines, heat exchangers, and seawater systems. Its page offers product features, pack sizes, a long application list, brochures, SDS/specifications, FAQ, and “Add to Quote.”

**Lesson for MASEST:** Marine buyers should not have to translate a generic facility page. Name vessels, bilges, sea-water systems, hulls, docks, lifts, trailers, engine rooms, and boatyard floors directly.

### 11. Star brite

[Star brite Slime & Grime Stain Remover](https://www.starbrite.com/products/star-brite-slime-grime-stain-remover-concentrate) separates “Designed for” surfaces from “Works against” stains, then makes concentrate value easy to grasp. [Power Pine Boat Wash](https://www.starbrite.com/products/star-brite-power-pine-premium-boat-wash-super-concentrate) groups benefits under clear labels such as cleaning power, all-surface use, protection, ease, and economy, with “Find Local” as the main action.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Marine product cards should expose two fields without a click: **Use it on** and **Removes**. Add **Choose it when** where several products overlap.

### 12. 3M Marine

[3M Marine Non-Skid Cleaner](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b40070790/) starts with one hard-to-clean boat surface and one recognizable problem: ground-in dirt in textured non-skid areas. It then gives a three-verb method—pour, brush, rinse—followed by surface fit, a reason ordinary household cleaners can be a poor choice, and a link to more marine products. The broader [3M marine maintenance page](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/marine-us/products/wax-and-maintenance/) uses “Ask a 3M Expert” for buyers who need help.

**Lesson for MASEST:** Specific jobs are more persuasive than universal claims. A product can own “bilge grease,” “aluminum oxidation,” “non-skid grime,” or “heat-exchanger scale” more clearly than “multi-purpose cleaning.”

## Recurring language patterns worth adapting

| Pattern | Common category wording | Why it works | MASEST use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mess first | grease, scale, rust, grime, road film, odor | Buyer recognizes problem immediately | Put soil or deposit in headline/subhead |
| Strong action verb | cuts through, breaks down, lifts away, dissolves | Makes product action easy to picture | Pair one verb with one named soil |
| Job fit | built for, made for, designed for | Moves from generic chemistry to a familiar job | Follow with equipment, surface, or industry |
| Fast result | fast-acting, on contact, quickly removes | Speaks to labor and downtime | Use only where timing/result record supports it |
| Clean finish | easy rinse, clean rinse, no residue, no film | Answers “what happens after cleaning?” | Add to relevant degreaser and machine-cleaning pages |
| Concentrated value | makes up to, adjust the dilution, cost per use | Converts jug price into job value | Show yield and ready-to-use cost when source data exists |
| Concrete reassurance | low foam, water-based, no solvent fumes, material list | Easier to trust than vague “green” or “safe” | State the exact supported attribute beside the use case |
| Operating outcome | restore flow, bring back heat transfer, reduce repeat passes | Connects cleaning to buyer economics | Lead HVAC, plant, fleet, and CIP sections with outcome |
| Guided choice | find your match, compare products, ask an expert | Reduces anxiety in a broad catalog | Keep product finder, comparison, sample, and help paths |
| Proof near claim | reviews, field result, test result, certification, SDS | Gives buyer a reason to believe | Link each strong claim to the most relevant proof or document |

### Popular turns of phrase to use selectively

These are category conventions, not recommended slogans by themselves:

- “Cuts through…”
- “Breaks down…”
- “Lifts away…”
- “Dissolves…”
- “Built for…”
- “Works on…”
- “Fast-acting”
- “Professional strength”
- “Low foam, easy rinse”
- “Leaves no residue”
- “One gallon makes…”
- “See it in action”
- “Find your match”
- “Ask an expert”

Use one or two per block. Repetition makes the site sound generated. Specific nouns and verified numbers should carry more weight than adjectives.

## Recommended product-page story

Use this order across product pages and generated catalog content:

1. **Product name + one customer result line** — one soil, job, or operating outcome.
2. **Four fast facts** — removes, best for, works on, available sizes or starting price.
3. **Primary purchase action** — add to cart when purchasable; quote when bulk or configured.
4. **Lower-friction action** — free sample, compare, or ask for help.
5. **Jobs it handles** — five to eight specific assets and messes, not a universal application list.
6. **Why customers switch** — fewer passes, lower odor, easy rinse, material fit, or another exact supported difference.
7. **How to start** — a short three-to-five-step method with dilution or dwell guidance when supported.
8. **Proof** — starting condition, product/method, time, result, and source.
9. **Pack and value** — sizes, yield, cost per ready-to-use gallon, bulk break, recurring supply.
10. **Labels, SDS, and guides** — direct, plainly labeled downloads.
11. **FAQ** — what it removes, where it works, how to use it, which size to buy, and where to get help.

This is close to the current VertKleen structure. Main change: move the specific outcome and job fit above the generic “alternative to” language, then make yield/value easier to see.

## Plain-English safety and handling language

Competitor pages work best when they use short, concrete instructions near the relevant benefit. They work worst when “safe” becomes an unsupported catch-all.

Recommended style:

- “Read the label. Test a small area. Then get to work.”
- “Use the dilution made for the job.”
- “Do not let the cleaner dry on the surface.”
- “Rinse well before the equipment goes back into service.”
- “Check the surface list before using it on aluminum or finished materials.”
- “Current label and SDS are available below.”
- “Not sure about the surface? Ask us before you start.”

Prefer exact, helpful reassurance such as “low foam,” “no solvent fumes,” “non-flammable,” “clean rinse,” “HMIS 0-0-0,” or a named material only when the exact VertKleen product record supports it. This is clearer marketing than a vague promise that a cleaner is universally safe.

## Bundle and buying language

[Zep’s direct-store cleaner pair](https://zep.com/products/zep-all-purpose-cleaner-32-oz-and-zep-fast-505-cleaner-degreaser-32-oz) shows a useful merchandising pattern: explain why two products belong together and which different jobs each one handles. Do not rely on “bundle” alone.

Recommended VertKleen constructions:

- **Two different messes. One ready-to-work kit.**
- **Everything you need for the first job.**
- **Wash, degrease, and finish—already matched.**
- **One kit for the whole work area.**
- **Buy the job, not four separate bottles.**

Every bundle card should show:

- what is included;
- the job assigned to each product;
- pack size and total price;
- savings versus separate purchase;
- whether the buyer can reorder each component;
- one action: **Order this kit** or **Get bulk kit pricing**.

## CTA ladder

Observed manufacturer vocabulary spans the full buying journey: **Where to Buy** at Simple Green, **Add to cart** at Zep, **Find a Distributor** at CLR PRO, **Find Local** at Star brite, **Request product consulting** at Bio-Circle, **Ask a 3M Expert** at 3M Marine, **Request a Sample** on [Ecolab product pages](https://www.ecolab.com/en-us/offerings/hc/disinfectant-wipes/quaternary-disinfectant-wipes), and **Add to Quote** at RYDLYME Marine. These labels are short because each describes one clear next step.

Use a small, consistent CTA set based on buyer readiness:

| Buyer state | Recommended CTA |
| --- | --- |
| Knows the product and size | **Add to cart** / **Shop this size** |
| Knows the problem, not the product | **Find my cleaner** |
| Wants low-risk proof | **Try a free sample** / **Try it on one real job** |
| Comparing an incumbent | **Compare it with my current cleaner** |
| Needs drums, totes, or recurring supply | **Get bulk pricing** |
| Has a technical surface or system question | **Ask a product expert** |
| Wants local/channel support | **Find a distributor** |
| Buying several matched products | **Build my job kit** / **Order this kit** |

Avoid switching among “request,” “inquire,” “submit,” “consult,” and “review” when the customer is doing the same thing. Use the customer’s desired outcome as the button label.

## Priority recommendations for MASEST

### Priority 1 — sharpen product outcomes

- Keep product names clean. Put a short, customer-facing result line below each name.
- Replace the repeated visible heading **“Alternative to…”** with a job headline. Keep replacement information as a smaller comparison fact.
- Give every card four stable fields: **Removes**, **Best for**, **Works on**, and **Choose it when**.
- Show available pack pricing or the correct bulk-quote action beside those facts.
- Add yield or ready-to-use value wherever exact dilution data supports it.

Suggested examples:

- CR HD: **Heavy grease. Cleaner rinse. No solvent fumes.**
- HCR / Descaler: **Break down scale and rust. Bring flow back.**
- Purgo: **Stop masking the smell. Treat the buildup behind it.**
- MultiWash: **Wash away road film, grime, and outdoor buildup.**
- AlumiBrite: **Lift oxidation and bring aluminum back to a clean, even finish.**

### Priority 2 — organize industries by jobs buyers recognize

- Marine: group the eight products under **Boat wash**, **Bilge and engine-room grease**, **Scale and water systems**, **Aluminum oxidation**, and **Odor and organic buildup**.
- HVAC/water: group by **Low flow or poor heat transfer**, **Scale and rust**, **Coils and condensate**, and **Ongoing tower care**.
- Food/CIP: separate **oil/protein soil** from **mineral scale/beer stone**, then explain when the job needs one stage or two.
- Replace the repeated heading “Cleaning products for [industry]” with an industry-specific result line.

### Priority 3 — make proof read like a short story

Every proof card should answer four questions in this order:

1. What was dirty or failing?
2. What product and method were used?
3. How long did it take?
4. What changed?

Use one image, one verified number, and one CTA. Avoid internal terms such as record, workflow, validation route, or controlled scope in visible customer copy.

### Priority 4 — sell bundles as complete jobs

- Rename kits around the result, not the department, when possible: **Kitchen Grease Kit**, **HVAC Scale Kit**, **Boat & Boatyard Kit**, or **Garage Cleanup Kit**.
- Explain each product’s role in one short line.
- Show combined price and savings immediately.
- Use **Order this kit** for online-ready packs and **Get bulk kit pricing** for drums, totes, and repeat programs.

### Priority 5 — keep documents familiar

- Use **Download label**, **Get SDS**, **Read directions**, and **See test results**.
- Use **Request file** only when a document truly cannot be downloaded directly.
- Keep customer benefit copy above document detail; do not lead sales pages with revision IDs, internal document names, or process language.

## Slogans and anti-patterns not to copy

Several brands have distinctive slogans or trademark-adjacent constructions. Their structure can inspire MASEST, but their wording should not be reused. Examples include Ecolab’s “clean like you mean business,” CLR’s “Boldly Safe. Boldly Strong,” Krud Kutter’s hard-on-mess/gentle-on-nature line, Bio-Circle’s “Making Green Work,” RYDLYME’s “Get RYD of,” and 3M Marine’s “Make the ‘New’ Look Last.”

Also avoid:

- SEO-loaded product titles that try to hold every keyword and benefit;
- “ultimate,” “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” or “best” without specific proof;
- blanket “safe for everything,” “safe for the environment,” or “non-toxic” language;
- generic “professional grade” repeated on every product;
- giant unranked lists of every possible use;
- chemistry explanations before the customer understands the result;
- the same testimonial repeated across unrelated products;
- all-caps feature walls;
- multiple CTAs that send the customer to the same form under different names.

## Final direction

VertKleen should sound like a knowledgeable salesperson who has seen the mess before:

> Tell us what needs to come off and what it is stuck to. We will match the cleaner, pack size, and first test—then show you how to get started.

That approach is simpler than legal or technical prose, more useful than generic “green cleaning” language, and consistent with the strongest first-party sites in this benchmark.
