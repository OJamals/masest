# VertKleen blog marketing and SEO roadmap

Date: 2026-08-02

## Goal

Turn the current blog into three connected search paths:

1. Problem education: help a buyer name the soil, risk, and operating cost.
2. Proof: show a documented VertKleen result on a recognizable job.
3. Conversion: send the qualified reader to a product match, trial plan, or quote.

The current 11 articles already cover controlled trials, CIP, cooling towers, HMIS 0-0-0, descaling, and four product comparisons. New pages should deepen those clusters, not create near-duplicate URLs.

## Recommended sequence

Publish the three case studies first. They convert the strongest existing proof into crawlable, shareable pages and give every related guide a credible internal link.

Publish the calculator and two procedural guides next. They target buyers already planning work and can earn repeat visits, downloads, and quote submissions.

Publish procurement, water-treatment, exterior, and distributor pages after those hubs exist. Each should link to a case study, a current article, one product path, and one specific intake.

## Editorial rules

- Lead with the buyer’s job, not the product name, unless the query is a direct comparison.
- Keep all current offered-product claims aligned with the universal HMIS 0-0-0 catalog contract.
- State 50+ countries, up to 280× less corrosion, and 15% higher calcium-carbonate capacity with the test or source context beside the claim.
- Separate field records from universal promises. Name the asset, deposit, starting condition, method, elapsed time, and endpoint.
- Use dynamic price tokens for VertKleen. Do not hard-code competitor prices; they drift and rarely express completed-task cost.
- Link each article to one proof page, one product or guide page, and one intake matched to the article’s intent.
- Give every article one useful original visual: a process diagram, comparison table, annotated result, calculator, or printable checklist.

## Measurement

Track qualified organic entrances, article-to-product clicks, article-to-proof clicks, intake starts, completed quote or audit requests, and assisted revenue. Rankings alone do not show whether the content sells.
