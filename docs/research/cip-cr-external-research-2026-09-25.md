# VertKleen CIP CR: manufacturer research and page decisions

Research date: September 25, 2026. Product: VertKleen CIP CR, SKU VK-CR. Reviewed current EMS pages, fresh manufacturer PDF, official NSF listing, official USPTO grant, distributor materials, and supplied product/trial documents. Research only; no external submissions or deployment.

## Lead with brewery cleaning and the current SKU label

**Position CIP CR as the high-pH cleaning stage for brewery lines, kegs, tanks and heat exchangers.** Lead with organic-soil removal, 50% caustic-soda replacement, straightforward circulation/rinsing, and reduced handling burden. Show the actual Brewlando CR/HCR sequence rather than a generic application illustration.

The supplied CIP CR label explicitly identifies this SKU as a **50% caustic soda replacement**. The older VertKleen CR label gives the same replacement strength. The current [EMS catalog](https://www.enviromfg.com/products1) presents Elevate as a 50% sodium-hydroxide replacement and its [descaler/line-cleaning page](https://www.enviromfg.com/descaler) explicitly names **Elevate/CR** for beer-line and vat cleaning. These are the strongest current product-family links for this page.

## Current manufacturer document

[EMS Elevate Technical Data Sheet](https://www.enviromfg.com/s/elevate-tds.pdf), copyright 2026, freshly fetched September 25. It is byte-identical to `/Users/omar/Desktop/masest/cr/elevate-tds.pdf`: 1,038,725 bytes; SHA-256 `96c4792c5b20ad30bf0a931cc636b635545da6fc53a94a95cfb1a82a4da36e2a`.

The two-page TDS supports pH 13, no traditional hydroxides, no salt residue after use, water-rinse neutralization without a separate acid-neutralizing wash, HMIS 0-0-0, non-fuming character, no exothermic reaction on water dilution, no VOCs, full water solubility, biodegradability, and non-regulated transport. It also reports two-year shelf life and freeze/thaw stability. Its toxicology summaries reference OECD 202, 471 and 404; they provide classifications rather than underlying numerical reports. Its EPA wording concerns meeting screening criteria, not an independently identified certification. These manufacturer properties strengthen the practical cleaning story; numeric transport savings and total injury elimination are not established by this document.

The supplied VertKleen CR TDS (source copyright 2023) separately supports less-than-ten-day biodegradation and reduced severe-caustic-burn risk. It lists pH 13 and no conventional hydroxides. Use “less caustic handling risk” rather than a universal “zero worker risk” promise.

## Practical CIP directions

Source: `docs/labels/cip/vertkleen-cip-cr-label-6x8.pdf`, image-only label, OCR inspected.

| Soil load | Exact label starting amount | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Light / krausen | 0.5 L per 10 gal | Circulate hot, above 140°F, then rinse. |
| Moderate | 1 L per 10 gal | Circulate, then rinse. |
| Severe | 1.5 L per 10 gal | Circulate, then rinse with water. |

The label does not provide universal circulation minutes, a universal maximum temperature, or an explicit distinction between ten gallons of added water and ten gallons of final solution. Preserve its quantities; do not manufacture a precise RTU-yield/cost calculation. The older CR TDS refers dilution questions to the label.

The “no neutralizing step” benefit concerns stopping/rinsing the base chemistry. It does **not** mean that a separate mineral-removal stage is unnecessary: Brewlando still used HCR for exposed beerstone. Keep CR's organic-soil job and HCR's mineral job clear.

## Best field evidence: Brewlando

Source: `docs/brewery-cip-trial-brewlando.pdf`; original supplied copy `/Users/omar/Desktop/masest/docs/VertKleen CR and HCR Brewery Trial-Brewlando Brewing.pdf`.

The mash tank had gone approximately ten days without CIP after IPA production, leaving a residue ring and heavy bottom deposits. The trial circulated **5 L CR added to 55 gallons water at 160–170°F for 25 minutes**, after one minute of mixing, through the tank and heat exchanger. The brewer judged the tank very clean; remaining organic residue rinsed away. The CR-stage photo describes beerstone exposed and ready for HCR.

The separate HCR stage used 5 L in 55 gallons water at 160–170°F for 30 minutes, followed by rinsing/draining. The final clean-tank outcome belongs to the **combined CR + HCR cycle**. The report says treatment concentration and cycle time matched the existing chemicals. It does not prove a universal shorter cycle, guaranteed chemical reduction, or CR-only mineral removal. No microbiological counts or named sanitization assay are included; use it as cleaning evidence.

Recommended presentation: a named field example with exact CR-stage conditions and a linked HCR follow-on. Retain the existing approved brewery proof record and controlled document.

## Laboratory comparison: useful, but not a superiority headline

Source: `docs/carib-brewery-lab-report.pdf`; supplied original `/Users/omar/Desktop/masest/docs/VertKleen-Green chemicals. Test report Carib Brewery.pdf`, dated December 15, 2023.

The CR comparison reports pH 13.1 versus incumbent caustic above 14; sample concentrations 25% versus 45%; yeast removal approximately 1.30 g versus 1.25 g. CR's measured N/P/COD values were zero in this test. The report describes the samples as similarly effective, says no important performance benefit was detected, and recommends industrial testing on safety grounds. It does not establish a 44% chemical saving or a universal lower-dose guarantee. The HCR rust-removal result is separate from CR.

Strong usable point: **comparable yeast-removal performance in the Carib laboratory comparison**, with the source available for technical buyers.

## Certification and patent checks

The [official NSF listing for EMS](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/PwsChemicals/Listings.asp?Company=C0284921&Standard=060) was fetched directly and showed September 25, 2026, 12:15 a.m. Eastern. It lists **pHlex CR**, NSF/ANSI/CAN 60, for pH adjustment, precipitation and softening at maximum **92 mg/L**. It does not name VertKleen CIP CR or Elevate. Keep this potable-water treatment entry separate from brewery cleaning directions and from any claim that cleaning establishes sanitization.

The [official USPTO grant PDF for US 11,311,036 B2](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11311036) was downloaded and its first/final pages OCR-inspected. It confirms *Synthetic base and associated methods*, inventor John T. MacDonald, Green Products & Technologies LLC, granted April 26, 2022; related grants 10,368,567 and 9,399,589. Its three issued claims concern hydraulic fracturing using a base made with glycine, calcium hydroxide and sodium percarbonate. This is a related technology-family record, not a public recipe or CIP CR patent-to-SKU mapping. No particular patent number should be promoted as the current CIP CR formulation's proof.

The [EMS patent page](https://www.enviromfg.com/patents) remains a request form, with no public formula crosswalk. No request submitted. Manufacturer-backed “EMS SynClean caustic-replacement technology” is sufficient if a platform reference is useful. Patent research does not establish current enforceability or certification.

## Distributor positioning worth adopting

[Heartland's pHlex CR page](https://heartlandenergygroup.net/product/phlex-cr/) emphasizes simpler water dilution, less salt residue, common-carrier shipping and reduced dependence on a neutralizing acid wash. These themes also appear in the current EMS Elevate document. Use those practical benefits; avoid importing its inconsistent 40%/60% strength presentation, one-year shelf-life figure, or treating its broad Safer Choice language as a verified SKU certification.

[ZEL Distribution](https://zeldistro.com/) frames EMS chemistry around the work crews perform and reduced dangerous-goods handling. This is a useful marketing pattern, not product-specific CIP performance evidence. Focus the MASEST page on cleaning outcomes, repeatable starting amounts, real brewery evidence and straightforward procurement.

## Internal follow-ups and image screening

- The CIP label says aluminum-safe when diluted, while the older CR label prohibits aluminum fittings and the TDS advises against aluminum piping/fittings. Keep transfer-system guidance specific: supplier-approved materials, with 316 stainless steel, polypropylene or schedule 80 PVC identified by the older label. Do not convert diluted-surface wording into blanket aluminum-system compatibility.
- The SDS describes mild skin/eye irritation and directs users to keep product out of storm drains. Do not add unrestricted ground/water disposal or a guarantee of no possible exposure injury.
- Manufacturer/label assertions about no separate containment or non-hazmat shipping can be presented as documented handling advantages; destination freight cost still depends on the shipment. No numeric shipping comparison was found.
- Do not create an EPA Safer Choice logo/certification claim from criteria-conformity wording alone.
- Screen the existing CIP application rendering against the real trial photos. Synthetic scenes should be flagged and replaced by authentic approved evidence or removed from this page; do not remove their registered source assets shared with other products.
