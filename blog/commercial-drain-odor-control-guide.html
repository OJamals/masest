How to remove commercial drain odors at the source

MASEST Technical Team · August 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Drain odor is usually a symptom

A spray of fragrance can hide a smell from the doorway. It does not clean the drain, restore a dry trap, or remove the organic film feeding the odor.

A better route starts with the source. Purgo gives facility teams a treatment for odor-causing organic residue instead of another scent layered over the problem.

Find which drain is talking

Walk the area when the odor is present. Check floor drains, sink drains, mop basins, wet-zone channels, and nearby traps one by one.

What the crew seesLikely starting pointFirst move
Odor after a quiet or dry periodTrap water has droppedRestore the trap and watch whether it holds
Odor after washdown or heavy useOrganic film inside the drainBrush and rinse the accessible surface
Slow flow with the odorPartial blockageRestore flow before treating the residue
Odor returns on a steady scheduleRecurring organic buildupClean, apply Purgo, and track the interval

That quick check keeps the team from pouring product into the wrong drain or treating a plumbing problem like a cleaning problem.

Clean away the film first

Remove loose soil, grease, hair, and visible residue from the drain opening and nearby surface. Flush the accessible area so Purgo reaches the film behind the smell instead of sitting on top of old dirt.

A clean starting point also makes the return interval easier to judge.

Treat the odor source with Purgo

Purgo targets odor-causing bacteria and organic residue. For odor control on non-food-contact surfaces, the current label calls for a 1:64 dilution and one minute of contact time.

Apply the measured solution across the cleaned drain surface. Give it contact, then put the area back into its normal routine.

Build the routine around the return interval

Record the drain, date, dilution, odor level, and days until the smell returns. The first few treatments show whether the route should run weekly, monthly, or around predictable high-use periods.

That turns an odor complaint into a maintenance schedule the crew can own.

Use one route across wet zones

Restaurants, hotels, gyms, campuses, and large facilities often have several drains with different traffic and soil. The same five-step route works across them: find, check, clean, treat, and track.

Start with the drain producing the most complaints. Once the interval holds, extend the method to the next wet zone instead of treating every drain on guesswork.
