How to remove commercial drain odors at the source

A practical Purgo plan for finding recurring commercial drain odors, cleaning the source, treating odor-causing organic residue, and setting a repeatable schedule.

Recurring drain odor is a maintenance signal

The mop sink smells after close, the dish-area floor drain slows during service, or the restroom odor returns the morning after a spray. Fragrance can cover the first impression. It cannot refill a dry trap, restore flow, or remove the organic film feeding the repeat call.

The fastest durable answer begins with diagnosis, then uses Purgo where organic residue is the source.

Identify the failure before adding product

Walk the area while the odor is present. Check floor drains, sink drains, mop basins, wet-zone channels, traps, and nearby cleanouts.

What you findLikely causeFirst action
Odor after a long dry periodTrap water lossRestore the seal and confirm it holds
Odor after washdownOrganic filmBrush and flush the accessible drain
Slow flow with odorPartial blockageRestore flow before treatment
Predictable recurrenceRebuilding organic loadClean, treat, and schedule by return interval

This step prevents crews from treating a plumbing defect as a chemical problem.

Remove the material Purgo should not have to fight through

Lift hair, grease, food soil, and loose debris from the opening. Brush the reachable wall and flush the line.

Purgo can then contact the residue responsible for odor instead of sitting on top of a dirty drain.

Apply the labeled odor-control method

For odor control on non-food-contact surfaces, the current Purgo label calls for a 1:64 dilution and one minute of contact time.

Measure the solution, wet the cleaned surface, allow the labeled contact time, and return the drain to service as directed.

Purgo targets odor-causing bacteria and organic residue. This is an odor-control application; it does not turn the drain routine into an antiseptic or disinfectant program.

Schedule the drain by how it behaves

Record the drain, date, dilution, condition, and days until odor returns. High-use kitchen drains may need a different interval from a quiet restroom or locker-room channel.

A simple return log converts complaints into planned labor and reveals drains that need plumbing attention.
