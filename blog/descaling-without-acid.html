Industrial descaling without the conventional mineral-acid burden

MASEST Technical Team · June 20, 2026 · 2 min read

Scale charges rent every operating hour

Carbonate scale narrows flow and insulates heat-transfer surfaces. The system pays through longer run time, higher energy demand, unstable temperatures, restricted capacity, and unplanned shutdown risk.

A descaling job must recover performance without trading the deposit for uncontrolled material attack, fumes, or a difficult restart.

This is controlled chemistry, not magic

VertKleen HCR and Descaler use controlled hydrogen-ion activity to consume carbonate scale: 2 H+ + CaCO3 → Ca2+ + CO2 + H2O.

Complexing, wetting, and inhibition help released material move toward rinse-out. The products are not “acid-free”; they replace a conventional mineral-acid workflow with controlled mineral removal.

The evidence changes the risk calculation

Source testing reports a 0.59 mpy steel-corrosion rate for HCR—up to 280× less corrosion than hydrochloric acid under the reported comparison.

The same source record reports 15% more calcium-carbonate capacity than hydrochloric acid. That combination targets scale while protecting more of the equipment value around it.

A field result worth testing against

At a Brevard County School District HVAC system, an incumbent CLR attempt ran 36 hours without derusting a 20-year stainless-steel plate and drain. DDC Engineering then selected HCR; the field report records a 30-minute application, garden-hose rinse, and no scrubbing.

That field record is not a universal cycle promise. It is a strong reason to run a controlled HCR trial on the actual system and count the completed job.
