How to test a safer industrial cleaner before you switch

A simple side-by-side trial shows which cleaner delivers the better finish with less product, labor, water, downtime, and hassle.

Make the cleaner prove itself on your dirt

A useful comparison does not begin with a polished sample. It begins with the recurring job that costs your crew time.

Choose the oily panel, scaled part, scrubber lane, or process surface everyone recognizes. Define what “finished” means before either cleaner touches it.

Match chemistry to the actual layer

Scale and rust need mineral removal. Grease, protein, and organic film need a degreaser or caustic replacement.

Layered soil may require two stages. Removing oil first can expose mineral scale that no descaler could reach through the film.

Use the product catalog and application guides to select the closest fit.

Keep four things equal

Hold constantExamples
SurfaceAlloy, coating, age, and condition
SoilType, load, and time on the surface
MethodArea, tool, temperature, dwell, and rinse
FinishVisible soil, residue, flow, pressure, or dryness

Change only the cleaner and its labeled use rate. That gives technicians and managers a result they can recognize without a complicated scoring system.

Capture the work surrounding the chemical

Record concentrate and mixed solution, hands-on minutes, scrub passes, water, equipment interruptions, rework, and time back to service.

The comparison should end at a completed job, not when the reaction stops.

A cleaner with a higher gallon price can still cost less when it reduces labor, repeat work, rinse volume, or asset downtime.

Include the handling difference

VertKleen products are HMIS 0-0-0, ship non-hazmat, and add no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use.

The surface, soil, pressure, heat, access, and site procedure still determine job protection.

When performance is comparable or better, that product profile can simplify freight, storage, training, and deployment across locations.

Turn the winning method into one clear instruction

Save the product, dilution, contact time, tool, rinse, finish, and completed-job cost.

Add a starting photo and a finished photo from the same angle. That single page becomes the crew method and the purchasing rationale.
