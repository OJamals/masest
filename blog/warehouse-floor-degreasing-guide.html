A warehouse floor records every shift

Forklifts leave rubber. Equipment leaves oil. Docks bring in dust, water, and road film. The buildup concentrates where traffic turns: dock approaches, staging lanes, cross aisles, and the edges a scrubber misses.

Clean in sections that can return to traffic between trailer moves. The finish is a recovered, dry lane with no slick film or dark transfer left for the next shift. OSHA's loading-dock guidance makes clear work surfaces part of the daily dock operation.

The floor becomes expensive when the scrubber repeats lanes, the recovery tank foams, or traffic waits for a greasy surface to dry.

Treat the heavy soil before the machine reaches it

Sweep loose debris and mark oil spots, greasy turns, and dense tire buildup.

Give those areas CR HD working time first. The scrubber can then clean and recover the full lane instead of using every pass as spot treatment.

CR HD Low Foam keeps the recovery tank focused on dirty solution. Fewer foam stops mean more floor completed per machine hour.

Run the lane in one direction

Sweep, spot-treat, scrub, recover, and inspect before traffic returns.

Use overlapping passes at the chosen machine speed. Check corners, rack ends, dock transitions, and the greasy turn radius where a straight scrubber path often misses.

Do not reopen the lane until the surface meets the facility’s traction and dryness standard.

Three Walmart sites show the consolidation value

Walmart distribution centers DC-8851, DC-7023, and DC-6099 replaced Simple Green with CR HD.

The use went beyond floors to forklifts, parts, workshops, kitchens, drains, windows, and glass.

One concentrate serving that range reduces overlapping stock while keeping heavy-duty capacity available for the hardest lane.

Make routine use easier across the building

CR HD, CR HD Low Foam, Neutral, and MultiWash are HMIS 0-0-0 and ship non-hazmat.

They add no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use. Traffic control, oily residue, moving machinery, wet floors, and recovered wastewater still shape the work plan.

Measure cost per clean lane

Count solution used, spot-treatment minutes, scrubber passes, foam stops, recovery water, drying time, and repeat work.

Then divide by lanes or square feet returned to traffic. That number shows whether the cleaner is improving both labor and machine use.
