Drone building cleaning: a practical plan for façades and hard-to-reach surfaces

MASEST Team · August 3, 2026 · 3 min read

The drone solves access. The cleaning plan solves the surface.

A cleaning drone can reach glass, cladding, roofs, canopies, and other elevated surfaces without putting a worker at every spray point.

The finish still comes from the same basics: identify the dirt, choose the right cleaner, give it time to work, rinse with control, and inspect the result.

Match the cleaner to the buildup

Exterior conditionStart withWhy it fits
Dust, traffic film, and everyday grimeMultiWashPractical cleaning across mixed exterior surfaces
Algae, mildew, moss, and organic stainingLAM3Longer wetting and working time on growth-related buildup

Start with a small ground-level test. The best product is the one that reaches the finish with fewer repeats and a clean, even rinse.

Run one five-stage plan

Map the building, test the cleaner, set the flight path, apply and rinse, then compare the finished surface. Each stage answers a different part of the job.

This keeps the crew from treating flight planning and cleaning as two separate projects. The hose, product, working time, flight path, runoff, and finish all belong in one plan.

Prove the mix from the ground

Choose a test patch that represents the real façade. Keep the surface, dirt, dilution, working time, scrubbing, rinse, and weather notes together.

Look for even wetting, dirt removal, rinse behavior, streaking, residue, and finish after drying. A good ground test gives the pilot a flight path worth repeating in the air.

Build the flight around the building

Most commercial drone-cleaning work in the United States is planned under FAA Part 107.

Map airspace, visual line of sight, people, wind, hose or tether movement, overspray, and the takeoff area. Then split the building into sections the crew can apply, working time, and rinse consistently.

A calm, repeatable flight path gives the cleaner enough time to work across the whole elevation.

Use coverage and working time instead of brute force

High pressure is a poor substitute for the right cleaner. MultiWash handles everyday exterior dirt, while LAM3 or CR HD can step in when growth or oily buildup calls for a specialist.

The drone places the solution where access is difficult. Wetting and working time loosen the dirt, and an even rinse carries it away with less dependence on close-range impact.
