A drone changes access. The chemistry still determines the finish.

Cleaning drones can place solution on glass, cladding, roofs, canopies, and façades without positioning a worker at every spray point.

The commercial gain comes when access, product, dwell, rinse, and flight path operate as one system. On a tenant-occupied building, the ground crew is also watching entrance doors, canopy edges, hose travel, and where the rinse runs. Price a façade section that can be inspected and reopened—not flight minutes alone.

Match the cleaner before planning the flight

Exterior soilProductWhy it fits
Dust and traffic filmMultiWashBroad cleaning across mixed exterior surfaces
Moss, algae, lichen, and mildew stainsLAM3Long working time on organic exterior buildup
Oily film, soot, and heavy grimeCR HDAdded capacity for demanding hydrocarbon soil

A ground-level patch should establish dilution, coverage, dwell, rinse behavior, and dry finish. That result becomes the operating standard for the elevation.

Design the route around working time

Divide the building into sections the crew can wet evenly and rinse in sequence. Account for wind, sun, overspray, hose or tether movement, water supply, and runoff.

A stable route lets the chemistry work consistently. Racing for square footage usually creates streaks, missed bands, and repeat flights.

Use cleaning action instead of unnecessary pressure

MultiWash handles common façade film. LAM3 works over time on organic staining. CR HD steps in for soot and oily grime.

The drone provides reach and even placement. Wetting and dwell loosen the soil so the rinse depends less on close-range impact.

That can protect delicate surfaces and reduce the labor tied to repeated access setup.

Keep aviation and job controls intact

Most commercial drone-cleaning work in the United States operates under FAA Part 107.

VertKleen products are HMIS 0-0-0, ship non-hazmat, and add no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use.

Flight operations, people below, electrical hazards, tether pressure, overspray, runoff, and the soil itself still determine the work-zone controls.
