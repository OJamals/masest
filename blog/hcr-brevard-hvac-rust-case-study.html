CLR had 36 hours. HCR needed 30 minutes.

A Brevard County School District HVAC crew faced heavy rust on a 20-year stainless-steel diamond plate and drain.

CLR remained on the area for 36 hours without clearing the rust. DDC Engineering then applied VertKleen HCR.

The crew used a simple method

StageMethodRecorded result
Initial attemptCLR on the plate and drainRust remained after 36 hours
HCR applicationSprayed onto the rusted surface30-minute contact time
FinishGarden-hose rinseBuildup washed away
ScrubbingNone recordedPlate and drain visibly recovered

DDC Engineering described the HCR result as exceptional.

DDC Engineering and Ivey Construction both said that starting with HCR would have saved time and money.

The before and after show the change

Before: rust and mineral buildup covered the drain area.

After: 30 minutes with HCR, a hose rinse, and no recorded scrubbing.

Faster effective cleaning changes the maintenance bill

The difference between 36 hours and 30 minutes affects scheduling, crew availability, water, monitoring, and return to service.

It also reduces the chance that a stubborn cleaning attempt occupies an equipment area across multiple shifts.

The result gives maintenance teams a concrete model for cutting labor, schedule pressure, and completed-job cost on difficult rust and scale.

HCR pairs power with straightforward handling

HCR is HMIS 0-0-0, non-fuming, non-hazmat for shipping, and adds no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use.

The HVAC task still carries its own controls for access, lockout, sharp metal, rinsate, and any surrounding equipment.

Source testing also measured HCR at 0.59 mpy on steel, up to 280 times less corrosion than hydrochloric acid in the same comparison.
