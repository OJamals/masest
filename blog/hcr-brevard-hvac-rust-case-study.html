How HCR cleaned a 20-year HVAC base plate after a 36-hour CLR attempt

MASEST Technical Team · August 3, 2026 · 4 min read

The job had already consumed 36 hours

A useful case study starts with the asset, the method, and the result—not a generic product promise.

The source field report covers emergency rust removal on a commercial HVAC system operated by the Brevard County School District. The work area was a 20-year stainless-steel diamond plate and drain. After 36 hours with CLR did not derust the area, DDC Engineering selected VertKleen HCR for the next attempt.

This was not a laboratory comparison or a claim about every CLR formula. It was a real maintenance problem with a documented operating clock.

The field sequence was short and visible

HCR applicationSprayed on the rusted surfaces30-minute contact time

The report describes the HCR result as exceptional. It also records comments from DDC Engineering and Ivey Construction that starting with HCR would have saved time and money.

That is the commercial point: chemistry matters, but the completed task includes elapsed time, labor, rinse, and the condition of the asset when the crew leaves.

See the recorded condition change

Before: visible rust and deposit remained around the HVAC drain area.

After: the field report records a 30-minute HCR application, garden-hose rinse, and no scrubbing.

The photos are field evidence, not a controlled coupon test. Lighting, framing, water, and surface geometry differ. The method and time record make them useful; they do not turn one job into a universal cycle guarantee.

The source names CLR, not the current CLR PRO MAX formulation. Use the case as a reason to test HCR—not as a substitute for current product documents and a matched comparison.

HMIS 0-0-0 changes the trial conversation

Every VertKleen product MASEST now offers is HMIS 0-0-0, including HCR. That lower-hazard profile sits beside the rust and scale result rather than being traded against it.

Current labels, SDS documents, site rules, wastewater controls, and task-specific PPE still govern the work. HMIS 0-0-0 supports a simpler operating plan; it does not remove the need for one.

Price the completed task, not the gallon

A 36-hour attempt and a 30-minute application are not equivalent operating events. The purchase decision should count everything the facility pays for.

A lower gallon price can still produce the more expensive job. A controlled HCR trial makes the full cost visible before a facility changes its SOP.

Recreate the decision safely

Start with a coupon, removable part, or bounded area. Hold substrate, deposit, application, contact time, agitation, rinse, and acceptance endpoint steady. Photograph the same view before and after.

Record the current product and formula, lot, concentration, surface temperature, crew time, water, wastewater, and material condition. Release the area only after the facility’s own inspection criteria pass.
