Oil and gas cleaning: match the deposit to the maintenance job

Clear grease and scale so equipment can reach inspection and repair. Match the deposit to the product before the maintenance window opens.

An asset cannot be inspected through the buildup

Hydrocarbon film hides leaks and service points. Scale masks water-side surfaces.

Carbon and drilling residue add labor before a valve, pump, exchanger, or rig component can reach the maintenance bench.

Once equipment is drained, cleared, and released for work, the fastest path is to identify the deposit and remove it with the product made for that job.

The Oil, Gas and Process Plants guide covers the broader sequence.

Degrease oil; descale minerals

Use CR HD for hydrocarbon film, grease, and carbon on compatible isolated equipment.

Use HCR or Descaler for rust and mineral scale in the defined descaling route.

Choose VertKleen Neutral for lighter compatible surface cleaning.

Do not spend a maintenance window circulating a degreaser through scale or applying a descaler to an oily skid.

Remove loose product, set containment, apply the matched cleaner, recover the wash, rinse, and inspect.

A pump skid may need clear fasteners and service points; an exchanger plate may need exposed surfaces for the next maintenance decision.

Define that finish before work starts.

The maintenance planner needs a valve manifold, pump skid, or plate pack ready for the next inspection—not another asset waiting at the wash station. Specify the deposit and required finish on the work order so the wash crew and mechanic agree on the handoff. OSHA's equipment-condition guidance connects inspection with the asset's maintenance and repair history.

Crews spend less time scrubbing the wrong chemistry, use fewer repeat applications, and move equipment to inspection or repair sooner.

Order against the turnaround scope: which skids return to the yard, which plates need descaling, and which wash stations need degreaser. That keeps a missing cleaning supply from holding up an otherwise ready inspection.
