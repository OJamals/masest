Beer line cleaner cost: compare the complete CIP cycle

MASEST Technical Team · July 9, 2026 · 2 min read

Bottle price hides the real bill

A cleaner can look inexpensive and still create an expensive cycle. Dose, circulation, rinse water, labor, repeat passes, downtime, and release determine what the brewery actually pays.

Compare completed cycles on the same line and soil load. A fair result includes clean equipment, a verified rinse, and release back to production.

Beer lines carry two soil families

Yeast, protein, fat, and organic film respond to alkaline soil lift. Beer stone, carbonate scale, and rust need controlled mineral removal. One chemistry rarely does both jobs efficiently.

Soil or depositVertKleen stageJob to complete
Yeast and proteinCIP CRWet, lift, and carry organic soil
Fat and organic filmCIP CRBreak loading and limit redeposition
Beer stone and carbonateCIP HCRRelease mineral deposit
Rust and mineral filmCIP HCRRemove deposit before final rinse

Sequence CR, rinse, then HCR

CIP CR uses alkalinity, wetting, and sequestration for the organic load. After a verified rinse, CIP HCR follows with controlled mineral-removal chemistry for beer stone and scale.

Do not mix stages. Record dose, temperature, circulation, rinse endpoint, and the facility’s sanitation and release steps for each circuit.

Compare the incumbent fairly

Micro Matic describes its alkaline beer-line cleaner as caustic and recommends a 2–3% use concentration. Its own cleaning program also separates alkaline and acid work.

That makes cycle design—not sticker price—the useful comparison. Test equal circuits and include every stage required to reach the same endpoint.
