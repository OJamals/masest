Beer line cleaner cost: what a full CIP cycle really costs

MASEST Team · July 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Bottle price is only the first line

A low-priced cleaner can still create an expensive cycle. Water, labor, repeat passes, and line downtime often cost more than the chemical itself.

The number that matters is the cost to get a clean line back into production.

Beer lines collect two kinds of buildup

Yeast, protein, fat, and organic film respond to an alkaline cleaner. Beer stone, mineral scale, and rust need a cleaner made for mineral buildup.

Using one cleaner for both jobs often means more time, more product, and more rinsing.

What needs cleaningVertKleen stageWhat it does
Yeast and proteinCIP CRLifts and carries away organic buildup
Fat and organic filmCIP CRBreaks buildup and helps keep it from returning quickly
Beer stone and carbonateCIP HCRReleases mineral buildups
Rust and mineral filmCIP HCRClears buildups before the final rinse

Run CR first, then HCR

CIP CR tackles the organic layer first. Once that stage is rinsed away, CIP HCR goes after beer stone, scale, and rust.

This two-step approach gives each cleaner one clear job and keeps the cycle easier to repeat.

Track the amount used, circulation time, water, and total time from setup to a line that is ready to pour again.
