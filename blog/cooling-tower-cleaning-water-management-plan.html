A cooling tower should not be managed one shutdown at a time

Evaporation concentrates minerals. Air adds dust and organic load. Heat-transfer surfaces lose efficiency long before the tower looks completely fouled.

The strongest program pairs daily water management with a planned method for removing what has already accumulated.

On a tower round, check leaving-water temperature alongside outdoor wet bulb, conductivity, makeup, blowdown, and strainer condition. Operators call the gap between leaving-water temperature and entering-air wet bulb the tower's approach. Compare it at similar load and flow when assessing a change in performance. ASHRAE explains approach and range.

Leave time in the shutdown for the basin, fill, strainers, and feed controls. The tower needs to return with its cleaning work and water-management controls complete.

Give each chemistry a defined role

Operating needProductRole in the program
Scale and corrosion controlWaterSafe60Ongoing water treatment
Organic buildup and odorPurgoTargeted ongoing treatment
Existing mineral scaleHCR or DescalerPlanned circulation clean
Oily filmCR or CR HDRemove the layer covering mineral deposits

This division prevents crews from overfeeding a maintenance product to solve an established deposit or using a shutdown cleaner as a permanent feed.

Read the deposit before scheduling the work

Inspect the basin, fill, strainers, piping, and accessible heat-transfer surfaces.

Hard scale points to HCR or Descaler. Oily film needs CR or CR HD first. Organic residue and odor belong in the Purgo portion of the program.

When deposits are layered, remove the oil or organic film before expecting a descaler to reach the mineral underneath.

Build the cleaning window backward from restart

The published HCR and Descaler guide sets out a circulation sequence for cooling towers: blow down, drain the sump, fill with the selected solution, circulate, drain, flush, and refill.

Plan product volume, temporary circulation, rinse-water handling, inspection, refill, and treatment restart before isolation begins.

The real economic measure is the interval from shutdown to stable operation, including repeat circulation and delayed restart.

Easier chemistry removes operating friction

The VertKleen products in this program are HMIS 0-0-0, ship non-hazmat, and add no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use.

Tower access, lockout, biological exposure, work at height, aerosols, and site water controls still determine the job protection.

The practical gain is simpler freight, storage, and product training around a complex maintenance event.

Keep the evidence for the next cycle

Record deposit photos, product and dilution, circulation time, water used, surface condition, restart readings, and the next inspection date.

Those records show whether the program is extending clean operation and reduce the guesswork at the next shutdown.
