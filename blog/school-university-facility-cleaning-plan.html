Campus cleaning: keep facilities ready on schedule

From the next lunch service to evening practice, campus cleaning has a deadline. Put the right products where crews need them.

The bell sets the deadline

Campus cleaning happens between events: a gym before practice, a kitchen before meal service, a restroom before the next class, or an air-handler coil during a short shutdown.

The custodial lead is working around lunch service, evening practice, deliveries, and the next bell. A room held for another scrub or a wet-floor callback shifts work onto the next crew. Put the reopening time on the work order, along with who checks the finished area.

The Education Facilities guide helps buyers match each space to its soil instead of stocking overlapping cleaners that still leave crews scrubbing.

One campus, five practical cleaning jobs

  • Use MultiWash for scuffs, tracked grime, and body film on compatible floors and public surfaces.
  • Use CR HD to cut grease and soil in kitchens and service areas before the required sanitation step.
  • Use Descaler or HCR for mineral buildup on compatible fixtures and mechanical equipment.
  • Use LAM3 for suitable exterior staining and hardscape work.
  • Use VertKleen Neutral for compatible everyday surfaces and sensitive-finish cleaning.

Work from the return-to-use time backward.

Empty the area, remove loose soil, apply the right product, allow the cleaning step to work, rinse as required, and inspect before reopening.

Matching product to soil helps crews finish faster with fewer repeat passes.

A smaller product set is easier to cover when a custodian is out or a team moves between buildings. Label the job clearly—public-surface grime, kitchen grease, or mineral scale—and keep the dilution instruction at the closet or cart. EPA's school maintenance guidance also calls for work orders that record work and cost by equipment, making repeat cleaning easier to spot.
