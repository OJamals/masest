Data-center cooling: clean scale within the maintenance window

Make the cooling maintenance window count. Target scale and water-side deposits with a defined cleaning route and a clear plan for restart.

MASEST Team · September 7, 2026 · 2 min read

In this article
  1. Make every minute of the cooling window count\n\nA fouled plate exchanger or condenser-water loop can consume a maintenance window before the real work is finished.\n\nScale, iron oxide, silt, and corrosion products restrict water-side surfaces and make heat-transfer equipment harder to inspect.\n\nIn a water-cooled plant, the chief engineer is tracing the load through computer-room air handlers, chilled-water equipment, condenser-water pumps, and heat rejection. Before taking a train offline, confirm which redundant equipment carries the live load and which valves define the cleaning loop. Reserve part of the window for refill, venting, leak checks, and stable temperatures. DOE maps this cooling chain.\n\nDefine the component before the outage: material, deposit, isolation points, circulation route, rinse destination, and restart requirement.\n\nKeep live IT equipment outside the work boundary. The Data Centers application guide helps match water-side deposits to the planned maintenance job.\n\n## Clean the loop you planned to clean\n\nUse HCR or VertKleen Descaler for the matched descaling route on plate exchangers, piping, strainers, and compatible closed-loop components.\n\nUse WaterSafe60 as part of the specified active cooling-water program.\n\nA productive sequence is short:\n\n1. Isolate the designated water-side component.\n2. Remove loose material from strainers and accessible surfaces.\n3. Circulate the selected cleaner through the defined route.\n4. Rinse to the restart requirement.\n5. Return the system to its water-treatment program.\n\nThis targeted approach is faster and more economical than treating the entire mechanical room as one cleaning problem.\n\nIt concentrates product and labor where fouling is actually restricting the system, shortens troubleshooting, and reduces repeat work during a limited outage.\n\n## Have the cleaning charge on site before the outage\n\nWaterSafe60, HCR, and Descaler are HMIS 0-0-0, noncorrosive, and nonflammable.\n\nNormal product use requires no chemical-specific PPE or ventilation. The products ship as nonhazardous freight, with no hazmat surcharge.\n\nMechanical-room PPE, lockout, isolation, drainage, and commissioning requirements still apply to the task.\n\nCleaning chemistry stays in the planned water-side route, away from active server aisles.\n\nRecord the component, deposit, product, circulation time, rinse result, and restart condition.\n\nThat record makes the next outage easier to price and schedule.\n\nSee the cooling-tower cleaning plan for treatment-program coordination.\n\nMASEST water-management services can help plan a broader system review.\n\nOrder WaterSafe60, shop descaling products, or schedule a cooling-maintenance review.
