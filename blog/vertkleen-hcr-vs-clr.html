The Brevard job turned a product comparison into a time comparison

CLR remained on a rusted 20-year stainless-steel HVAC plate and drain for 36 hours without clearing the area.

DDC Engineering then applied VertKleen HCR for 30 minutes and rinsed it with a garden hose. No scrubbing was recorded.

That named result puts labor and downtime at the center of the decision.

CLR PRO MAX is built for heavy scale

The Brevard report names CLR. The product comparison below covers CLR PRO MAX, the industrial descaler in the CLR range.

CLR PRO MAX is sold for industrial rust, lime, calcium, and scale. Its product guide starts heavy-duty work at 1:1 and lists a two-to-three-hour use window.

Its current SDS lists corrosive skin and eye hazards for the sold product and use dilution.

See CLR PRO MAX product informationhttps://www.clrbrands.com/proline/products/clr-pro/clr-pro-max-industrial-descaler/

HCR brings stronger equipment economics

Source testing measured HCR at 0.59 mpy on steel, up to 280 times less corrosion than hydrochloric acid.

The same testing measured 15% more calcium-carbonate capacity than hydrochloric acid.

More capacity for the deposit and less attack on the steel protect the asset while the crew works toward a clean restart.

HCR simplifies the work around the deposit

HCR adds no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use and creates no hazmat shipping surcharge.

Maintenance controls for pressure, isolation, access, gas release, rinsate, and the equipment remain in place.

The practical gain is fewer acid-specific complications before, during, and after the shutdown.
