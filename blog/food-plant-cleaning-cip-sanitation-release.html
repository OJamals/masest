A productive CIP cycle removes soils in the right order

Protein, fat, yeast, and process film behave differently from beer stone, rust, and mineral scale.

CIP CR clears the organic layer. CIP HCR follows on the inorganic deposit. This two-stage sequence lets each chemistry reach the soil it handles best.

Stage one: remove organic process soil

CIP CR loosens protein, fat, yeast, krausen, and organic film so flow can carry them out of the circuit.

Track concentration, temperature, return clarity, and visible load. Rinse the CR stage fully before changing chemistry.

Stage two: remove beer stone and mineral film

CIP HCR reaches the beer stone, carbonate, rust, and mineral buildup exposed by the first stage.

The separation reduces the tendency to extend one chemistry, add more product, or repeat a cycle that was aimed at the wrong soil.

A completed timer alone is not a useful handoff to quality and production. Record return flow, concentration, temperature, and cycle time, then confirm the rinse and the plant's release checks. Tetra Pak's dairy-equipment handbook treats those variables as a connected cleaning system. It also notes that milk film on cold surfaces becomes harder to remove when allowed to dry—one reason a delayed start can lengthen the next cycle.

Cleaning prepares the line for sanitation

CIP CR and CIP HCR remove soil. They are not presented as antiseptics or as replacements for the facility’s approved sanitizer.

After cleaning, complete the established rinse, sanitation, inspection, and production-release checks.

A cleaner surface gives that final control a better starting point.

The product profile simplifies a recurring cycle

Both CIP products are HMIS 0-0-0, ship non-hazmat, and add no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use.

Hot solution, pressure, confined spaces, lockout, process residue, and the plant’s sanitation program still determine job protection.

The advantage is less chemical-handling friction around work the plant performs repeatedly.
