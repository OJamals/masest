Food-plant CIP: match each cleaning stage to the soil

MASEST Technical Team · July 26, 2026 · 2 min read

One circuit can hold two kinds of buildup

Protein, fat, yeast, and organic film behave differently from beer stone, rust, and mineral scale.

Asking one cleaner to do both jobs usually adds time, product, and rinse water. A two-step cycle lets each chemistry focus on the soil it handles best.

Use CIP CR for the organic layer

VertKleen CIP CR loosens protein, fat, yeast, krausen, and organic film. Flow carries that released soil toward the drain.

Watch the return clarity, temperature, concentration, and visible load. Those signs tell the crew when the organic stage has done its job.

Follow with CIP HCR for beer stone and scale

After the CR stage is rinsed away, CIP HCR tackles beer stone, carbonate scale, rust, and mineral film.

Keeping the stages separate gives each product a clean shot at its target and makes the cycle easier to repeat.

Clean first, sanitize second

Cleaning clears away the soil that can hide microorganisms or weaken a sanitizer.

Once CR and HCR have done that work, the facility can run its normal final rinse, sanitation, inspection, and production check on a cleaner surface.

Count the time until production restarts

A fast chemical reaction only helps if the full line comes back sooner.

Count preparation, circulation, rinsing, inspection, and any repeat work—not chemical time alone.
