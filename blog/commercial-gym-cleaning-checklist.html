Commercial gym cleaning checklist for equipment and wet zones

Clean the gym around how members use it

A wiped console can sit beside a sticky bench seam, a cloudy mirror, and a shower threshold with slippery soap film. Members notice those details. Run grips, upholstery, and consoles as one cleaning route; give showers, tile, and drains their own products and working time.

Split the close into member-floor and wet-zone work so one stubborn shower does not delay every treadmill and bench.

Give each cart one clear job

ZoneProductCleaning goal
Cardio and strength equipmentPurgoRemove residue and address odor-causing organic load
Mats, benches, and weightsPurgoClean high-use surfaces on a repeatable route
Showers, tile, and restroomsMultiWashCut soap film, body oil, and mixed wet-zone soil
Mirrors and glassMultiWashRemove fingerprints and water film at a lighter dilution

Purgo is not a substitute for an approved disinfectant where the facility requires one. Clean first, then complete any separate labeled disinfection step in the gym’s hygiene program.

Run the floor as a route, not a checklist pile

Start with the highest-use cardio and strength zones. Move in one direction so cleaned equipment is easy to see and members do not redirect the crew.

Spray the cloth where manufacturers prohibit direct application. Keep liquid out of consoles and seams, and flag torn upholstery or loose grips during the pass.

Give showers enough working time

Wet zones consume labor when crews spray and scrub immediately. Apply MultiWash evenly, let it work on the film, then agitate grout lines and deposits that remain.

Rinse as directed and inspect after drying. A clean wet zone should be free of soap film, streaks, and residue that makes the next shift harder.

Use the safety profile to simplify recurring work

Purgo and MultiWash are HMIS 0-0-0, ship non-hazmat, and add no product-specific PPE or ventilation requirement for routine use.

The cleaning job may still call for gloves, eye protection, slip controls, or exposure precautions based on the soil and facility policy.

That distinction lets operators simplify chemical receiving and training without weakening the hygiene or workplace plan.

Manage cost per member-ready zone

Track completed equipment groups, wet zones, repeat passes, and the time each area becomes ready for members.

Product consolidation pays when carts become easier to stock, crews switch products less often, and the close finishes with fewer missed areas.
