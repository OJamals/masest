One formula, five water-system jobs

Why NSF 60 belongs in the conversation

NSF/ANSI/CAN 60 covers the health effects of chemicals used to treat drinking water. WaterSafe60 carries that certification for potable-water work.

For utilities, contractors, hospitals, campuses, and well teams, that gives purchasing and operations a familiar starting point for product review.

Plan the job before the first feed

Measure the system volume, starting pH, hardness, flow, and visible buildup. Mark sample points and decide what a good finish looks like before product enters the system.

For pH work, a bench titration gives the team a practical starting point. For cleaning work, the system layout and deposit load shape the circulation, contact time, and flush plan.

Match the feed method to the work

Ongoing pH, scale, and corrosion programs usually call for controlled metering. Pipe and equipment cleaning work better as planned, isolated jobs with circulation or soak time.

Well rehabilitation follows its own sequence: calculate the treated volume, apply the product, give it time to work, then purge while watching pH return to normal.

The current product directions contain the use levels for each application. MASEST can help turn those directions into a job plan for the actual system.

Measure the finished result

Track the numbers the system already gives you: pH, flow, pressure, product use, and time. Add a visual check wherever scale or deposits can be seen safely.

For ongoing treatment, trend the system over time. For a cleaning or well job, record the starting condition, treatment, flush, finished reading, and any adjustment for the next run.
