Field preparation

A practical sequence for each job.

Use these planning steps with the current supplied label, SDS, equipment instructions, and approved site method. Confirm concentration, temperature, contact time, and acceptance limits before chemical work.

Clean coils, pans, drains, and exposed surfacesUse HVAC CR for compatible grease and organic soils; review HVAC HCR for mineral scale. Match the cleaner to the soil and equipment.+

Product starting points: VertKleen HVAC CR · VertKleen HVAC HCR

Before you start

Read the supplied label and SDS. Confirm coil metal, coatings, seals, and the equipment maker's cleaning limits. Isolate power and protect controls; arrange rinse-water capture.

  1. Inspect and test

    Photograph the buildup and check drainage. Test the selected product on a small inconspicuous area at the approved dilution; inspect for discoloration, coating loss, and seal changes.

  2. Mix for this job

    Use the current label's dilution and mixing order. Record product, water-to-product ratio, batch volume, and planned contact time. Surface-cleaning ratios are not recirculation doses.

  3. Apply and keep control

    Apply by the equipment-approved method. Keep cleaner away from energized parts. Observe the label contact time, keep the surface from drying, and use only tools that will not damage fins or coatings.

  4. Rinse and inspect

    Rinse by the approved method, capture runoff, and inspect fins, pan, drain, and seals. Check that the drain flows and no cleaner residue remains before restoring equipment.

Stop and reassess

Stop for leaks, discoloration, coating damage, blocked drainage, or unexpected reaction. Do not mix cleaners or use cleaning instructions as a disinfectant procedure.

Record the result

Save before/after photos, dilution, contact time, rinse method, drain check, and any equipment defects.

Descale an isolated exchanger or water-side loopPlan a temporary cleaning circuit using HVAC HCR or Descaler after confirming scale type and compatibility.+

Product starting points: VertKleen HVAC HCR · VertKleen Descaler

Before you start

Have a qualified technician approve the method. Confirm metallurgy, gaskets, coatings, isolated volume, pump/hose compatibility, venting, working pressure, concentration, temperature, contact time, monitoring intervals, rinse limits, and waste route before starting.

Reservoir→Pump→Isolated asset→Contained return
  1. Baseline and isolate

    Record flow, pressure drop, inlet/return temperatures, and visible scale. Shut down under the site's energy-control procedure, verify isolation, release stored pressure, cool as required, and protect adjacent operating equipment.

  2. Build and water-test the circuit

    Connect the cleaning reservoir to a compatible pump, the isolated asset, and a contained return. Use the approved vent arrangement; do not seal a circuit that can evolve gas. Test with water for leaks, flow, return capacity, and isolation from the live system.

  3. Charge the approved solution

    Measure the actual cleaning volume, including reservoir and hoses. Prepare the specified product concentration using its label mixing order. Do not extrapolate surface dilution or add a second chemical to the circuit.

  4. Circulate and log

    Run at the approved flow, temperature, and pressure. Log elapsed time, supply/return pH, flow, pressure drop, temperature, and visible reaction at the method's intervals. Filter or remove dislodged solids as specified. Replenish only within the approved method; pH alone does not prove cleaning is complete.

  5. Drain and rinse

    Stop at the approved endpoint or time limit. Collect spent solution for the site's wastewater assessment. Flush with approved water until the specified pH, conductivity, and residue limits are met; inspect strainers and check flow/pressure drop.

  6. Restart and hand off

    Restore valves, probes, strainers, and guards; leak-test and return the system under the equipment maker's restart procedure. Re-establish the approved treatment program. Verify operating readings and obtain the owner's acceptance.

Stop and reassess

Stop circulation for a leak, loss of flow, blocked return, excessive pressure/temperature, uncontrolled gas or foam, or signs of material attack. Never run a cleanout through an occupied or live potable-water circuit.

Record the result

Keep the circuit sketch, isolated volume, product/batch, concentration, readings, waste disposition, rinse acceptance, restored operating data, and owner sign-off.

Clean a cooling tower during a planned shutdownSeparate physical cleaning, descaling, and the site's disinfection procedure before restarting treatment.+

Product starting points: VertKleen HVAC HCR · VertKleen Descaler · VertKleen HVAC CR

Before you start

Coordinate the outage with the owner and water-management team. Follow the tower maker's instructions and site water-management plan for shutdown, aerosol control, cleaning, disinfection, and restart.

  1. Shut down and contain

    Isolate electrical and mechanical energy under the site procedure. Establish containment and the approved waste route. Follow site controls for fans, nearby air intakes, and exposure during cleaning.

  2. Remove loose deposits

    Drain as specified and remove sludge and debris from the basin, strainers, and accessible surfaces. Inspect fill, drift eliminators, distribution nozzles, coatings, and corrosion before choosing a cleaner.

  3. Clean for the actual soil

    Use the compatible product and approved method for grease, organic soils, or mineral deposits. Apply surface directions to surfaces; use the isolated recirculation method only where the equipment and method permit it.

  4. Rinse and complete site disinfection

    Capture cleaning waste and verify the rinse endpoint. Complete the separate disinfection procedure specified by the water-management plan using the appropriate registered product and its label; a descaler is not a substitute.

  5. Refill, treat, and verify

    Inspect and restore equipment, refill with approved water, restart the approved treatment/feed controls, and check leaks and operating readings. Record control measurements and owner approval before routine operation.

Stop and reassess

If contamination or a Legionella concern is suspected, involve the water-management team and follow its response plan before proceeding. Do not improvise chemical mixtures or disinfection doses.

Record the result

Log shutdown, inspection findings, cleaning product/method, separate disinfection record, waste route, refill chemistry, controller checks, and restart approval.

Set up ongoing scale and corrosion controlUse WaterSafe60 within a site-specific treatment plan; cleaning and maintenance treatment have different concentrations and endpoints.+

Product starting points: WaterSafe60

Before you start

Identify the system and use: open cooling tower, closed loop, process water, or potable application. Confirm applicable product listing, maximum use level, system restrictions, and current label with the treatment specialist.

  1. Characterize the water

    Measure makeup and system water chemistry, including the specialist's required hardness, alkalinity, conductivity, pH, temperature, and corrosion indicators. Confirm system volume, makeup demand, blowdown, materials, and existing chemicals.

  2. Set the treatment target

    Have the treatment specialist select the dose, target residual, control limits, sampling points, and response actions from the current product documentation and site water analysis. Do not use a descaling concentration as a continuous feed setting.

  3. Commission the feed

    Use compatible metering equipment and containment. Verify pump calibration and controller/probe readings, set the approved feed/interlocks, and confirm the actual treatment response with the specified test or titration method.

  4. Monitor and correct

    Trend the required residual and water parameters at the site's intervals. Inspect for scale, corrosion, fouling, and leaks. Correct out-of-limit results under the approved plan; record water changes, feed adjustments, and service work.

  5. Maintain the full water-management plan

    Coordinate scale/corrosion treatment with the site's separate microbiological controls, cleaning schedule, stagnation controls, and shutdown/restart procedures. Review results and responsibilities with the owner.

Stop and reassess

Escalate unknown water chemistry, loss of feed, unexplained pH change, corrosion, or a water-management control failure. WaterSafe60, HVAC CR, and HVAC HCR do not replace a Legionella water-management program.

Record the result

Keep water analyses, selected dose and control limits, calibration/test records, trend logs, corrective actions, service visits, and the current site plan.
