## Buyer research: questions that can disprove our assumptions

Use three provisional recruitment lanes, not persona claims: maintenance/HVAC contractors, cleaning/restoration businesses or resellers considering their own label, and facility teams evaluating a replacement cleaner. Recruit from existing relationships or voluntary inbound inquiries before seeking unrelated contacts. No unapproved incentive, recording or marketing enrollment.

**Opening draft:** “We’re improving how we explain product selection and first tests. Could you walk us through one recent cleaning or procurement job? We want to understand what happened, including where our guidance was unhelpful. This is a learning conversation; you do not need to buy anything.”

Ask about actual behavior first:

1. What was the last job where your usual cleaner or method did not give the result you needed? What equipment/material, deposit and operating constraint were involved?
2. What did you try first? What changed that made you look for another product or supplier?
3. Where did you search, which terms did you use, and what supplier/document did you open first?
4. What information did you need to approve a test? Who had to check compatibility, documents, handling, wastewater/disposal or purchasing terms?
5. What nearly stopped the purchase or test? What would have made you stay with the existing method?
6. What did you measure to decide whether the job was acceptable? Which cost mattered most: product, labor, passes, water, shutdown, disposal or delivered price?
7. For a label/supply discussion: what made you consider your own label? Which products, package, artwork, quantity or lead-time requirement would rule a supplier out?
8. Looking at the relevant MASEST page, what can you decide from it? What is still missing? What would your next action be?
9. Would you be willing to let us record a short factual job summary or quote for review? What details must stay private? Only request recording/publication permission after explaining the use.

Capture one record per participant. Use their exact words only with source/permission retained. Separate interviewer interpretation from what the participant said. Do not combine contractor and private-label responses into one persona. Treat fewer than five independent sources in a lane as early signals; even larger qualitative samples do not establish population prevalence.

## Free-tool specification: completed-job cost comparison

This is a scoped candidate, not a built/published calculator. Validate interest with the worksheet and actual buyers first. It should help compare a finished job using buyer-entered measurements; it must not prescribe product, dilution, temperature, dwell time, compatibility, efficiency improvement or revenue savings.

**Inputs per method:** concentrate actually used in a declared volume unit; current concentrate unit cost; total person-hours; hourly labor cost; measured water volume and its unit cost; disposal charge; other measured job charges; optional measured shutdown cost. Every input is user-supplied, with no default VertKleen/competitor performance figures. Keep units explicit and equivalent on both sides. Note quote validity and whether freight/tax is included.

**Transparent formula:**

```text
product_cost = concentrate_used * cost_per_same_volume_unit
labor_cost = total_person_hours * cost_per_person_hour
water_cost = water_used * cost_per_same_water_unit
```
